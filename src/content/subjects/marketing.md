## Marketing Management: Needs Before Products

Marketing questions are usually about sequence and orientation. The same idea returns in different words — which philosophy a firm is actually practising, what must exist before a product is designed, and who the seller is aiming at.

### What These 29 Questions Cover

- **The marketing philosophies:** the production, product, selling and marketing concepts, and how to identify each from a described firm — the densest area on this page.
- **Goods and buyer types:** unsought goods such as insurance and funeral plots, and business markets versus consumer markets.
- **Customer orientation:** target markets, needs and wants, and customer value.
- **Brand, pricing and promotion:** branding decisions, pricing as a mix element, and advertising.
- **Retailing and modern channels:** retailers, and the comparison between brick-and-click firms and pure-click businesses.

Read the explanation even when you answer correctly — the distinction being tested is usually hiding in the wrong options. For the missing topics above, treat the rules below as a checklist of what you still need to learn elsewhere.

### Rules Worth Memorising

- Segmentation precedes targeting, and targeting precedes positioning — the order is examinable.
- Introduction-stage pricing is either penetration or skimming; the choice depends on price sensitivity and the risk of imitation.
- Intensive distribution suits convenience goods; exclusive distribution suits specialty goods.
- A pull strategy aims at the consumer; a push strategy aims at the channel.
- Services add physical evidence, people and process to the four Ps because they are intangible, inseparable, variable and perishable.
