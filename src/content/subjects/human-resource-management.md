## Human Resource Management: Functions, Not Definitions

HRM papers are won by candidates who can place a practice inside the right function. An examiner who asks about a job description is really testing whether you separate it from job analysis; one who asks about a selection test is testing whether you know validity from reliability.

### What These 171 Questions Cover

- **Scope of the function:** personnel management versus strategic HRM, and the manager's role in human-resource activities.
- **Workforce planning:** human resource planning and demand forecasting techniques.
- **Job analysis:** methods of analysis, and the difference between a job description and a job specification.
- **Recruitment & selection:** sources of recruitment, interview formats, aptitude testing, and the psychometric ideas of validity and reliability — the single densest area in this bank.
- **Training & development:** induction and orientation, and the distinction between training and development.
- **Performance appraisal:** the concept and its purpose (the bank touches this lightly).
- **Employee turnover & morale:** job satisfaction, absenteeism, and turnover as measurable outcomes.
- **Industrial relations:** trade unions and collective bargaining.

### Still Missing From This Page

Motivation and leadership theory (Maslow, Herzberg, Theory X and Y, leadership styles) is largely absent, along with grievance handling and discipline, delegation and line-versus-staff authority, incentive and wage structures, and the specifics of Pakistani labour law — the Industrial Relations Act 2012, EOBI and the Workers' Welfare Fund. Several of those are examinable in establishment and HR-manager posts, so work them from your own notes until sets covering them are added.

### How to Use These Sets

Read the explanation even when you answer correctly — the distinction being tested is usually hiding in the wrong options.

### Distinctions to Memorise

- Reliability is consistency of a measure; validity is whether it measures what it is supposed to.
- A job description states the duties; a job specification states the person required for them.
- Job analysis comes first; recruitment follows it and never precedes it.
- Induction happens when an employee joins; training addresses a current skill gap; development prepares for a future role.
- Collective bargaining covers wages, hours and working conditions; grievance handling and discipline are individual matters.
