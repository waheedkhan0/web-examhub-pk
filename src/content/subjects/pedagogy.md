# Pedagogy and Teaching Methodology

Every teaching-category post recruited in Pakistan — Primary Education Teacher (PET), Lecturer, and the NTS teacher eligibility tests — carries a pedagogy component. It is the highest-scoring section for candidates who study it deliberately, because it tests a limited, well-defined body of theory rather than open-ended general knowledge.

## What is Actually Tested

### Teaching Methods and Classroom Practice
- **Instructional objectives** — Bloom's taxonomy and its three domains (cognitive, affective, psychomotor), and Behavioural Objectives (ABC D: Audience, Behaviour, Condition, Degree).
- **Lesson planning** — Herbart's five steps, the 5E model, and the difference between scheme of work, lesson plan and unit plan.
- **Methods** — demonstration, project, discussion, heuristic/self-discovery, micro-teaching, and the learner-centred versus teacher-centred distinction.
- **Aids and technology** — TLM selection, the role of audiovisual aids, and the projected versus non-projected distinction.
- **Evaluation** — formative versus summative, achievement versus diagnostic versus aptitude tests, norm-referenced versus criterion-referenced marking, validity, reliability and the item analysis of MCQs.

### Child Development and Psychology
- **Developmental theorists** — Piaget's four stages of cognitive development, Kohlberg's moral stages, Erikson's psychosocial crises, Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development and scaffolding.
- **Learning theorists** — Pavlov, Skinner (operant conditioning and reinforcement), Thorndike's laws, Gestalt insight learning, Bandura's social learning.
- **Individual differences** — intelligence theories (Gardner multiple intelligences, Spearman g), giftedness, learning disabilities such as dyslexia and dyscalculia.
- **Inclusive education and guidance** — the principle of mainstreaming, and the teacher's role in identification and referral.

## Preparation Strategy

1. Learn each theorist as a set of **stage names in order** — questions test sequence and the naming of stages, not broad descriptions.
2. Keep a table contrasting formative/summative, norm/criterion and aptitude/achievement. Nearly every pedagogy paper contains at least one such pairing question.
3. Practise writing a behavioural objective using Bloom's measurable verbs; avoid 'understand' and 'know' which cannot be assessed.

## Common Traps

Options frequently attribute one theorist's stage to another (Erikson against Piaget), or confuse negative reinforcement with punishment. Negative reinforcement *increases* a behaviour by removing an aversive stimulus; punishment decreases it.
