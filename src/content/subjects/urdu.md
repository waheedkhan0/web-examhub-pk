# Urdu Language and Literature

Urdu is a compulsory subject in Provincial Management Services exams and one of the optional papers in CSS. Public service commissions also test it directly in general knowledge and subject-specific recruitment posts, including lecturer and translator positions.

## What is Actually Tested

### Grammar and Vocabulary (Grammatical Precision)
- **Ism, Fail, Harf** — the classical parts-of-speech division and the case markers that follow them.
- **Murakkab tereeb (compound constructions)** — Izafi, Taqsimi, Sifati and Verbal compounds, and the agreement rules each imposes on the verb.
- **Idioms (Mutlokat aur Muhawrat)** — commission papers lean heavily on idioms drawn from standard textbooks and classical prose.
- **Vocabulary** — synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutes and Urdu equivalents of common English administrative terms.
- **Spelling and script** — the distinction between homophonous letters (ز ,ذ ,ظ ,ض and ص ,س) is a regular source of objective questions.

### Literature and Authors (Adha-o-Tareekh)
- **Classical poets** — Amir Khusro, Dard, Mir, Ghalib, Sauda and Momin, with their ghazal tradition, style and well-known couplets.
- **Modern prose and poetry** — Allama Iqbal (Shikwa Jawab-e-Shikwa, Bang-e-Dra, Zarb-e-Kaleem), Faiz Ahmed Faiz, Josh Malihabadi, and the Progressive Writers' Movement of 1936.
- **Forms and genres** — ghazal, nazm, qasida, marsia, sonnet, afsana (short story) and dramatic writing.
- **Firsts and milestones** — the first Urdu prose book, the first dictionary, and the journals that shaped modern criticism.

## Preparation Strategy

1. Start with the grammar foundation from your board-level Urdu grammar text; the objective section rarely goes beyond standard rules.
2. Maintain an idiom notebook: about 300 commonly-tested idioms cover most commission papers.
3. Learn couplets with their poet, the collection they belong to, and their theme — questions usually pair poet and work, not poet and date.
4. For PMS written papers, practise essay and precis writing in Urdu itself; thinking in Urdu and writing in English costs marks.

## Common Traps

Commission MCQs pair poets with the wrong collections and idioms with near-miss literal meanings. Read the complete options before answering; several options will be superficially plausible.
