    question: In interpersonal communication, ethics
    options:
      - increase barriers to understand
      - are not a consideration
      - stand in the way of honesty
      - are important
    answer: 3
    explanation: "In interpersonal communication, ethics Option A: increase barriers to understand This option is incorrect. Ethics in communication do not create barriers; instead, they facilitate clearer and more respectful exchanges. Option B: are not a consideration This option is incorrect. Ethics are a crucial consideration in all forms of communication, ensuring that interactions are honest and respectful. Option C: stand in the way of honesty This option is incorrect. Ethics promote honesty and integrity in communication rather than hindering it. Option D: are important This is the correct option. Ethics play a vital role in interpersonal communication by fostering trust, respect, and effective dialogue, ensuring that interactions are conducted with integrity and honesty. In conclusion, the correct answer is Option D: are important , as ethics are essential for maintaining honesty, respect, and effectiveness in interpersonal communication."

    question: Our 'self-concept' which influences our communication behaviour is developed due to the following factors 1. cultural traditions 2. self-evaluation 3. social roles 4. social organisation
    options:
      - 1, 2 and 4
      - 2, 3 and 4
      - 1, 2 and 3
      - 1, 3 and 4
    answer: 2
    explanation: Self-concept means how a person views themselves and this directly affects how they communicate with others. Cultural traditions shape how we learn to behave, express, react and communicate from childhood. Self-evaluation affects how confident we feel and how we judge our own abilities, which influences our communication style. Social roles (student, parent, teacher, leader etc.) define how we communicate differently in different roles. Social organisation affects society as a whole, but is not a direct personal level factor for self-concept development. Therefore, only statements 1, 2 and 3 directly contribute to the development of self-concept.
