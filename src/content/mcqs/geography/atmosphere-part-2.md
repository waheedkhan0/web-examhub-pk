    question: The continuous circulation of water among the hydrosphere, atmosphere and lithosphere is known as
    options:
      - Water circulation
      - Hydrologic cycle
      - Hydro-equilibrium
      - Precipitation
    answer: 1
    explanation: The continuous circulation of water among the hydrosphere, atmosphere and Lithosphere is known as hydrologic cycle. The water, or hydrologic, cycle describes the pilgrimage of water as water molecules make their way from the Earth’s surface to the atmosphere and back again, in some cases to below the surface. This gigantic system, powered by energy from the Sun, is a continuous exchange of moisture between the oceans, the atmosphere, and the land.

    question: What is the major role of a greenhouse gas that contributes to temperature rise of the Earth's surface?
    options:
      - Transparent to both incoming sunlight and outgoing infrared radiation
      - Stops both incoming sunlight and outgoing infrared radiation
      - Lets outgoing infrared radiation pass through but stops incoming sunlight
      - Lets incoming sunlight pass through but stops outgoing infrared radiation
    answer: 3
    explanation: Solar energy heats the earth’s surface. But the energy does not stay bound up in the earth’s environment forever. Instead, as the earth warms, it emits thermal radiation. This thermal radiation, which is largely in the form of long-wave infrared rays, eventually finds its way out into space, leaving the earth and allowing it to cool. However, not all of the infrared rays pass into space. Some of the infrared rays are absorbed by greenhouse gases and warm the atmosphere. So the amount of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere is directly related to the temperature of the atmosphere.

    question: The tropical cyclones - Hurricanes and Typhoons - develop and mature ________
    options:
      - At the same place
      - Over the land masses only
      - Over the water bodies only
      - Anywhere in the tropical zone
    answer: 3
    explanation: Tropical cyclones, including hurricanes and typhoons, can develop and mature anywhere within the tropical zone. The tropical zone encompasses the region between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn, which includes warm ocean waters in tropical and subtropical regions around the globe. These cyclones form over warm ocean waters, where the sea surface temperatures are typically above 26.5°C (80°F), providing the necessary energy for their development. The warm and moist air near the ocean's surface rises and creates a spinning motion due to the Earth's rotation, leading to the formation of these cyclonic systems. As they move across the warm waters, tropical cyclones can intensify and become powerful storms. Therefore, they can develop and mature anywhere within the tropical zone.
