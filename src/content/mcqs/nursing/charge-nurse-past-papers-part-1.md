    question: What is the adult chest compression to ventilation ratio in single-rescuer basic life support (CPR)?
    options:
      - 15:2
      - 30:2
      - 50:2
      - Continuous compressions only
    answer: 1
    explanation: According to American Heart Association (AHA) and international resuscitation guidelines, the compression-to-ventilation ratio for adult CPR is 30 compressions to 2 breaths, at a rate of 100-120 compressions per minute and depth of at least 2 inches (5 cm).
    keyConcept: Basic Life Support CPR Compression Ratio
    examTip: "In both single and two-rescuer adult CPR the ratio is 30:2. For infants and children, two-rescuer CPR becomes 15:2."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC Charge Nurse 2022
      - KPPSC Staff Nurse 2023

  - id: 2
    question: A nurse is monitoring a patient receiving intravenous potassium chloride (KCl). Which method of administration is strictly contraindicated?
    options:
      - Diluted in 500 mL normal saline via IV infusion pump
      - IV push / direct rapid intravenous bolus
      - Administered piggyback with 5% Dextrose
      - Infused at a rate not exceeding 10-20 mEq/hour
    answer: 1
    explanation: Direct intravenous push or rapid bolus of potassium chloride causes immediate cardiac arrest and lethal ventricular fibrillation. KCl must always be administered diluted in compatible IV solutions and delivered slowly with an infusion controller.
    keyConcept: Safe Intravenous Potassium Administration Protocols
    testedIn:
      - KPPSC Charge Nurse 2023
      - PPSC Charge Nurse 2022

    question: "On the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), the minimum possible total score and maximum score are:"
    options:
      - 0 and 10
      - 3 and 15
      - 1 and 15
      - 0 and 15
    answer: 1
    explanation: The Glasgow Coma Scale evaluates Eye Opening (1-4), Verbal Response (1-5), and Motor Response (1-6). The lowest possible score is 3 (deep coma / brain death), and the highest score is 15 (fully alert and oriented).
    keyConcept: Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) Score Range

    question: What is the primary antidote for acute unfractionated heparin toxicity?
    options:
      - Vitamin K (Phytonadione)
      - Protamine Sulfate
      - Naloxone
      - Flumazenil
    answer: 1
    explanation: Protamine sulfate binds heparin through ionic interaction to form a stable, inactive salt complex, rapidly neutralizing heparin's anticoagulant activity. Vitamin K is the antidote for Warfarin toxicity.
    keyConcept: Antidotes in Clinical Pharmacology
    examTip: Protamine sulfate = Heparin; Vitamin K = Warfarin; Naloxone = Opioids; Flumazenil = Benzodiazepines.

    question: An adult post-thyroidectomy patient complains of tingling around the mouth and fingers with muscle cramping. What is the suspected condition?
    options:
      - Hyperkalemia
      - Hypocalcemia (tetany due to accidental parathyroid injury)

    answer: 1

    keyConcept: "Post-operative Thyroid Complications & Tetany"

  - id: 8
    question: A physician prescribes 1000 mL normal saline to be infused over 8 hours. The drop factor of the IV administration set is 15 drops/mL. What is the flow rate in drops per minute?
    options:
      - 21 gtts/min
      - 31 gtts/min
      - 45 gtts/min
      - 60 gtts/min
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Flow rate (gtts/min) = (Total volume in mL * Drop factor) / (Total time in minutes) = (1000 * 15) / (8 * 60) = 15000 / 480 = 31.25, rounded to 31 drops/minute."
    keyConcept: Intravenous Infusion Drip Rate Calculation
    testedIn:
      - KPPSC Charge Nurse 2024
      - FPSC Charge Nurse 2023
