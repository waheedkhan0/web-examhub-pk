    question: The enhanced greenhouse effect warms the Earth's surface chiefly because greenhouse gases
    options:
      - Absorb most incoming visible sunlight before it reaches the ground
      - Block solar ultraviolet radiation from reaching the surface
      - Absorb and re-emit the long-wave infrared radiation given off by the warmed Earth
      - Reflect heat back into space from low clouds
    answer: 2
    explanation: The atmosphere is largely transparent to short-wave solar radiation but absorbs terrestrial long-wave infrared in specific bands, re-emitting part of it downward. Blocking ultraviolet is the function of stratospheric ozone, not of greenhouse gases — the trap in option 2.
    keyConcept: Differential transmission of short-wave and long-wave radiation
    examTip: "Every radiation question turns on one distinction: solar energy is short-wave, Earth's emission is long-wave infrared."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - NTS

    question: Which gas accounts for the largest share of human-emitted greenhouse gases worldwide?
    options:
      - Carbon dioxide
      - Methane
      - Nitrous oxide
      - Chlorofluorocarbons
    answer: 0
    explanation: Carbon dioxide from fossil-fuel combustion, cement making and land-use change contributes roughly three-quarters of anthropogenic greenhouse emissions, well ahead of methane. Water vapour is the most abundant greenhouse gas overall but is not counted in inventories because its concentration responds to temperature rather than to emissions.
    keyConcept: Ranking of anthropogenic greenhouse gases by contribution
    examTip: Distinguish abundance (water vapour) from human-caused contribution (carbon dioxide) — the trick this item is built on.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC

    question: After carbon dioxide, the largest human-caused greenhouse gas, with major sources in flooded rice fields, ruminant livestock, landfills and leaking natural gas, is
    options:
      - Nitrous oxide
      - Methane
      - Sulphur hexafluoride
      - Tropospheric ozone
    answer: 1
    explanation: Methane is the second-ranked anthropogenic greenhouse gas and is notable for agriculture, wetlands, fossil-fuel extraction and waste. Nitrous oxide is third and comes mainly from fertiliser use in soils, which makes it the close distractor here.
    keyConcept: Methane sources and its high per-mass warming potential
    examTip: "Sources identify the gas: rice, cattle and landfills mean methane; fertiliser and industrial nitric acid mean nitrous oxide."

    question: The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit in
    options:
      - "1987"
      - "1997"
      - "2001"
      - "1992"
    answer: 3
    explanation: The UNFCCC was opened for signature at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development in Rio de Janeiro in June 1992 and entered into force in 1994. 1987 is the Montreal Protocol year and 1997 the Kyoto year, so both are deliberate chain-shift traps.
    keyConcept: The treaty chain — 1992 UNFCCC, 1997 Kyoto, 2015 Paris
    examTip: "Learn the chain as one line: Rio 1992 (framework) - Kyoto 1997 (targets) - Paris 2015 (pledges)."
    testedIn:

    question: The first protocol under the UNFCCC, which set legally binding emission-reduction targets for industrialised countries only, was adopted at Kyoto in
    options:
      - "1994"
      - "1997"
      - "2005"
      - "2012"
    answer: 1
    explanation: The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in December 1997 at COP3; its first commitment period ran from 2008 to 2012. Option 2005 is the entry-into-force year and is the standard trap, while option 2012 relates to the end of the first period and the Doha amendment.
    keyConcept: Kyoto Protocol — binding targets, differentiated responsibility
    examTip: "Adoption versus entry into force are tested separately: Kyoto adopted 1997, in force 2005; Paris adopted and in force in 2015-16."

    question: The 2015 Paris Agreement commits its parties to hold the increase in global average temperature well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, while pursuing efforts to limit the increase to
    options:
      - 1.0 degree Celsius
      - 2.5 degrees Celsius
      - 1.5 degrees Celsius
      - 3.0 degrees Celsius
    answer: 2
    explanation: Article 2 of the Paris Agreement sets the well-below-2-degrees goal with the 1.5-degree aspiration, now central to the argument of low-lying and glacier-dependent countries such as Pakistan. The two-degree figure is already in the stem, so the aspiration value is what is being tested.
    keyConcept: Paris Agreement temperature goal and nationally determined contributions
    examTip: "Note the mechanism as well as the number: Paris relies on self-declared NDCs updated every five years, unlike Kyoto's assigned targets."
    testedIn:

      - FPSC

    question: The final text of which conference of the parties, adopted in 2021, referred for the first time to phasing down unabated coal power?
    options:
      - COP26, Glasgow
      - COP15, Copenhagen
      - COP21, Paris
      - COP24, Katowice
    answer: 0
    explanation: The Glasgow Climate Pact of November 2021 was the first UNFCCC decision to mention coal explicitly, calling for a phase-down of unabated coal power. Last-minute objection to stronger wording means the phrase is 'phase down', not 'phase out' — a distinction papers exploit.
    keyConcept: Glasgow Climate Pact and the first explicit fossil-fuel language
    examTip: "Anchor each COP by its product: Kyoto 1997, Paris 2015, Glasgow 2021 coal language, Sharm el-Sheikh 2022 finance."

    question: The decision to establish a dedicated loss-and-damage fund for climate-vulnerable developing countries was taken at
    options:
      - COP21, Paris

    answer: 3

    question: In Pakistan's 2022 super-monsoon floods, the number of people reported as affected, in the assessment prepared with the United Nations and the World Bank, was closest to
    options:
      - 3 million
      - 33 million
      - 12 million
      - 60 million
    answer: 1
    explanation: The Post-Disaster Needs Assessment recorded about 33 million people affected, more than 1,700 deaths and some eight million displaced, with total damages and losses estimated near 30 billion dollars. The peak of the Indus and its tributaries ran far above flood stage from July to September 2022.
    keyConcept: Scale and consequences of the 2022 Pakistan floods
    examTip: "Hold three figures for this disaster: 33 million affected, 1,700-plus dead, roughly a third of the country under water at the peak."
    testedIn:

    question: By the conventional freshwater benchmark, a country is described as water-stressed when renewable freshwater availability falls below about
    options:
      - 1,700 cubic metres per person per year
      - 500 cubic metres per person per year
      - 100 cubic metres per person per year
      - 5,000 cubic metres per person per year
    answer: 0
    explanation: The indicator sets stress below 1,700 cubic metres per capita annually, scarcity below 1,000 and absolute scarcity below 500. Option 2 is therefore the absolute-scarcity line, not the stress line — the exact substitution this question tests.
    keyConcept: Per-capita renewable water thresholds and Pakistan's Indus-basin stress
    examTip: Pakistan's availability has fallen steadily as population grew on a nearly fixed Indus flow, which is why the country now sits at or below the stress line.
    testedIn:

      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - NTS

    question: Glaciologists use the term 'Karakoram anomaly' to describe the observation that
    options:
      - Karakoram glaciers are the only glaciers found entirely within a single country's borders
      - Karakoram ice is unusually free of dust and therefore melts faster than Himalayan ice
      - all Himalayan glaciers advanced during the twentieth century while Karakoram glaciers shrank
      - a substantial number of glaciers in the Karakoram remained stable or even advanced while most of the world's glaciers retreated
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Surges and regionally increased snowfall have kept several Karakoram glaciers stable or advancing through a period of general global ice loss. This does not mean glacier-dependent regions are safe: the Indus system still loses long-term ice storage as temperatures rise."
    keyConcept: Regional variation in glacier response and Indus-basin ice storage
    examTip: Treat the anomaly as a qualified fact — stable or surging glaciers in part of the range, not proof of no warming.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC

    question: Which pairing of an energy resource with the part of Pakistan where its potential is greatest is correct?
    options:
      - Hydropower — the Thar coal belt of eastern Sindh
      - Wind — the high glacier valleys of Gilgit-Baltistan
      - Solar — the sunlit plains, deserts and coastlands of Sindh and Balochistan
      - Geothermal — the alluvial doab plains of central Punjab
    answer: 2
    explanation: Sindh and Balochistan combine the highest solar irradiation in the country with vast unused land, so utility-scale solar belongs to the south and west. Hydropower potential lies on the Indus and its northern tributaries in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Gilgit-Baltistan, while the developed wind sites are the Jhimpir belt of Sindh and the Balochistan coast near Gwadar.
    keyConcept: Geography of Pakistan's renewable energy endowment
    examTip: "Resource-to-region mapping is the format: solar and wind to the south and west coast, hydel to the north, coal to Thar."
