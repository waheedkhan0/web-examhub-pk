    question: In a grazing food chain, an animal that feeds only on green plants occupies which role?
    options:
      - Primary producer
      - Primary consumer
      - Secondary consumer
      - Decomposer
    answer: 1
    explanation: Producers form the first trophic level, so a herbivore that eats them is the primary consumer at the second level. Choosing 'secondary consumer' is the usual error of counting the plant level as level zero.
    keyConcept: Trophic levels and the structure of a grazing food chain
    examTip: "Count from the producer: producers are always level 1, herbivore 2, first carnivore 3, top carnivore 4."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 2
    question: In an ecosystem only about ten per cent of the energy in one trophic level is converted into biomass at the next. The remainder is lost chiefly
    options:
      - Because decomposers oxidise it completely into mineral nutrients
      - Because predators eat every organism at the level below
      - As heat given off in respiration and the ordinary life processes
      - Through the excretion of undigested matter only
    answer: 2
    explanation: Most assimilated energy is spent on metabolism and lost as heat, with some leaving in waste and uneaten parts; this is Lindemann's ten-per-cent law of energy flow. Undigested waste (option 4) is a real but much smaller route, and option 1 misstates where the energy goes.
    keyConcept: The ten-per-cent rule of energy transfer between trophic levels
    examTip: Energy is lost as heat, matter is recycled — the pair of words that decides almost every energy-flow question.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
  - id: 3
    question: Which statement correctly distinguishes a habitat from a niche?
    options:
      - A habitat applies to animals while a niche applies only to plants
      - A niche changes with the season while the habitat never changes
      - A habitat is the place only, while a niche is the food the animal eats
      - A habitat is the physical place where an organism lives, while its niche is its functional role, including what it eats, when it is active and how it interacts with other species
    answer: 3
    explanation: "The standard formulation is that a habitat is an organism's address and its niche is its profession: the total of its requirements and relationships. Option 3 is the trap because it reduces the niche to diet alone."
    keyConcept: Habitat versus ecological niche
    examTip: Two species can share a habitat but cannot stably share one niche — a favourite follow-on statement in objective papers.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 4
    question: In the nitrogen cycle, the conversion of soil nitrates back into gaseous nitrogen, carried out in waterlogged soils, is performed by
    options:
      - Denitrifying bacteria such as Pseudomonas
      - Nitrogen-fixing bacteria such as Rhizobium
      - Nitrifying bacteria such as Nitrobacter
      - Ammonifying bacteria acting on urea
    answer: 0
    explanation: Denitrifiers such as Pseudomonas and Clostridium reduce nitrates to nitrogen gas under anaerobic conditions, removing fertility from soil. Nitrobacter completes nitrification from nitrite to nitrate, while Rhizobium fixes nitrogen — the opposite direction, and the tempting distractor.
    keyConcept: Nitrogen cycle bacteria — fixation, ammonification, nitrification, denitrification
    examTip: "Memorise the direction of each process, not just the name: denitrification is the only step returning N2 to the air."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 5
    question: Rhizobium bacteria living inside the root nodules of leguminous plants improve soil fertility because they
    options:
      - Convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia that the host plant can assimilate
      - Break down soil nitrates into nitrogen gas
      - Release phosphate locked in soil rock
      - Produce hormones that enlarge the root system
    answer: 0
    explanation: Within nodules, the enzyme nitrogenase reduces inert atmospheric N2 to ammonia, which the plant uses to build amino acids in an energy-costly symbiosis. Option 2 describes denitrification, which reduces rather than adds soil nitrogen.
    keyConcept: Biological nitrogen fixation in the legume-Rhizobium symbiosis
    examTip: Crop rotation with pulses exploits exactly this process — the same fact reappears in agriculture-oriented papers.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 6
    question: A species that occurs naturally only in one defined geographic area, and nowhere else in the world, is described as
    options:
      - An exotic or invasive species
      - Endemic
      - A keystone species
      - An indicator species
    answer: 1
    explanation: Endemism means restriction to a particular area, which is why endemic species are disproportionately threatened by habitat loss there. The Indus river dolphin is a familiar Pakistani example, confined to the Indus system. A keystone species is defined by its outsized ecological role, not by range.
    keyConcept: Endemism, rarity and the basis of extinction risk
    examTip: Endemic is about where a species lives; endangered is about how threatened it is — do not merge the two.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 7
    question: Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation of biodiversity?
    options:
      - A national park
      - A wildlife sanctuary
      - A gene and seed bank holding collected germplasm
      - A biosphere reserve
    answer: 2
    explanation: Ex-situ conservation means conserving components of biodiversity outside their natural habitat, as in seed banks, zoological parks, botanical gardens and captive breeding. Parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves protect ecosystems in situ, which is why they appear together as distractors.
    keyConcept: In-situ versus ex-situ conservation strategies
    examTip: If protection happens where the species lives it is in-situ; if material or animals are removed for safekeeping it is ex-situ.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 8
    question: Which sequence shows increasing degree of threat on the IUCN Red List?
    options:
      - Endangered, Vulnerable, Critically Endangered, Least Concern
      - Least Concern, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Critically Endangered
      - Critically Endangered, Endangered, Near Threatened, Least Concern
      - Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered
    answer: 3
    explanation: The threat categories run Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered, Extinct in the Wild and Extinct. Options 1 and 2 place Vulnerable and Endangered out of order, and option 3 is the sequence reversed.
    keyConcept: IUCN Red List categories and their order of threat
    examTip: "Learn five categories as one string: LC - NT - VU - EN - CR; nearly all Red List questions test order, not definitions."
    testedIn:

  - id: 9
    question: CITES, the international convention adopted in 1973 and in force from 1975, is the instrument concerned with
    options:
      - Prohibiting all sport hunting of big game worldwide
      - Controlling and regulating international trade in specimens of threatened wild animals and plants
      - Designating wetlands of international importance
      - Funding the management of national parks in developing countries
    answer: 1
    explanation: CITES works through import and export permits listed in its appendices, so it governs cross-border trade rather than hunting inside a country. Option 3 describes the Ramsar Convention on wetlands, a different instrument.
    keyConcept: CITES and the trade-regulation approach to species conservation
    examTip: CITES regulates trade; the CBD covers ecosystems and species diversity generally; Ramsar covers wetlands.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
  - id: 10
    question: A river-water sample showing a very high BOD value indicates that the water
    options:
      - Carries a large load of biodegradable organic waste, so dissolved oxygen is being consumed by microbes
      - Is essentially free of pollution and safe for fish
      - Is saturated with oxygen and therefore unpolluted
      - Has high salinity and temporary hardness
    answer: 0
    explanation: Biochemical oxygen demand measures the oxygen micro-organisms need to oxidise biodegradable matter over five days at 20 degrees, so a high BOD means heavy organic pollution and oxygen depletion. Chemical oxygen demand (COD) covers chemically oxidisable matter too, biodegradable or not.
    keyConcept: BOD and COD as measures of organic water pollution
    examTip: High BOD equals heavily polluted; the reverse reading is the most common wrong answer in this item.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 11
    question: Stratospheric ozone is depleted chiefly because
    options:
      - Carbon dioxide warms the stratosphere and evaporates ozone
      - Sulphur dioxide oxidises ozone into oxygen
      - Water vapour condenses into ice crystals that absorb ozone
      - Ultraviolet radiation splits chlorine-bearing CFC molecules, releasing chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy ozone
    answer: 3
    explanation: In the stratosphere, UV liberates chlorine from chlorofluorocarbons, and each chlorine atom catalytically destroys many ozone molecules before it is removed. Carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide are involved in warming and acid rain respectively, not in catalytic ozone loss.
    keyConcept: CFC-driven catalytic ozone destruction and the ozone hole
    examTip: Remember the catalyst logic — chlorine is not consumed, so a small CFC release destroys a large amount of ozone.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 12
    question: Acid rain, which lowers the pH of lakes and corrodes stone buildings, is caused chiefly by atmospheric oxides of
    options:
      - Carbon and hydrogen
      - Methane and nitrous oxide
      - Sulphur and nitrogen
      - Chlorine and fluorine
    answer: 2
    explanation: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are oxidised and dissolved in cloud water to form sulphuric and nitric acid, falling as rain with pH well below the natural value of about 5.6. Carbon oxides form weak carbonic acid only, and chlorine compounds are not the main cause.
    keyConcept: Chemistry of acid rain from SO2 and NOx emissions
    examTip: "Link the source to the pollutant: coal-burning power stations give SO2, vehicle exhaust gives NOx — the pair examiners ask together."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
      - NTS
