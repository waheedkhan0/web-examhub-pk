    question: The operation of cutting a cylindrical hole in a sheet of metal by the punch and die is called
    options:
      - Shearing
      - Piercing
      - Punching
      - Blanking
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Punching is a sheet metal operation in which a cylindrical hole or any other desired shape is cut out from a metal sheet using a punch and die set . In this process, the punch applies pressure on the sheet metal, forcing the material into the die opening . The portion of the metal that is removed (the slug) is considered scrap, and the remaining sheet with the hole is the useful part. Piercing is a similar process, but it generally refers to making small holes or slots without removing significant material, often as part of a larger forming operation. Blanking is the reverse of punching. In blanking, the cut-out portion (blank) is the useful part, while the remaining sheet is scrap. Shearing refers to the cutting of a sheet along a straight line using shearing blades, not by punch and die action. Therefore, the operation of cutting a cylindrical hole in a sheet of metal using a punch and die is known as punching . Hence, the correct answer is Option C: Punching ."

    question: In forehand welding, the angle between the welding torch and the work is ________ as compared to backhand welding.
    options:
      - Same
      - Less
      - More
      - None of these
    answer: 2
    explanation: "In forehand welding, the angle between the welding torch and the work is more as compared to backhand welding. This difference in the angle of the torch makes the two techniques distinct. Forehand Welding: In this technique, the welding torch is held at an angle greater than 90 degrees in relation to the direction of the weld. This means the torch points slightly forward in the direction of welding. It is often used for applications where deeper penetration and faster travel speeds are required. Backhand Welding: In contrast, backhand welding involves holding the torch at an angle less than 90 degrees, with the torch pointing slightly backward in the direction of welding. It is commonly used when a more shallow weld with less penetration is desired. So, the angle is \"more\" acute in forehand welding compared to backhand welding. This distinction in torch angles allows welders to control the depth and profile of the weld bead to suit the specific requirements of the welding project."

    question: In sheet metal work, he cutting force on the tool can be reduced by
    options:
      - Grinding the cutting edges sharp
      - Increasing the hardness of tool
      - Providing shear on tool
      - Increasing the hardness of die
    answer: 2
    explanation: Providing shear on the tool helps to reduce the cutting force in sheet metal operations. When shear is provided, the cutting action takes place progressively rather than simultaneously over the entire area, thereby decreasing the amount of material being cut at any instant. This progressive cutting lowers the cutting force, improves the quality of cut, and increases the tool life. Hence, providing shear on the tool effectively reduces the cutting force required during the operation.
