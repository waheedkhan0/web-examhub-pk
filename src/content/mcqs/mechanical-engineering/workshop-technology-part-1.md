    question: For obtaining a cup of diameter 25 mm and height 15 mm by drawing, the size of the round blank should be approximately
    options:
      - 42 mm
      - 44 mm
      - 46 mm
      - 48 mm
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The correct answer is (C) 46 mm . Here's why: In drawing operations, we start with a flat, round piece of metal (the blank) and form it into a desired shape, like a cup. To determine the initial blank size, we need to consider that the volume of material remains approximately constant during the drawing process. Imagine flattening out the cup. The area of that flattened-out material (the blank) must equal the surface area of the finished cup. A simplified way to estimate this for a cylindrical cup is: 1. Calculate the surface area of the cup (bottom circle + side wall). * Area of the bottom circle: π * (diameter/2)^2 = π * (25/2)^2 ≈ 490.87 mm 2 * Area of the side wall: π * diameter * height = π * 25 * 15 ≈ 1178.10 mm 2 * Total surface area ≈ 490.87 + 1178.10 = 1668.97 mm 2 2. Calculate the blank diameter : the area of the blank circle is equal to the total surface area of the cup. * Area of the blank circle = π * (diameter/2)^2 * π * (diameter/2)^2 = 1668.97 mm 2 * diameter/2= sqrt(1668.97/π) = 23.05 * diameter = 2 * 23.05 = 46.1 mm Therefore, the blank size is approximately 46 mm . In practice, a few more factors might influence the *exact* size (like material properties and thinning during drawing), but this gives a good estimate."

    question: Scribing block is used to
    options:
      - Hold the round bars during marking
      - Check the trueness of flat surfaces
      - Locate the centres of round bars
      - Check the surface roughness
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Option A: Hold the round bars during marking A scribing block is not meant to hold workpieces. Its design includes a heavy base and a scriber, but no clamping mechanism. For holding round bars, a V-block or other holding fixture is used. Therefore, this option is incorrect. Option B: Check the trueness of flat surfaces While a scribing block can be moved across a surface plate to detect variations in height, this is not its primary or most accurate function. Dedicated tools like surface plates, straight edges, or dial indicators are used to check the trueness of flat surfaces. Hence, this option is misleading and not correct in the context of its main purpose. Option C: Locate the centres of round bars This is the correct function. A scribing block, often used along with a V-block , is ideal for locating and marking the centre lines on cylindrical workpieces . By adjusting the scriber and rotating the bar, accurate centre marking can be achieved, which is essential for machining operations. Option D: Check the surface roughness Surface roughness is determined using roughness comparators or profilometers , not a scribing block. A scribing block has no capability to measure or compare the surface finish quality, so this option is incorrect. Conclusion: After evaluating all options, it is clear that the primary and correct use of a scribing block is to locate the centres of round bars . Therefore, the correct answer is Option C."

    question: Which of the following welding method uses a pool of molten metal?
    options:
      - Carbon arc welding
      - Submerged arc welding
      - TIG arc welding
      - MIG arc welding
    answer: 1
    explanation: Submerged arc welding (SAW) uses a pool of molten metal that is formed beneath a blanket of granular flux. The flux covers the arc and molten pool, preventing contamination from atmospheric gases like oxygen and nitrogen. This method provides deep penetration, high quality, and efficiency in welding thick materials. Carbon arc welding , TIG welding , and MIG welding also involve an arc, but they do not use the same submerged molten pool process as SAW. Hence, the welding method that uses a pool of molten metal is Submerged arc welding .

    question: Which one of the following material will require the largest size of riser for the same size of casting?
    options:
      - Aluminium
      - Cast iron
      - Steel
      - Copper
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The correct answer is (C) Steel. Here's why: What is a Riser? A riser is a reservoir of molten metal connected to the casting. Its main job is to compensate for shrinkage that happens as the metal cools and solidifies. Why different materials need different sized risers: Different metals shrink by different amounts when they cool down from liquid to solid. The greater the shrinkage, the bigger the riser needs to be to feed the casting and prevent voids (holes) from forming inside. Why Steel needs the biggest riser: Steel has the highest shrinkage rate among the given options (Aluminum, Cast Iron, Copper). This means it shrinks the most when it solidifies. Cast iron actually expands slightly during solidification, making it need the smallest riser. Aluminum and Copper shrink, but less than steel, so they require risers smaller than steel's. In short: Because steel shrinks the most as it solidifies, it needs the largest riser to supply enough extra metal to prevent defects in the final casting."

    question: In arc welding, the electric arc is produced between the work and the electrode by
    options:
      - Voltage
      - Flow of current
      - Contact resistance
      - All of these
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Arc welding is a fusion welding process where heat is generated to melt and join metals. The electric arc is created between the electrode and the workpiece, which produces intense heat. Voltage is responsible for initiating and maintaining the arc gap between the electrode and the workpiece. Flow of current provides the energy required to sustain the arc and generate heat for melting the metal. Contact resistance at the point of initial contact helps in momentarily generating heat, which assists in the arc initiation. Therefore, all these factors — voltage, current flow, and contact resistance — contribute to producing the electric arc in arc welding. Hence, the correct answer is Option D: All of these."
