    question: If the floor is supported at or near the bottom but top chords of a bridge is not braced, then the bridge is called
    options:
      - Deck type
      - Through type
      - Half through type
      - Double deck type
    answer: 2
    explanation: "In the design of steel bridges, the term \"through type\" refers to a bridge where the floor is supported at or near the bottom, but the top chords of the bridge are not braced. In other words, the traffic or load-carrying portion of the bridge passes through the structure formed by the trusses or girders without any obstruction or bracing at the top. This configuration provides an unobstructed path for traffic, allowing vehicles to pass through the bridge without any interference from structural members above. Through-type bridges are commonly used for spanning longer distances, such as over rivers or valleys, where a clear span is desirable to accommodate navigation or other considerations. Options A, C, and D are incorrect because: Option A: \"Deck type\" bridges have their traffic-carrying surface (deck) placed on top of, and typically supported by, the main girders or trusses of the bridge. Option C: \"Half through type\" bridges have characteristics that are intermediate between through-type and deck-type bridges, with the floor supported at or near the bottom and partial bracing at the top. Option D: \"Double deck type\" bridges have two levels of traffic-carrying surfaces, one above the other, with each level typically supported by its own set of girders or trusses. Therefore, the correct type of bridge described in the question is Option B: Through type."

    question: The effect of racking forces is considered in the design of i) lateral braces ii) chord members The correct answer is
    options:
      - Only (i)
      - Only (ii)
      - Both (i) and (ii)
      - None of the above
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Let's break down this question about designing steel structures in a simple way. First, what are racking forces ? Imagine you have a simple rectangular frame, like a picture frame or a doorframe. If you push hard on one of its top corners, the frame will try to get squashed and turn into a slanted shape, like a diamond or parallelogram. This sideways pushing force that tries to distort the shape is what we call a racking force . In real buildings, these forces usually come from things like strong wind pushing on a wall or an earthquake shaking the structure sideways. Now let's look at which parts of a steel structure's design consider these forces: i) Lateral braces Think of \"braces\" as diagonal bars or members that are specifically added to a rectangular frame. These diagonal pieces turn the squares or rectangles into stable triangles. Triangles are very strong and cannot easily be pushed sideways and distorted (racked). So, lateral braces are specifically designed to resist these racking forces . Their main job is to provide stability against horizontal forces and prevent the entire structure from collapsing or distorting sideways. ii) Chord members Chord members are the main horizontal (and sometimes vertical) parts of a frame or truss, like the top and bottom beams. While lateral braces *primarily prevent* the racking, the entire structure is a connected system. When racking forces act on a building, even if braces are present, the forces are still distributed throughout the entire frame. This means the chord members will also experience additional forces (like extra pushing, pulling, or bending) because of the racking effect on the overall structure. For example, the ends of the chords might experience more stress where they connect to columns. Therefore, when engineers design these main chord members, they must also consider these additional forces caused by racking to make sure the chords are strong enough and don't fail under all possible load conditions. In summary: - Lateral braces are directly designed to resist racking forces and maintain the frame's shape. - Chord members are also affected by racking forces , and their design must account for the stresses that racking induces in them as part of the overall structural response. Since both elements are important and their design must consider the effects of racking forces, the correct answer is Option C: Both (i) and (ii) ."
