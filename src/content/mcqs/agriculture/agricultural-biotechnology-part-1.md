    question: Rare cutter is
    options:
      - Hae III
      - Alu I
      - Pvu II
      - Eco R I
    answer: 3
    explanation: 1. Rare cutters are restriction enzymes that recognize longer and less frequent sequences in DNA. - These enzymes cut DNA at specific recognition sites, usually 6 to 8 base pairs long. 2. Eco R I is a rare cutter. - It recognizes the specific sequence 5'-GAATTC-3', which occurs less frequently in the genome compared to shorter recognition sequences. 3. Rare cutters are useful in genome mapping and cloning. - They generate larger DNA fragments, making them ideal for analyzing larger genomic regions. 4. Options like Hae III and Alu I are frequent cutters. - They recognize shorter sequences and cut more frequently, producing smaller fragments. 5. Option D is correct as Eco R I is classified as a rare cutter due to its longer recognition sequence.

    question: During splicing of proteins which part is removed
    options:
      - Exons
      - Introns
      - Exteins
      - Inteins
    answer: 3
    explanation: 1. Splicing of proteins refers to the process where specific sequences within a protein are removed and the remaining parts are joined together. 2. Inteins are the parts removed during protein splicing. - They are internal protein sequences that excise themselves from a precursor protein. 3. Exteins are the remaining protein sequences. - These sequences are joined together to form the functional protein after the removal of inteins. 4. Protein splicing is an autocatalytic process. - It does not require external enzymes and occurs naturally in some organisms. 5. Option D is correct because inteins are removed during protein splicing, leaving exteins to form the final protein.

    question: For cloning of the gene, which among following is not improtant
    options:
      - Vector
      - Host
      - Restriction enzymes
      - DNase
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Gene cloning is like making photocopies of a specific gene. To do this, we need some important tools. A vector is like a vehicle that carries the gene we want to copy. Think of it as a special type of DNA that can easily enter a cell. A host is the living organism (like a bacteria) that will receive the gene and make many copies of it. It's where our copied gene will live and multiply. Restriction enzymes are like molecular scissors. They cut the DNA at specific spots, allowing us to precisely insert our gene into the vector. DNase is an enzyme that breaks down DNA. In gene cloning, we want to preserve the DNA, not destroy it, so we don't need DNase. It would actually prevent the cloning process from working properly. Therefore, the answer is D: DNase because it is not needed for gene cloning."
