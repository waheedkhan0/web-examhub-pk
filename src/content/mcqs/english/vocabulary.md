    question: "Choose the nearest synonym of ABATE: the violence of the storm soon abated."
    options:
      - subside
      - intensify
      - recur
      - contract
    answer: 0
    explanation: To abate is to become less violent or widespread, so 'subside' is the closest match. 'Intensify' is the opposite, 'recur' means happen again, and 'contract' means shrink or bind by agreement.
    keyConcept: abate (verb) = lessen in force or degree (an abated storm, an abated nuisance)
    examTip: Never confuse abate with abdicate (resign a throne) — examiners print both to catch careless readers.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 2
    question: "Choose the word closest in meaning to ACUMEN: she closed the deal with real business acumen."
    options:
      - obstinacy
      - eloquence
      - shrewdness
      - haste
    answer: 2
    explanation: Acumen is the ability to judge correctly and decide quickly, so 'shrewdness' carries the same sense. 'Obstinacy' is stubbornness, 'eloquence' is fluency in speech, and 'haste' is undue speed.
    keyConcept: acumen = keen practical judgment (business acumen, mental acuity)
    examTip: "'Acumen' is always a praising word; if an option names a fault of character, discard it at once."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PMS
  - id: 3
    question: "Choose the nearest synonym of ALLEVIATE: the relief camp sought to alleviate the suffering of the flood victims."
    options:
      - aggravate
      - prolong
      - levitate
      - relieve
    answer: 3
    explanation: To alleviate is to make pain or hardship less severe, which is exactly what 'relieve' means. 'Aggravate' means make worse, 'prolong' means extend in time, and 'levitate' means rise into the air — a look-alike trap set for readers of 'alleviate'.
    keyConcept: alleviate = relieve in severity (suffering, distress, shortage)
    examTip: "Alleviate is not eliminate: the hardship is reduced, not removed — a distinction examiners test in sentence-completion items."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 4
    question: "Choose the antonym of ARDENT as used here: he made an ardent appeal for reform."
    options:
      - fervid
      - lukewarm
      - glowing
      - fervent
    answer: 1
    explanation: Ardent means passionately enthusiastic, so its opposite is lukewarm, meaning half-hearted. 'Fervid', 'glowing' and 'fervent' are all synonyms of ardent, leaving 'lukewarm' as the only antonym.
    keyConcept: ardent = passionate, zealously warm (an ardent supporter)
    examTip: Do not read 'ardent' as 'accidental' — in antonym items the examiner often hides the trap inside a familiar-looking word.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
  - id: 5
    question: "Choose the word closest in meaning to BENEVOLENT: the trust was founded by a benevolent merchant family."
    options:
      - charitable
      - malevolent
      - austere
      - garrulous
    answer: 0
    explanation: Benevolent means kindly and disposed to do good, so 'charitable' is the closest synonym. 'Malevolent' is the exact opposite (wishing harm), 'austere' means stern and plain, and 'garrulous' means excessively talkative.
    keyConcept: bene- (good) + vol (wish) = wishing well; contrast malevolent (mal = bad)
    examTip: "The prefix pair bene- / male- decides the answer in dozens of these items: benign, benevolent, benefactor against malevolent, malign."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 6
    question: "In the sentence 'her candid reply surprised the panel', the word CANDID most nearly means:"
    options:
      - frank
      - reserved
      - astute
      - cautious
    answer: 0
    explanation: Candid means truthful and straightforward, saying what one really thinks, so 'frank' is the synonym. 'Reserved' and 'cautious' describe people who hold back, and 'astute' means sharp in judging, which is a different quality.
    keyConcept: candid = open and sincere (a candid photograph = an unrehearsed one)
    examTip: "'Candid' is not 'candidate', and a candid person is not necessarily blunt; in synonym items choose openness, never rudeness."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PMS
  - id: 7
    question: "Choose the nearest synonym of CEASELESS: the machine ran with ceaseless vigour."
    options:
      - intermittent
      - incessant
      - careless
      - motionless
    answer: 1
    explanation: Ceaseless means never stopping, the same as 'incessant'. 'Intermittent' is the opposite (stopping and starting), while 'careless' and 'motionless' trade on the '-less' ending without sharing the meaning.
    keyConcept: cease + -less = without stopping; movement is a different idea
    examTip: A word built on 'cease' (to stop) refers to action continuing, not to movement, so 'motionless' is a decoy.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 8
    question: "Choose the word closest in meaning to EBB as used here: hopes of a quick settlement began to ebb."
    options:
      - advance
      - overflow
      - recede
      - sink
    answer: 2
    explanation: To ebb is to draw back or grow weaker, like the tide going out, so 'recede' matches. 'Advance' and 'overflow' describe the tide coming in or spilling over, and 'sink' means go down below a surface rather than withdraw.
    keyConcept: ebb (verb and noun) = recede or decline; the ebb and flow of fortune
    examTip: The fixed pair is 'the ebb and flow'; if an option reverses that pair, it is the antonym, not the synonym.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 9
    question: "Choose the antonym of FRUGAL as used here: he lived a frugal life and saved for his daughters' education."
    options:
      - thrifty
      - extravagant
      - economical
      - sparing
    answer: 1
    explanation: Frugal means careful about money and simple in living, so the opposite is extravagant, meaning spending far more than is needed. 'Thrifty', 'economical' and 'sparing' all agree with frugal.
    keyConcept: frugal = thrifty by choice; parsimonious = meanly sparing (a stronger, negative word)
    examTip: In antonym items three options are usually synonyms of the stem word — find the odd one out instead of hunting blindly for an opposite.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - PMS
  - id: 10
    question: "In the sentence 'he read the notice in a lugubrious tone', the word LUGUBRIOUS most nearly means:"
    options:
      - cheerful
      - flippant
      - turgid
      - mournful
    answer: 3
    explanation: Lugubrious means dismal or sorrowful in manner and sound, so 'mournful' is the synonym. 'Cheerful' is the opposite, 'flippant' means frivolously disrespectful, and 'turgid' means swollen or bombastic in style — a look-alike trap.
    keyConcept: lugubrious = mournful, doleful (from Latin lugere, to lament)
    examTip: Lugubrious describes grief, not dullness; do not pick a 'boring' or 'slow' option even when it looks plausible.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - FPSC
  - id: 11
    question: "Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning to MAGNANIMOUS: the victorious party was magnanimous towards its defeated rivals."
    options:
      - generous
      - noble
      - petty
      - magnificent
    answer: 2
    explanation: Magnanimous means great-hearted, generous in forgiving and free from spite, so its opposite is petty, meaning small-minded and mean. 'Generous' and 'noble' are close synonyms, and 'magnificent' means splendid, which is merely similar in sound.
    keyConcept: magna- (great) + animus (spirit) = noble in feeling or conduct
    examTip: Pair it with the prefix magn- family (magnate, magnificence, magniloquent) to keep it apart from 'magnet' and 'magnificent'.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 12
    question: "Choose the nearest synonym of OBTUSE as used here: he was obtuse about the hint they dropped."
    options:
      - dull
      - keen
      - abrupt
      - polite
    answer: 0
    explanation: Applied to a person, obtuse means slow to notice or understand, so 'dull' is the synonym. 'Keen' is the opposite, and 'abrupt' is the trap for readers who take obtuse in its geometric sense of blunt rather than sharp.
    keyConcept: "obtuse = mentally blunt, slow to grasp (also: an obtuse angle exceeds 90 degrees)"
    examTip: Obtuse describes understanding, never manners; if an option means rude or sudden, it belongs to 'abrupt' or 'brusque'.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PMS
  - id: 13
    question: "In the sentence 'she was reticent about her own achievements', the word RETICENT most nearly means:"
    options:
      - effusive
      - reserved
      - reckless
      - relevant
    answer: 1
    explanation: Reticent means restrained and unwilling to reveal one's thoughts, so 'reserved' is the synonym. 'Effusive' is the opposite, pouring out feeling without restraint, while 'reckless' and 'relevant' merely resemble the word in shape.
    keyConcept: reticent = silent by temperament (from tacere, to be silent); reluctant = unwilling to act
    examTip: "Reticent is about speech, reluctant about action: a reticent person still does the work, a reluctant person does not want to."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
  - id: 14
    question: "Choose the antonym of VERBOSE as used here: the judge criticised the verbose submission."
    options:
      - wordy
      - garrulous
      - prolix
      - concise
    answer: 3
    explanation: Verbose means using far more words than needed, so the opposite is concise, meaning brief and clear. 'Wordy' and 'prolix' are direct synonyms, and 'garrulous' is the talkative counterpart applied to people.
    keyConcept: verbose = wordy; concise, terse, laconic = using few words
    examTip: In an antonym question all three distractors may be synonyms of the stem word; the four options are not always the same part of speech, so check what the sentence needs.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - NTS
