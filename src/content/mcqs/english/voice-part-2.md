    question: Circumstances will oblige me to go.
    options:
      - I will oblige the circumstances and go.
      - I shall be obliged to go by the circumstances.
      - Under the circumstances, I should go.
      - I shall be obliged by the circumstances to go.
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The given sentence, “Circumstances will oblige me to go.” , is in the Active Voice . Here, “circumstances” is the subject, “will oblige” is the verb, and “me” is the object. To change it into the Passive Voice , the object “me” becomes the subject. The verb phrase “will oblige” changes to “will be obliged” . Thus: Active: Circumstances will oblige me to go. Passive: I shall be obliged to go by the circumstances. Why Option B is correct: The meaning and grammatical structure of the original sentence are correctly retained. “Me” becomes “I” , and “will oblige” changes to the passive construction “shall be obliged” . Why the other options are incorrect: Option A: Incorrect. It does not change the sentence into the passive voice; it merely rearranges the meaning. Option C: Incorrect. “I should go” changes the meaning and does not form the passive voice of the given sentence. Option D: Incorrect. The phrase “obliged by the circumstances to go” is awkward in this construction. The standard passive transformation is “I shall be obliged to go by the circumstances.” Final Answer: Option B — I shall be obliged to go by the circumstances."

Let's analyze each option: Option A: \"Our colleague gave us a gift when she retired.\" - This is in active voice and changes the meaning of the original sentence. Option C: \"A gift was given to our colleague when she retired.\" - This is the correct transformation to passive voice. It correctly states that \"a gift\" (the object in active voice) is now the subject, and \"our colleague\" (the subject in active voice) is now the object. Option D: \"We gave our colleague a gift when she retired.\" - This is in active voice and changes the meaning of the original sentence.     question: I don't like people keeping me waiting.
    options:
      - I don’t like people are kept me waiting.
      - I don’t like people were kept me waiting.
      - I don’t like being kept waiting.
      - I don’t like people are being kept waiting
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Active voice : The subject performs the action (e.g., \"I threw the ball\"). Passive voice : The subject receives the action (e.g., \"The ball was thrown by me\"). The original sentence, \"I don't like people keeping me waiting,\" is in the active voice. \"People\" is the subject performing the action of \"keeping me waiting\". Option A: \"I don’t like people are kept me waiting.\" This is grammatically incorrect. The verb tense is wrong; it incorrectly uses the continuous tense (\"are kept\") and doesn't match the meaning of the original sentence. Option B: \"I don’t like people were kept me waiting.\" This is also grammatically incorrect for the same reason as option A. It uses the past continuous incorrectly. Option C: \"I don’t like being kept waiting.\" This is the correct answer. It uses the passive voice correctly. The speaker is the one being kept waiting, making \"being kept waiting\" the object of the dislike. Option D: \"I don’t like people are being kept waiting.\" This is grammatically incorrect. Similar to options A and B, it uses the continuous tense wrongly and doesn't reflect the meaning of the original sentence. The sentence focuses on the speaker's dislike of the action being done to *them*, not a general observation about people being kept waiting."
