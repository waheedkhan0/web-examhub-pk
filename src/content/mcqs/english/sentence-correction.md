    question: "Choose the correct form: The number of complaints against the department ___ still increasing."
    options:
      - are
      - is
      - were
      - have
    answer: 1
    explanation: The subject is 'the number', a singular noun, so the singular 'is' agrees with it; the plural 'complaints' sits inside a prepositional phrase and cannot govern the verb. 'Have' would need a plural subject too.
    keyConcept: the number of + plural noun + SINGULAR verb; a number of + plural noun + PLURAL verb
    examTip: "Remember the pair: 'A number of clerks were absent' (many) but 'The number of clerks is falling' (a figure)."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 2
    question: "Choose the correct form: Neither of the two audit reports ___ signed by the engagement partner."
    options:
      - was
      - were
      - have been
      - are
    answer: 0
    explanation: "'Neither' means not one and not the other, so it is singular and takes the singular verb 'was', and the noun after 'of' stays plural ('neither of the reports'). 'Were' and 'have been' are the tempting plural errors."
    keyConcept: neither of + plural noun + SINGULAR verb; either of likewise singular
    examTip: Treat neither, either, each, everyone and anyone as singular; the plural noun after 'of' never pulls the verb into the plural in exam keys.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 3
    question: "Choose the correct word: Hardly had the delegates taken their seats ___ the session began."
    options:
      - than
      - then
      - when
      - that
    answer: 2
    explanation: "'Hardly' (like 'scarcely') is a temporal adverb, so it is followed by 'when' introducing the second event. 'Than' belongs only to 'no sooner', and 'then' cannot join the two clauses."
    keyConcept: hardly / scarcely... WHEN...; no sooner... THAN...
    examTip: "Mnemonic: the words that mean 'almost not' take 'when'; only the word containing 'sooner', a comparative, takes 'than'."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PMS
  - id: 4
    question: "Choose the correct word: No sooner had the chairman struck the gavel ___ the delegates began to protest."
    options:
      - the instant
      - when
      - as soon as
      - than
    answer: 3
    explanation: "'No sooner' is a comparative, so the pair is 'no sooner... than'. 'When' and 'as soon as' would belong to a different construction ('the instant the gavel fell, the delegates protested'), and 'the instant' cannot follow a comparative."
    keyConcept: no sooner... THAN (never 'then' or 'when')
    examTip: If the stem says 'sooner', answer 'than'; if it says 'hardly', 'scarcely' or 'no sooner had' with no comparative, answer 'when'.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS

    question: "Choose the correct word: The candidate ___ the committee interviewed separately later withdrew his application."
    options:
      - whom
      - who
      - whose
      - which
    answer: 0
    explanation: The relative pronoun is the object of 'interviewed' (the committee interviewed him), so the objective case 'whom' is required. 'Who' would be the subject of the clause, and 'which' cannot refer to a person.
    keyConcept: who = subject case; whom = object case after a verb or preposition
    examTip: "Read the clause alone and supply 'he' or 'him': if 'him' fits (interviewed him), the answer is 'whom'."

    question: "Choose the correct form: Between you and ___, the new recruit has been asked to resign."
    options:
      - I
      - myself
      - me
      - mine
    answer: 2
    explanation: "'Between' is a preposition and governs the objective case, so the correct phrase is 'between you and me'. 'Myself' is a reflexive and cannot replace the object of a preposition, and 'I' would make the phrase nominative."
    keyConcept: "objective case after prepositions: between you and me, it was me, for her and him"
    examTip: "'Between you and I' and 'myself' are the two favourite traps; every pronoun after between, let, make or for takes the object form."

    question: "Choose the correct form: If the driver ___ the junction any sooner, we would have caught the last bus."
    options:
      - reached
      - has reached
      - had reached
      - would reach
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The result clause 'would have caught' is in the third conditional, so the if-clause must use the past perfect: 'had reached'. A simple past 'reached' would pair with 'would catch', which refers to the present or future."
    keyConcept: "unreal past condition: If + past perfect... would have + past participle"
    examTip: "Match the halves: would have + participle asks for had + participle; would + verb asks for the simple past."

  - id: 9
    question: "Choose the correct word: The new schedule is expected to ___ a significant change in the way registrations are done."
    options:
      - affect
      - impart
      - inflict
      - effect
    answer: 3
    explanation: Here the verb means bring about, which is the verb 'effect' (as in effect a change, effect a reform). 'Affect' means influence and would take the thing influenced as its object, not the change itself; 'impart' means convey, and 'inflict' needs 'on'.
    keyConcept: affect (verb) = influence; effect (noun) = result; effect (verb) = bring about
    examTip: If the object is a change, reform or cure, choose 'effect'; if the object is a person, decision or health, choose 'affect'.

  - id: 11
    question: "Choose the punctuation that correctly fills the blank: The report was tabled late ___ however, the members still read it."
    options:
      - a comma
      - a semicolon
      - a colon
      - no punctuation
    answer: 1
    explanation: Two independent clauses joined by the conjunctive adverb 'however' need a semicolon before it and a comma after it. A comma alone produces a comma splice, a colon cannot introduce 'however', and no punctuation runs the clauses together.
    keyConcept: semicolon joins two independent clauses linked by however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless
    examTip: A full stop before 'However' would also be correct, but it is not offered here; between the marks listed, only the semicolon works.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 12
    question: "Choose the correct form: The unsigned file ___ unnoticed on the registrar's table for a whole week."
    options:
      - lain
      - lay
      - laid
      - lied
    answer: 1
    explanation: The file was itself in a place, so the intransitive verb 'lie' is needed, and its past form is 'lay'. 'Laid' is the past of the transitive 'lay', which requires an object, 'lain' needs has or had, and 'lied' means told a falsehood.
    keyConcept: lie-lay-lain (no object) against lay-laid-laid (object required)
    examTip: "Ask whether the verb has an object: a thing that rests lies; a person that puts it there lays it."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 13
    question: "Choose the correct article: The officer was posted to ___ European country for two years."
    options:
      - a
      - an
      - the
      - no article
    answer: 0
    explanation: "The choice of article depends on sound, not spelling: 'European' opens with the consonant sound /j/ (as in 'yes'), so it takes 'a'. 'An' is used only before a vowel sound, which is why the same word would be 'an' in 'an embassy'."
    keyConcept: a before consonant sounds (a European, a university, a one-rupee note); an before vowel sounds (an honour, an hour)
    examTip: Silent h takes 'an' (an honest man, an heir), sounded u takes 'a' (a useful tool, a union).
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 14
    question: "Choose the correct form: So determined ___ to win the case that she refused every compromise."
    options:
      - she was
      - was she
      - is she
      - she is
    answer: 1
    explanation: When 'so + adjective' is placed at the beginning for emphasis, the verb and subject invert, giving 'So determined was she'. Ordinary statement order is not allowed after this fronting, and the present 'is she' clashes with the past 'refused'.
    keyConcept: "negative or emphatic fronting causes inversion: so... was she, never had I, rarely does she"
    examTip: The same inversion follows 'not only', 'no sooner', 'hardly', 'never' and 'seldom' at the start of a sentence.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
