  - id: 1
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The clerk was accused ___ forging official signatures."
    options:
      - for
      - of
      - with
      - in
    answer: 1
    explanation: The fixed pattern is 'accuse somebody OF something', so the clerk was accused of forging signatures. 'Accused for' is the common interference error, and 'accused with' really belongs to the different verb 'charged with'.
    keyConcept: accuse OF a crime; charge WITH a crime
    examTip: Examiners pair 'accused of' with 'charged with' — the preposition changes with the verb, not with the offence.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 2
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The tennis match was called ___ twice because of heavy rain."
    options:
      - up
      - out
      - down
      - "off"
    answer: 3
    explanation: "'Call off' means to cancel, so a match that is cancelled is called off. 'Call up' means to summon or phone, 'call out' means to order to strike or shout, and 'call down' means to reprimand — none of them expresses cancellation."
    keyConcept: Phrasal verb CALL OFF = cancel; CALL UP = summon
    examTip: "Do not mix this with 'put off', which means postpone: a postponed match is put off, a cancelled match is called off."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 3
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The staff can no longer put ___ with the supervisor's rudeness."
    options:
      - "off"
      - up
      - out
      - back
    answer: 1
    explanation: The three-word verb meaning tolerate is 'put up with', and the sentence already supplies the 'with'. 'Put off' means postpone, 'put out' means extinguish or inconvenience, and 'put back' means return, so only 'up' completes the idiom.
    keyConcept: PUT UP WITH = tolerate (three-word verb)
    examTip: When the particle 'with' is already printed after the blank, look for 'up' — 'put up with' is a favourite of paper setters.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PMS
  - id: 4
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The findings of the new survey are quite different ___ the earlier estimates."
    options:
      - from
      - against
      - across
      - beyond
    answer: 0
    explanation: "The adjective 'different' governs 'from': findings are different from estimates. 'Against', 'across' and 'beyond' form no accepted collocation with 'different'."
    keyConcept: different FROM; things differ FROM each other, persons differ WITH each other
    examTip: In objective papers always mark 'different from'; 'different to' (British speech) and 'different than' (American speech) exist but are avoided in exam keys.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 5
    question: "Choose the correct word: It is five years ___ the two brothers last spoke to each other."
    options:
      - for
      - from
      - since
      - by
    answer: 2
    explanation: In the pattern 'It is (or has been) + period + clause referring to the past', the required word is 'since'. 'For' is used before a bare period ('they have not spoken for five years'), and 'from' cannot introduce such a clause.
    keyConcept: since + point of time or past clause; for + length of time
    examTip: "Spot the blank: if what follows is a clause or a point of time, use since; if a stretch of time stands alone, use for."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 6
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: He is senior ___ me by three years of service."
    options:
      - than
      - to
      - from
      - over
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Latin comparatives take 'to', never 'than': senior to, junior to, superior to, inferior to, prior to. 'Senior than' is tempting because ordinary English adjectives do take 'than'."
    keyConcept: senior / junior / superior / inferior / prior + TO
    examTip: The same 'to' also appears in 'with a view to' and 'in addition to' — treat a Latin comparative followed by 'than' as an error.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - PMS
  - id: 7
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The driver took a shortcut, so we reached the hospital ___ time to see the patient before he was shifted."
    options:
      - in
      - "on"
      - at
      - by
    answer: 0
    explanation: "'In time' means soon enough for a purpose and takes the pattern 'in time to do something', which is what the infinitive here requires. 'On time' means punctual against a fixed schedule and is not followed by an infinitive of this kind."
    keyConcept: in time = with time to spare; on time = punctual as scheduled
    examTip: A meeting or a train starts on time; an emergency or a deadline is met in time ('in the nick of time').
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 8
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The acquisition fell ___ when the buyer withdrew at the last minute."
    options:
      - "off"
      - away
      - through
      - out
    answer: 2
    explanation: "'Fall through' means fail to materialise, which suits a deal that collapses. 'Fall out' means quarrel, 'fall away' means diminish gradually, and 'fall off' means decrease or drop from a surface."
    keyConcept: FALL THROUGH = fail, come to nothing (of plans and agreements)
    examTip: "Pair it with 'call off' and 'put off': examiners rotate these three phrasal verbs across one English section."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - FPSC
  - id: 9
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: She takes ___ her grandmother in her love of classical music."
    options:
      - for
      - "on"
      - to
      - after
    answer: 3
    explanation: "'Take after' means resemble a relative, the sense required by 'her grandmother'. 'Take for' means mistake one person for another, 'take to' means develop a liking or habit, and 'take on' means employ or accept."
    keyConcept: TAKE AFTER = resemble; TAKE TO = begin to like or do
    examTip: "'Take after' is used of family resemblance only — you cannot take after a friend."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 10
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The simplified rules did away ___ the requirement of a no-objection certificate."
    options:
      - of
      - with
      - by
      - from
    answer: 1
    explanation: "'Do away with' is the fixed three-word verb meaning abolish, so rules do away with a requirement. 'Do with' appears only in questions and negatives such as 'what did you do with it', and 'of', 'by' and 'from' cannot follow 'do away'."
    keyConcept: DO AWAY WITH = abolish; MAKE DO WITH = manage with what one has
    examTip: Keep 'do away with' apart from 'done with' ('I am done with that job') — one abolishes a rule, the other ends a personal involvement.
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - FPSC
  - id: 11
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The revised schedule of rates has come ___ effect from the date of its publication."
    options:
      - in
      - to
      - into
      - "on"
    answer: 2
    explanation: The idiom is 'come into effect', meaning begin to operate; the noun 'effect' also combines with 'in' in the different phrase 'in effect' (= actually). 'Come to effect' and 'come on effect' are not English.
    keyConcept: come INTO effect = begin to operate; take effect; in effect = actually
    examTip: Motion requires 'into' (come into force, come into operation); a static state uses 'in' (in force, in operation).
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
  - id: 12
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: He joined the evening course with a view ___ improving his English."
    options:
      - of
      - for
      - in
      - to
    answer: 3
    explanation: "The phrase is 'with a view to', and the 'to' is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund: with a view to improving. 'For improving' is the usual tempting error, but the idiom admits no other preposition."
    keyConcept: with a view TO + gerund (= with the intention of)
    examTip: "Because this 'to' is prepositional, never follow it with a base verb: 'with a view to improve' is wrong."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 13
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: The prize money was divided equally ___ the two winners."
    options:
      - between
      - among
      - across
      - along
    answer: 0
    explanation: "'Between' is used when two items or persons are involved, as in 'the two winners'. 'Among' is reserved for more than two, and 'across' and 'along' express distribution over a space or a line, not sharing."
    keyConcept: between = two parties; among = three or more
    examTip: "Count the nouns: 'between' for a pair, 'among' for a crowd — examiners love to write 'among the two brothers'."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 14
    question: "Choose the correct preposition: Do you agree ___ me that this rule needs amendment?"
    options:
      - to
      - "on"
      - with
      - in
    answer: 2
    explanation: You agree WITH a person, so 'agree with me' is correct. 'Agree to' takes a proposal or plan, and 'agree on' takes the point settled by both sides; neither can govern the pronoun 'me'.
    keyConcept: agree WITH a person; agree TO a proposal; agree ON a date or plan
    examTip: If the words after the blank name a person, choose 'with'; if they name a scheme or terms, choose 'to'.
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - NTS
