    question: If a D.C. motor is connected across the A.C. supply it will
    options:
      - Run at normal speed
      - Not run
      - Run at lower speed
      - Burn due to heat produced in the field winding by eddy currents
    answer: 3
    explanation: If a D.C. motor is connected across an A.C. supply, it will burn due to the heat produced in the field winding by eddy currents. The field windings in a D.C. motor are designed for direct current, and when subjected to alternating current, the changing magnetic fields induce eddy currents in the windings. These eddy currents generate excessive heat, leading to potential damage or burning of the motor.

    question: In case of D.C. shunt motors the speed is dependent on back e.m.f. only because
    options:
      - Back e.m.f. is equal to armature drop
      - Armature drop is negligible
      - Flux is proportional to armature current
      - Flux is practically constant in D.C. shunt motors
    answer: 3
    explanation: In the case of D.C. shunt motors, the speed is dependent on back e.m.f. only because Flux is practically constant in D.C. shunt motors. In a shunt motor, the field winding is connected in parallel with the armature winding. This configuration ensures that the flux (magnetic field) remains relatively constant, regardless of changes in load or speed. As a result, the back electromotive force (e.m.f.) generated in the armature is primarily determined by the speed of the motor and not significantly affected by changes in the flux. Therefore, the correct option is Flux is practically constant in D.C. shunt motors .

    question: Which D.C. motor has got maximum self loading property?
    options:
      - Series motor
      - Shunt motor
      - Cumulatively compounded motor
      - Differentially compounded motor
    answer: 0
    explanation: "Self-loading property refers to a motor's ability to automatically increase its load when the speed decreases slightly. In simpler terms, it means how well a motor can handle sudden increases in the amount of work it needs to do. Option A: Series motor This statement is correct . Series motors have a high starting torque and naturally increase their torque output as the speed decreases. This makes them ideal for applications requiring heavy loads from the start, such as cranes or electric trains. The self-loading property is especially high in series motors because as the load increases, the current increases, which in turn increases the magnetic field and torque. This is why series motors are commonly used in high-torque applications where the motor needs to handle sudden increases in load. Option B: Shunt motor This statement is incorrect . Shunt motors maintain a relatively constant speed regardless of changes in load. While they are efficient and provide stable operation, they do not exhibit the self-loading property like series motors. The field winding in a shunt motor is connected in parallel to the armature, so the field current remains constant and does not increase as the load increases. This means that the torque does not increase significantly with load, leading to a poor self-loading property. Option C: Cumulatively compounded motor This statement is incorrect . Cumulatively compounded motors combine both series and shunt motor characteristics. They have a good balance between starting torque and speed regulation, but their self-loading property is not as high as a series motor. While they do have some self-loading features due to the series winding, they are not as efficient at increasing torque when speed decreases, making their self-loading property less pronounced compared to a series motor. Option D: Differentially compounded motor This statement is incorrect . Differentially compounded motors have poor starting torque and can even become unstable under heavy loads. They have the worst self-loading property of all the D.C. motor types. The field winding configuration in these motors causes the magnetic flux to oppose the armature flux, resulting in decreased torque and an inability to handle sudden increases in load effectively. Therefore, the correct answer is Option A: Series motor , as it has the maximum self-loading property due to its high starting torque and ability to automatically adjust to load changes by increasing its torque output as speed decreases."

    question: At the instant of starting when a D.C. motor is put on supply, it behaves like
    options:
      - A highly resistive circuit
      - A low resistance circuit
      - A capacitive circuit
      - None of the above
    answer: 1
    explanation: "At the instant of starting, when a D.C. motor is put on supply, it initially behaves like a highly resistive circuit . This behavior is primarily due to the low resistance of the armature windings and brushes, which offer minimal opposition to the flow of current. As a result, the motor draws a high starting current, often several times higher than its rated current, to overcome the initial inertia and start rotating. This high starting current, combined with the low resistance of the circuit, results in a situation akin to a highly resistive circuit. As the motor gains speed and reaches its operating condition, the resistance decreases, and the motor settles into its normal operating state. Options B and C are incorrect because: Option B: A low resistance circuit implies minimal opposition to the flow of current, which is not characteristic of a D.C. motor at the instant of starting. Option C: A capacitive circuit involves the storage and release of electrical energy in a capacitor, which is not relevant to the behavior of a D.C. motor at startup. Therefore, the correct answer is Option A: A highly resistive circuit, as this best describes the initial behavior of a D.C. motor when it is put on supply."

    question: Which method of braking is generally used in elevators?
    options:
      - Plugging
      - Regenerative braking
      - Rheostat braking
      - None of the above
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Regenerative braking: This method is highly efficient and is widely used in elevators. In regenerative braking, the motor acts as a generator, converting the kinetic energy of the elevator into electrical energy, which is then fed back into the power supply. This results in a significant energy-saving benefit while ensuring a smooth and controlled stop. Why it's preferred for elevators: Efficiency: Regenerative braking does not waste energy as heat, unlike rheostat braking. It instead recovers the energy and returns it to the system, which is a major advantage in reducing operational costs and improving the system's overall efficiency. Safety and Reliability: Regenerative braking causes less wear and tear on the motor and other components compared to methods like plugging. It provides a reliable and smooth deceleration, which is critical for the safe operation of elevators. Comparison with other methods: Option A: Plugging: This involves reversing the polarity of the motor's voltage, generating a counter-torque that brings the elevator to a stop. While effective, it creates more wear on the motor and is not ideal for frequent use like in elevators. Option C: Rheostat braking: This method adds resistance to the motor circuit to dissipate energy as heat. While simple, it is less efficient because it wastes energy and is not as effective as regenerative braking in terms of energy recovery. Therefore, regenerative braking is the most efficient, safe, and reliable method for elevators, making it the best choice for frequent and smooth braking in such systems."
