Step 2: Why Compound Generators Are Preferred? - They maintain a stable voltage when loads change. - They ensure equal load sharing between generators. - They prevent overloading of a single generator when connected in parallel. Step 3: Analyzing the Given Options - Option A: Shunt generators (Not preferred due to poor voltage regulation under varying loads.) - Option B: Series generators (Not suitable as they do not work effectively in parallel operation.) - Option C: Compound generators (Correct, as they provide better voltage stability and load sharing.) - Option D: None of the above (Incorrect, as compound generators are the correct choice.)     question: In a D.C. generator the critical resistance can be increased by
    options:
      - Increasing its field resistance
      - Decreasing its field resistance
      - Increasing its speed
      - Decreasing its speed
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Explanation: The critical resistance is the maximum field resistance for a DC generator to begin voltage buildup or excitation. It is the resistance represented by the tangent to the O.C.C (Open Circuit Characteristic) curve of a DC generator. The O.C.C curve shows the relationship between the generated EMF and field current at a constant speed . If we increase the speed of the generator, the generated EMF increases at a given field current. This means the O.C.C curve shifts upwards . A steeper O.C.C curve (at higher speed) allows for a higher tangent , hence a higher critical resistance. Changing the field resistance doesn't directly affect the critical resistance . It affects the operating point after the generator has built up voltage, but not the initial build-up. Therefore, the correct answer is increasing its speed . Option C is the correct answer."

    question: For both lap and wave windings, there are as many commutator bars as the number of
    options:
      - Slots
      - Armature conductors
      - Winding elements
      - Poles
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Commutator bars: In both lap and wave windings of a DC generator, the number of commutator bars is equal to the number of armature conductors. Reason: Each armature conductor is connected to a commutator bar, and the commutator ensures the reversal of current direction as the armature rotates. This reversal is necessary for maintaining unidirectional current output in the external circuit. Lap winding: In lap winding, the number of commutator bars is the same as the number of armature coils, which corresponds to the number of armature conductors. Wave winding: Similarly, in wave winding, the number of commutator bars also equals the number of armature conductors, ensuring proper commutation for each conductor. Thus, the correct relationship is that the number of commutator bars equals the number of armature conductors."

    question: In case of a 4 pole D.C. generator provided with a two layer lap winding with sixteen coils, the pole pitch will be
    options:
      - "4"
      - "8"
      - "16"
      - "32"
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Here's an explanation to help you understand the question: First, let's define pole pitch . It is the distance between the center of two adjacent poles in a DC generator. To calculate the pole pitch, we need to know the total number of slots or coil sides around the armature and the number of poles . We are given: * Number of poles (P) = 4 * Number of coils = 16 * Two layer winding means each coil has two coil sides. Since it's a two-layer winding, the total number of coil sides is twice the number of coils: 16 coils * 2 coil sides/coil = 32 coil sides. In a lap winding, the number of coil sides equals the number of armature conductors or slots. So, we have 32 slots. The formula for pole pitch is: Pole Pitch = (Total Number of Slots) / (Number of Poles) So, Pole Pitch = 32 / 4 = 8 Therefore, the correct answer is Option B: 8 ."

    answer: 2

Option A: Rising voltage characteristics Rising voltage characteristics are not suitable for parallel operation as they could cause instability, leading to one generator taking more load than others. Option B: Identical voltage characteristics Identical voltage characteristics are desirable for parallel operation, but in practice, it's challenging to achieve exact identical voltage, making this option less feasible. Option C: Drooping voltage characteristics Drooping voltage characteristics are ideal for parallel operation as they ensure that as the load increases, the voltage decreases slightly, allowing for automatic load sharing between generators. Option D: Linear voltage characteristics Linear voltage characteristics do not provide the necessary voltage drop needed for stable load sharing in parallel operation. Conclusion: Shunt generators with drooping voltage characteristics are most suited for stable parallel operation because they facilitate effective load sharing between generators."
