    question: "An assessment administered continuously during instruction to monitor student learning and provide ongoing pedagogical feedback is known as:"
    options:
      - Summative assessment
      - Formative assessment
      - Norm-referenced assessment
      - Placement assessment
    answer: 1
    explanation: Formative assessment occurs during the learning process (assessment for learning) to monitor student progress, identify learning gaps, and adapt pedagogical techniques. Summative assessment evaluates cumulative achievement at the end of an instructional unit (assessment of learning).
    keyConcept: Formative vs Summative Assessment

    question: "In Lev Vygotsky's sociocultural theory, the distance between what a learner can do independently and what they can achieve with guided support is termed:"
    options:
      - Schema equilibration

    answer: 1
    explanation: Vygotsky defined the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) as the range of tasks that a learner cannot yet perform alone but can accomplish with the scaffolding, guidance, and collaboration of a More Knowledgeable Other (MKO) or teacher.

    question: Which teaching method is based primarily on John Dewey's educational philosophy of 'learning by doing'?
    options:
      - Traditional Lecture Method
      - Project Method (Killpatrick)

      - Grammar-Translation Method

  - id: 8
    question: "An exam where an individual's performance is interpreted relative to a predefined standard or mastery criterion rather than relative to other test-takers is:"
    options:
      - Norm-referenced test
      - Criterion-referenced test
      - Aptitude percentile ranking
      - Speed test
    answer: 1
    explanation: A criterion-referenced test measures student performance against fixed learning objectives or performance standards (e.g., scoring 80% on clinical dosage calculation). A norm-referenced test compares a student's score against a peer group (e.g., 90th percentile).
    keyConcept: Criterion-Referenced vs Norm-Referenced Testing
    testedIn:
      - FPSC Lecturer 2023
      - KPPSC Lecturer 2022

  - id: 9
    question: "In B.F. Skinner's operant conditioning, when a teacher removes an unpleasant stimulus (e.g., canceling a scheduled detention) to strengthen a desirable student behavior, this is known as:"
    options:
      - Positive Punishment
      - Negative Reinforcement
      - Extinction
      - Negative Punishment
    answer: 1
    explanation: Negative reinforcement involves the removal, reduction, or avoidance of an aversive stimulus following a desired behavior, which increases or strengthens the likelihood that the desirable behavior will recur in the future.
    keyConcept: "Operant Conditioning: Negative Reinforcement vs Punishment"
    examTip: Reinforcement ALWAYS increases behavior; punishment ALWAYS decreases behavior. Positive = adding something; Negative = taking away something.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC Lecturer 2022
      - PPSC Lecturer Education 2024
