    question: The famous Sikh leader Banda Bahadur was captured and executed during the reign of the Mughal emperor?
    options:
      - Aurangzeb
      - Bahadur Shah I
      - Jahandar Shah
      - Farrukhsiyar
    answer: 3
    explanation: Baba Banda Singh Bahadur was a Sikh leader who, by early 1700, had captured parts of the Punjab region. Mughal emperor Bahadur Shah I failed to suppress Bahadur's uprising. In 1714, the Sirhind faujdar (garrison commander) Zainuddin Ahmad Khan attacked the Sikhs near Ropar. In 1715, Farrukhisyar sent 20,000 troops under Qamaruddin Khan, Abdus Samad Khan and Zakariya Khan Bahadur to defeat Bahadur. After an eight-month siege at Gurdaspur, Bahadur surrendered after he ran out of ammunition. Bahadur and his 200 companions were arrested and brought to Delhi; he was paraded around the city of Sirhind. Bahadur was put into an iron cage and the remaining Sikhs were chained.

    question: The Sikh gurus who compiled the Guru Granth Sahib was
    options:
      - Nanak
      - Har Rai
      - Arjan
      - Bahadur
      - Angad
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The Sikh Guru who compiled the Guru Granth Sahib , the holy scripture of Sikhism, was Guru Arjan . Guru Arjan Dev Ji was the fifth Sikh Guru and he is known for compiling and finalizing the writings of the earlier Sikh Gurus, along with adding his own compositions, hymns, and those of other saints, into the sacred scripture. Nanak (Option A) is the first Sikh Guru and the founder of Sikhism. He laid the foundation of the faith and his teachings are an integral part of the Guru Granth Sahib. Har Rai (Option B) was the seventh Sikh Guru, known for his compassion and efforts in propagating Sikhism. Bahadur (Option D) refers to Guru Tegh Bahadur, the ninth Sikh Guru, who sacrificed his life for the protection of religious freedom. Angad (Option E) was the second Sikh Guru, succeeding Guru Nanak, and he contributed to the growth of the Sikh community. Therefore, the correct option is Option C: Arjan , who compiled the Guru Granth Sahib, the central scripture of Sikhism."
