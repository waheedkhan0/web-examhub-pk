    question: "'Ayurveda' has its origin in"
    options:
      - Rig Veda
      - Sama Veda
      - Yajur Veda
      - Atharva Veda
    answer: 3
    explanation: "'Ayurveda,' the ancient Indian system of medicine and healing, has its origins in the Atharva Veda . The Atharva Veda is one of the four Vedas in Hindu scriptures and contains hymns, prayers, and verses related to various aspects of life, including health and well-being. Ayurveda is a holistic system of medicine that focuses on promoting a balanced and harmonious life by emphasizing the interconnectedness of the body, mind, and spirit. It provides guidelines for maintaining health, preventing illnesses, and treating various ailments using natural remedies, herbs, diet, lifestyle practices, and therapies. While the other Vedas (Rig Veda, Sama Veda, and Yajur Veda) also contain valuable knowledge related to various aspects of life, Ayurveda specifically finds its foundation in the Atharva Veda. Therefore, the correct answer is Option D: Atharva Veda , as it is the Veda from which the principles and knowledge of Ayurveda originated."

    question: Two popular Assemblies of the Vedic period were
    options:
      - Sabha and Mahasabha
      - Mahasabha and Ganasabha
      - Sabha and Samiti
      - Ur and Kula
    answer: 2
    explanation: The term Sabha denotes both the assembly (in early Rig-Vedic) and the assembly hall (Later Rig-Vedic). Women who were called Sabhavati also attended this assembly. It was basically a kin-based assembly and the practice of women attending it was stopped in later-Vedic times. The references to samiti come from the latest books of the Rig-Veda showing that it assumed importance only towards the end of the Rig-Vedic period. Samiti was a folk assembly in which people of the tribe gathered for transacting tribal business. It discussed philosophical issues and was concerned with religious ceremonies and prayers. References suggest that the Rajan was elected and re-elected by the Samiti.

    answer: 2
    explanation: The Rigveda contains accounts of conflicts between the Aryas and the Dasas and Dasyus. It describes Dasas and Dasyus as people who do not perform sacrifices (akratu) or obey the commandments of gods (avrata). Their speech is described as mridhra which could variously mean soft, uncouth, hostile, scornful or abusive.
