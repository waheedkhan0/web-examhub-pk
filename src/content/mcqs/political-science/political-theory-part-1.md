    question: Who among the following has give 'Sleeping Dogs' theory of democratic culture?
    options:
      - Lucian Pye and Robert E. Ward
      - James S. Coleman and Myron Weiner
      - James C. Charlesworth and F. W. Riggs
      - Almond and Verba
    answer: 3
    explanation: "The ' Sleeping Dogs ' theory, in the context of democratic political culture, refers to the idea that citizens in a democracy don't need to be constantly and actively involved in politics. It suggests that a healthy democracy can function even with a relatively passive citizenry, as long as people are willing to become engaged when important issues arise or when their rights are threatened. They are like ' sleeping dogs ' - quiet unless disturbed. Almond and Verba , in their seminal work \"The Civic Culture,\" introduced and discussed this concept extensively. The other options are incorrect because: * Lucian Pye and Robert E. Ward are known for their work on political development and modernization, particularly in Asian countries, but not specifically for the ' Sleeping Dogs ' theory. * James S. Coleman and Myron Weiner were prominent scholars in comparative politics and development, but their work isn't directly linked to this specific theory. * James C. Charlesworth and F. W. Riggs contributed to the fields of public administration and comparative administration, respectively, not to the development of the ' Sleeping Dogs ' theory."

    question: Which one of the following is not included in the well known sayings of Mao?
    options:
      - Weapons are an important factor in war, but no the decisive one
      - Politics is war without bloodshed
      - The party commands the gun and the gun must never be allowed to command the party
      - Let a hundred flowers blossom and a hundred schools of thought contend
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Weapons are an important factor in war, but not the decisive one → This is a well-known saying of Mao, emphasizing that people and ideology are more important than weapons. The party commands the gun and the gun must never be allowed to command the party → This is a famous Maoist principle highlighting the supremacy of the Communist Party over the military. Let a hundred flowers blossom and a hundred schools of thought contend → This was Mao’s slogan encouraging freedom of expression and diversity of ideas (though later suppressed). Politics is war without bloodshed → This is not Mao’s saying. It is generally attributed to Mao’s reinterpretation of Clausewitz’s idea, but the original wording is not part of Mao’s well-known quotations. Conclusion: The statement not included in Mao’s well-known sayings is Option B ."
