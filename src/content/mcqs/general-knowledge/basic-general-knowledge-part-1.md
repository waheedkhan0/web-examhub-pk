    options:
      - "1958"
      - "1922"
      - "1967"
      - "1968"
    answer: 2
    explanation: The first human heart transplant operation was conducted by Dr. Christiaan Barnard on December 3, 1967 , in Cape Town, South Africa. The patient was Louis Washkansky , a 54-year-old grocer who suffered from chronic heart disease. Dr. Barnard and his team performed the surgery at Groote Schuur Hospital , using a heart from a young woman who had died in a car accident. The surgery took several hours, and at one point, Washkansky's heart stopped beating, but it was restarted with a defibrillator. After the surgery, Washkansky was given drugs to suppress his immune system, in order to prevent his body from rejecting the new heart. He initially appeared to be recovering well, but several days later, he developed pneumonia and died on December 21, 1967, just 18 days after the surgery. Although the first heart transplant was not a complete success, it marked a major milestone in the development of cardiac surgery and organ transplantation. Dr. Barnard and his team went on to perform many more heart transplants, and the procedure became increasingly successful and widespread in the following decades.

      - states that no one was to be imprisoned without a writ or warrant stating the charge against him

    answer: 3
    explanation: Habeas Corpus Act 1679 provided facilities to a prisoner to obtain either speedy trial or release in bail, states that no one was to be imprisoned without a writ or warrant stating the charge against him and safeguarded the personal liberties of the people against arbitrary imprisonment by the king's orders. The Habeas Corpus Act 1679 was a landmark English law that safeguarded the personal liberties of the people against arbitrary imprisonment by the king's orders. The act provided several key protections for those who were imprisoned, including the right to obtain a writ of habeas corpus, which allowed them to challenge the legality of their detention in court. The act stated that no one was to be imprisoned without a writ or warrant stating the charge against him, and it also provided facilities to a prisoner to obtain either speedy trial or release in bail. This meant that individuals could not be held indefinitely without trial, and that they had a legal right to challenge their imprisonment and seek release. The Habeas Corpus Act was a significant milestone in the development of individual rights and protections in English law. It helped to establish the principle that no one could be detained without a legal basis for doing so, and that individuals had the right to challenge their detention in court. The act also had a lasting influence on legal systems around the world, including in the United States, where similar protections were enshrined in the Constitution.

    question: First China War was fought between
    options:
      - China and Egypt
      - China and Greek
      - China and Britain
      - China and France
    answer: 2
    explanation: The First China War, also known as the First Opium War, was fought between China and Great Britain. The First Opium War, also known as the Anglo-Chinese War, was a conflict between China and Great Britain that took place from 1839 to 1842. The war arose due to the Chinese government's attempts to suppress the opium trade, which was dominated by British merchants who were illegally importing opium into China from their colonies in India. In response to China's crackdown on the opium trade, the British government sent a military expedition to force China to open its ports to foreign trade and to legalize the opium trade. The British were also supported by other Western powers including France and the United States. The conflict involved a series of naval and land battles, with the British and their allies ultimately emerging victorious. In 1842, the Treaty of Nanking was signed, which opened up five Chinese ports to foreign trade and granted extraterritoriality to Western powers in China. The treaty also forced China to pay a large indemnity to Britain, cede Hong Kong to British control, and recognize the British as having "most favored nation" status. The First Opium War had a significant impact on China, leading to increased foreign influence and weakening of the Qing dynasty's authority. It also set the stage for future conflicts and tensions between China and Western powers, as well as the rise of anti-foreign sentiment in China.
