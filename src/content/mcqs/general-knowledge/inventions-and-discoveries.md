    question: Who introduced movable-type printing to Europe, producing the famous 42-line Bible at Mainz in the 1450s?
    options:
      - Johannes Gutenberg
      - William Caxton
      - Aldus Manutius
      - Anton Koberger
    answer: 0
    explanation: Gutenberg combined metal movable type, oil-based ink and a screw press at Mainz around 1440 to 1450, printing the 42-line Bible. Caxton, the tempting distractor, later brought the press to England in 1476, and Aldus Manutius pioneered italic type at Venice.
    keyConcept: "Gutenberg's press (Mainz, c. 1440-1450): movable metal type, oil-based ink and mechanised printing"
    examTip: Europe's first movable type is Gutenberg; the first press in England belongs to Caxton in 1476.

    question: Whose 1769 patent for a separate condenser made the steam engine efficient enough to drive factories and mines?
    options:
      - Thomas Newcomen
      - James Watt
      - Richard Trevithick
      - Thomas Savery
    answer: 1
    explanation: Watt's separate condenser, patented in 1769, avoided the wasteful repeated heating and cooling of the cylinder that limited Newcomen's atmospheric engine of 1712. Trevithick and Stephenson came later with high-pressure steam locomotives, and Savery's steam pump of 1698 preceded them all.
    keyConcept: "Steam timeline: Savery 1698 pump, Newcomen 1712 atmospheric engine, Watt 1769 separate condenser, railway steam 1804 onwards"
    examTip: "Examiners ask for the improvement, not the first engine: Watt for the separate condenser, Newcomen for the first practical mine engine."
    testedIn:
      - CSS

      - PMS

    question: Who received the first United States patent for the telephone, an application filed on 14 February 1876?
    options:
      - Antonio Meucci
      - Thomas Edison
      - Alexander Graham Bell
      - Elisha Gray
    answer: 2
    explanation: Bell's application, filed on 14 February 1876, was granted that March as the first US patent for the telephone; Elisha Gray filed a caveat for a similar apparatus on the same day. Antonio Meucci's earlier voice-communicating device is why the question of who 'really' invented the telephone stays a favourite trap.
    keyConcept: "The 1876 patent race: Bell's application versus Gray's caveat of the same day, with Meucci's claimed priority"
    examTip: For the telephone patent the answer is Bell; Gray's caveat and Meucci's apparatus appear as distractors, not as the grant holder.

    question: Which physicist discovered X-rays in 1895 and received the first-ever Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901?
    options:
      - Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen
      - Henri Becquerel
      - Marie Curie
      - Max Planck
    answer: 0
    explanation: Röntgen detected the unknown radiation he labelled 'X' while experimenting with cathode rays in 1895 and was awarded the inaugural Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901. Becquerel discovered spontaneous radioactivity in 1896 and shared the 1903 Physics Nobel with Pierre and Marie Curie.
    keyConcept: "Radiation discoveries: Röntgen X-rays 1895, Becquerel radioactivity 1896, Curies radium and polonium 1898"
    examTip: The first Nobel Prize in Physics went to Röntgen in 1901, not to Curie; the 1903 prize went to Curie, Curie and Becquerel.

    question: Who carried out the 1796 experiment that gave immunology its first practical vaccine?
    options:
      - Louis Pasteur
      - Joseph Lister
      - Paul Ehrlich
      - Edward Jenner
    answer: 3
    explanation: Edward Jenner used material from a cowpox lesion in 1796, publishing his findings in 1798 and coining 'vaccine' from vacca, the Latin for cow, opening the road to smallpox eradication. Pasteur developed attenuated vaccines for chicken cholera, anthrax and rabies later in the nineteenth century.
    keyConcept: Jenner's cowpox vaccination (1796, published 1798) versus Pasteur's later attenuated vaccines for chicken cholera, anthrax and rabies
    examTip: Smallpox and cowpox point to Jenner; antisepsis is Lister; serum therapy is Ehrlich; rabies and anthrax vaccines are Pasteur.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC

      - NTS

    question: At St Mary's Hospital in 1928, who discovered penicillin from a mould-contaminated staphylococcus culture?
    options:
      - Robert Koch
      - Alexander Fleming
      - Louis Pasteur
      - Joseph Lister
    answer: 1
    explanation: Alexander Fleming observed in 1928 that a Penicillium mould was inhibiting bacterial growth and named the active substance penicillin. Howard Florey and Ernst Chain developed it into a usable drug, and the three shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
    keyConcept: "Antibiotic era: Fleming's 1928 discovery, Florey and Chain's clinical development, 1945 Medicine Nobel"
    examTip: If the question asks who made penicillin a practical medicine, the answer is Florey and Chain, not Fleming alone.

    question: Which inventor developed the first commercially practical incandescent lamp and opened New York's Pearl Street power station in 1882?
    options:
      - Nikola Tesla
      - Michael Faraday
      - Thomas Alva Edison
      - Guglielmo Marconi
    answer: 2
    explanation: Edison's long-lived high-resistance filament lamp at Menlo Park in 1879, together with his complete generation and distribution system, made electric lighting commercially viable, and Pearl Street began supplying power in 1882. Joseph Swan demonstrated a similar lamp in Britain, and Faraday's electromagnetic induction supplied the scientific basis for the dynamos.
    keyConcept: "Commercial electrification: Edison's lamp (1879-1880 patents) plus the Pearl Street station (1882)"
    examTip: Ask for the British pioneer and the answer is Joseph Swan; ask for commercial practicality and a full power system and it is Edison.

      - PPSC
      - NTS
      - FPSC

    question: The 1962 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the molecular structure of nucleic acids was shared by James Watson, Francis Crick and a third laureate. Who was he?
    options:
      - Linus Pauling
      - Maurice Wilkins
      - Erwin Chargaff
      - Rosalind Franklin
    answer: 1
    explanation: Maurice Wilkins, who applied X-ray diffraction to DNA fibres at King's College London, shared the 1962 prize with Watson and Crick. Rosalind Franklin's diffraction data, including the famous Photo 51, was decisive to the double-helix model but she died in 1958, and Nobel Prizes are not awarded posthumously.
    keyConcept: "DNA double helix (1953): Watson and Crick's model, Franklin and Wilkins's X-ray diffraction evidence, 1962 Medicine Nobel"
    examTip: The three 1962 laureates are Watson, Crick and Wilkins; Franklin is the correct answer only when the question asks whose X-ray data made the model possible.

    question: Which chemist published the first widely accepted periodic table in 1869, leaving gaps for undiscovered elements and predicting their properties?
    options:
      - John Dalton
      - Lothar Meyer
      - Dmitri Mendeleev
      - Antoine Lavoisier
    answer: 2
    explanation: Mendeleev arranged the elements by atomic weight and recurring properties in 1869 and predicted the 'eka' elements later found as gallium, scandium and germanium. Lothar Meyer produced a similar classification independently, but the predictive gaps are the reason the answer key credits Mendeleev.
    keyConcept: "Periodic law: Mendeleev's 1869 table and eka-element predictions; Meyer's parallel arrangement"
    examTip: Meyer is the sophisticated distractor; the phrase 'left gaps and predicted properties' always points to Mendeleev.

    question: Which English mathematician designed the Analytical Engine in the 1830s, the first blueprint for a general-purpose mechanical computer?
    options:
      - Charles Babbage
      - Michael Faraday
      - George Boole
      - James Clerk Maxwell
    answer: 0
    explanation: Babbage's Analytical Engine paired a 'mill' for processing with a 'store' for numbers and punched-card input, the design of a programmable general-purpose machine. Ada Lovelace's 1843 notes on the engine contain what is widely described as the first published programme.
    keyConcept: "Mechanical computing: Babbage's Difference Engine (1820s) and Analytical Engine (1830s), Lovelace's programming notes"
    examTip: Difference Engine is a calculating machine; the programmable general-purpose design is the Analytical Engine, and both are Babbage's.

    question: Which was the first human-made object to orbit the Earth, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957?
    options:
      - Explorer 1
      - Vostok 1
      - Luna 1
      - Sputnik 1
    answer: 3
    explanation: Sputnik 1, a polished sphere of about 58 cm diameter, entered orbit on 4 October 1957 and opened the space age. Explorer 1 was the first United States satellite in January 1958, Luna 1 the first probe to escape Earth's gravity in 1959, and Vostok 1 carried Yuri Gagarin as the first human in orbit in 1961.
    keyConcept: "Space race milestones: Sputnik 1 in 1957, Explorer 1 in 1958, Gagarin in 1961, Apollo 11 in 1969"
    examTip: The order asked in most papers is 1957 first satellite, 1961 first human in orbit, 1969 first crewed Moon landing.

    question: Who was the first person to be awarded two Nobel Prizes?
    options:
      - Marie Curie
      - Linus Pauling
      - John Bardeen
      - Frederick Sanger
    answer: 0
    explanation: Marie Curie took the 1903 Physics Nobel, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel, and then the 1911 Chemistry Nobel for her work on radium and polonium. Pauling (Chemistry 1954, Peace 1962), Sanger (Medicine 1958, Chemistry 1980) and Bardeen (Physics 1956 and 1972) all collected second prizes after her, so she was the first.
    keyConcept: "Double Nobel laureates: Curie first, then Pauling, Sanger and Bardeen"
    examTip: "All four options are double laureates, so answer with the one who did it first: Marie Curie."

    question: Abdus Salam shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physics with Sheldon Glashow and Steven Weinberg for work on which theory?
    options:
      - Quantum electrodynamics
      - The Pauli exclusion principle
      - The electroweak unification of electromagnetic and weak nuclear interactions
      - The theory of superconductivity
    answer: 2
    explanation: Salam's gauge-theory formulation completed the unified electroweak model of electromagnetic and weak interactions, recognised by the 1979 Physics Prize he shared with Glashow and Weinberg. He was the first Pakistani to win a Nobel Prize; quantum electrodynamics (1965) and the theory of superconductivity (1972) were recognised in separate prizes.
    keyConcept: Electroweak theory (Glashow, Weinberg, Salam); Abdus Salam, 1979 Physics, first Pakistani Nobel laureate
    examTip: Allama Iqbal was never a Nobel laureate; among Pakistani-linked names the verified laureates are Abdus Salam in 1979 and Malala Yousafzai in 2014.
