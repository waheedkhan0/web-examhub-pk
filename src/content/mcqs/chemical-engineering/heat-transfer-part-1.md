This phenomenon occurs because the solutes lower the vapor pressure of the solution, requiring a higher temperature for boiling to occur. As a result: Reduce the economy : The boiling point elevation increases the energy required to evaporate the solvent in each effect of the evaporator system. This increase in energy consumption reduces the overall efficiency or economy of the evaporator system. Reduce the capacity : Boiling point elevation does not directly reduce the capacity of the evaporator, as it refers to the efficiency rather than the maximum processing capacity. Increase the economy : This is incorrect because boiling point elevation actually decreases the efficiency or economy of the evaporator system. None of these : This option is incorrect because boiling point elevation does have a specific effect on the evaporator system. Therefore, the correct answer is Option B: Reduce the economy."

    question: Radiator of an automobile engine is a __________ type of heat exchanger.
    options:
      - Co-current
      - Cross-current
      - Counter-current
      - Direct contact
    answer: 2
    explanation: "A: Co-current: In a co-current heat exchanger, both fluids move in the same direction. While this can be efficient in some cases, it typically results in a lower overall temperature difference between the fluids, reducing the effectiveness of heat transfer as they approach similar temperatures. This is not how automotive radiators are designed to operate. B: Cross-current: In a cross-current heat exchanger, the fluids flow perpendicular to each other. While this design can be used in some applications, it is not as efficient as counter-current systems for maximizing heat exchange. Automotive radiators do not employ this design. C: Counter-current: This is the correct answer. In a counter-current heat exchanger, one fluid flows in one direction while the other flows in the opposite direction. In a radiator, the hot coolant from the engine flows through the radiator while the cooler air flows in the opposite direction. This arrangement maintains a high temperature gradient, allowing for more effective heat transfer and cooling of the coolant. D: Direct contact: In direct contact heat exchangers, the two fluids mix directly, allowing for heat transfer through physical contact. While effective in some systems, it is not applicable for radiators where the coolant and air should remain separate to prevent contamination and maintain fluid integrity. Conclusion: Based on the explanation, the radiator of an automobile engine operates as a C: Counter-current type of heat exchanger, which is the most efficient design for transferring heat in this application."

    question: __________ chart is known as transient heat conduction chart.
    options:
      - Dirhing's
      - Heisler's
      - Mollier's
      - Cox
    answer: 1
    explanation: A Heisler's chart is used in the field of heat transfer to solve problems related to transient heat conduction. Transient heat conduction refers to the transfer of heat within a material over time, where the temperature distribution changes with time. Heisler's charts provide graphical solutions for the temperature distribution and heat transfer rate in various geometries (such as slabs, cylinders, and spheres) subjected to convective boundary conditions. These charts are particularly useful because they allow engineers to quickly estimate the thermal response of a system without needing complex calculations. The other options, such as Dirhing's , Mollier's , and Cox charts, are associated with different fields and are not used for solving transient heat conduction problems.

    options:
      - 1 to 100
      - 0.5 to 50
      - 10 to 100
      - 100 to 1000
    answer: 1
    explanation: "strong>Electromagnetic spectrum for thermal radiation extends over a part of the full EM spectrum where bodies at ordinary temperatures (like furnaces, heaters, process equipment) emit significant energy. In heat transfer, thermal radiation is generally taken to be important over wavelengths from about 1 to 100 microns (μm) . This range covers: Shorter infrared waves near the visible region (around a few microns), where hot bodies at high temperature radiate strongly. Longer infrared waves (tens of microns), important for bodies at moderate temperatures that are common in chemical and process industries. Option A: 1 to 100 μm therefore represents the conventional engineering range used for radiation heat transfer calculations. Why others are wrong: Option B (0.5 to 50 μm) is too narrow; it misses part of the longer wavelength range where cooler surfaces still emit appreciable energy. Option C (10 to 100 μm) ignores the shorter wavelengths (1–10 μm), where very hot surfaces have their peak emission. Option D (100 to 1000 μm) lies mainly in the far infrared/microwave region and does not represent the main band of thermal radiation used in heat transfer. So, the important radiation range in heat transfer is 1 to 100 microns , making Option A correct."
