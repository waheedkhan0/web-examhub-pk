    question: Radiation thermometer can not measure the temperature
    options:
      - Inside a pressure vessel
      - Of an object without coming in contact with it
      - Of liquid oxygen
      - Of moving objects at high temperature
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The correct answer is A: Inside a pressure vessel . Here's why: A radiation thermometer works by detecting the infrared radiation emitted by an object. This radiation travels through the air (or a vacuum) to the thermometer. Option A: Inside a pressure vessel , the radiation thermometer cannot directly \"see\" the object whose temperature it needs to measure. The vessel walls would block the radiation. Think of it like trying to see something behind a solid wall – you can't! Option B: Of an object without coming in contact with it is a key advantage of radiation thermometers; they measure temperature remotely. Option C: Of liquid oxygen - Radiation thermometers *can* measure the temperature of liquid oxygen, even though it's very cold. Everything emits some radiation. Option D: Of moving objects at high temperature - Radiation thermometers are well-suited for measuring the temperature of hot, moving objects because they don't require physical contact."

    question: Thermal wells used in temperature measurements should have
    options:
      - Very thick walls
      - Low emissivity
      - Polished surface
      - High transmissivity of radiation
    answer: 2
    explanation: Thermowells protect temperature sensors from harsh process conditions, but their design also affects measurement accuracy. A polished surface reduces the emissivity of the thermowell, which minimizes radiative heat losses to the surroundings and ensures the sensor measures the true process temperature. Very thick walls provide mechanical strength to withstand high pressures and flows, but they cause increased thermal lag, slowing down the sensor’s response to temperature changes, which can be problematic for process control applications. Low emissivity is effectively achieved through a polished surface, which is why Option C covers this aspect practically. High transmissivity of radiation is not relevant since thermowells measure temperature through conduction, not radiation transmission. For accurate and fast temperature measurement in process control, a polished surface is essential, making Option C the correct choice in this context.

    question: Out of the following temperature measuring instruments, the measurement accuracy will be minimum for the
    options:
      - Mercury in glass thermometer
      - Optical pyrometer
      - Iron-constantan thermocouple
      - Alcohol filled thermometer
    answer: 1
    explanation: Optical pyrometers measure temperature based on the visible radiation emitted by hot objects and are typically used for very high temperatures where contact methods are impractical. However, they have minimum measurement accuracy among the options because they depend on the emissivity of the object's surface, which can vary widely with material, surface finish, and oxidation, introducing significant errors. Mercury in glass thermometers (Option A) and alcohol filled thermometers (Option D) offer good accuracy for lower temperature ranges with direct contact measurement. Iron-constantan thermocouples (Option C) provide reliable and accurate measurements over a wide range of temperatures with reasonable precision. Therefore, due to their reliance on visual estimation and sensitivity to surface emissivity, optical pyrometers have the lowest measurement accuracy among the instruments listed.

    question: What is the Laplace transform of impulse input having magnitude 'X'?
    options:
      - X
      - X2
      - $$\frac{{\text{1}}}{{\text{X}}}$$
      - "1"
    answer: 0
    explanation: "The correct answer is A: X . Here's why: An impulse function (also called a Dirac delta function) is a signal that is infinitely short and has an area of 1. When the impulse has a magnitude of 'X', it means its area is 'X' instead of 1. The Laplace transform is a mathematical tool used to convert time-domain functions (like signals) into the frequency domain. A key property of the Laplace transform is that the Laplace transform of the unit impulse function (magnitude 1) is 1 . Since our impulse has a magnitude of 'X', we can think of it as 'X' times the unit impulse function. The Laplace transform is a linear operator , meaning the Laplace transform of X * f(t) is X * F(s), where F(s) is the Laplace transform of f(t). Therefore, the Laplace transform of an impulse with magnitude 'X' is X * 1 = X ."

    question: The function of a transducer is to
    options:
      - Modify the input signal
      - Amplify the input signal
      - Convert the primary signal into a more useful quantity, usually an electric impulse
      - Codify/decodify the input signal
    answer: 2
    explanation: A transducer is a device used to convert one form of energy or signal into another form. In the context of process control and instrumentation, the primary signal could be anything from pressure, temperature, flow rate, etc. The primary signal is typically in a non-electric form. The function of a transducer is to convert this primary signal into a more useful quantity, usually an electric impulse. For example, a pressure transducer converts the pressure signal into an electric voltage signal, which can then be easily manipulated, processed, and transmitted to control systems or displays. Similarly, temperature transducers convert temperature signals into electric signals. Therefore, the function of a transducer is to convert the primary signal into a more useful quantity, usually an electric impulse .

    question: Which of the following is a desirable characteristic of an instrument?
    options:
      - High drift
      - High fidelity
      - High measuring lag
      - Poor reproducibility
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Instruments used for process control and measurement are expected to provide accurate, reliable, and consistent readings of process variables such as pressure, temperature, flow, and level. A desirable characteristic of a good instrument is high fidelity , which means the instrument is capable of accurately reproducing the input signal without distortion or delay . High fidelity ensures that the output of the instrument truly represents the measured variable and responds correctly to rapid changes in the process conditions. On the other hand: High drift (Option A) is undesirable because it causes the instrument reading to change with time even when the measured variable remains constant. High measuring lag (Option C) is also undesirable because it delays the instrument’s response to changes in the input signal, reducing accuracy in dynamic systems. Poor reproducibility (Option D) means the instrument cannot produce the same reading under identical conditions, which affects reliability and consistency. Therefore, the most desirable characteristic of an instrument is high fidelity . Hence, the correct answer is Option B: High fidelity ."

    question: Which of the following thermocouples is capable of measuring a temperature of -50°C?
    options:
      - Platinum - platinum + rhodium
      - Chromel - alumel
      - Iron-constantan
      - Copper - constantan
    answer: 3
    explanation: "1. Temperature Range of Each Thermocouple: • Platinum–Platinum Rhodium (Type R or S) → Typically used for high temperatures, around 0°C to 1600°C. These thermocouples are designed for furnaces, metallurgy, and very high-temperature environments. Below 0°C, they become extremely insensitive and inaccurate. • Chromel–Alumel (Type K) → Has a wide range of about −200°C to 1250°C, but its accuracy at low temperatures is poor. It becomes unstable and exhibits non-linearity below −50°C, so it is not preferred for precise low-temperature measurement. • Iron–Constantan (Type J) → Range is around −40°C to 750°C. It cannot reliably measure below −40°C because iron begins to undergo changes (oxidation and magnetic effects). Measuring −50°C is already outside its optimal working range. • Copper–Constantan (Type T) → Range is −200°C to +350°C. This thermocouple is specifically known for its excellent accuracy and stability at low temperatures. This makes it ideal for refrigeration, cryogenic applications, and any measurement involving temperatures below 0°C. 2. Why Copper–Constantan is Best for −50°C: Copper–constantan provides: • High sensitivity at low temperatures • Very stable EMF output • Minimal drift in the sub-zero range • Accurate readings in the −50°C region This is why industries such as food processing, environmental monitoring, and cryogenics commonly use Type T thermocouples for temperatures like −50°C. 3. Final Conclusion: Only Copper–constantan (Type T) is specifically designed to measure −50°C accurately and reliably. ✔ Correct Answer: Option D – Copper–constantan"
