    question: In a ghazal, the closing couplet that conventionally carries the poet's pen-name (takhallus) is called
    options:
      - Maqta
      - Matla
      - Sher
      - Misra
    answer: 0
    explanation: The maqta is the final couplet in which the poet signs the poem with the takhallus. The matla is the opening couplet, a sher is any couplet, and a misra is a single line (hemistich).
    keyConcept: Ghazal anatomy — matla, maqta, sher, misra, takhallus
    examTip: Maqta and matla are the pair examiners swap inside a question; read whether the stem says 'opening' or 'closing'.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
  - id: 2
    question: In ghazal prosody, the term 'radif' refers to
    options:
      - The rhyming word that comes immediately before the repeat
      - The first couplet that sets the metre and rhyme
      - The word or phrase repeated identically at the end of every couplet
      - The metrical pattern of the two hemistichs
    answer: 2
    explanation: The radif is the monotonous repeat following the rhyme (qafiya) in both hemistichs of every sher. Option 1 defines the qafiya itself, which is the classic confusion tested here.
    keyConcept: Qafiya (rhyme) and radif (refrain) in the ghazal
    examTip: "Order to remember: qafiya is the rhyming sound, radif is the identical word after it, matla establishes both."
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 3
    question: How does a nazm differ from a ghazal in Urdu poetry?
    options:
      - A nazm must be written in a classical metre while a ghazal may be free verse
      - A nazm is always devotional while a ghazal is always romantic
      - A nazm is longer than two hundred couplets, a ghazal never is
      - A nazm is built on one unified theme with a title and is not bound by the ghazal's matla, qafiya and radif scheme
    answer: 3
    explanation: The ghazal is a set of autonomously rhymed couplets without a single subject, whereas the nazm is a poem with a beginning, middle and end on one theme and usually carries a title. Length, metre and subject matter alone do not define the difference, so the other options fail.
    keyConcept: Forms and genres — ghazal versus nazm
    examTip: If the couplets can be shuffled without loss, the poem is a ghazal; a nazm collapses when reordered.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
  - id: 4
    question: Mir Babar Ali 'Anis' and Mir Ata Husain 'Dabeer' are celebrated chiefly for perfecting which long narrative elegy on the tragedy of Karbala?
    options:
      - Qasida
      - Marsiya
      - Masnavi
      - Rubai
    answer: 1
    explanation: Anis and Dabeer are the two great masters of the Urdu marsiya, the extended elegy on the martyrs of Karbala, in the Lucknow school. A nauha is a shorter lament, and the qasida is a panegyric — neither is the long narrative form associated with these two names.
    keyConcept: Genre and school — the marsiya of the Lucknow poets
    examTip: "Pair genre with city: marsiya with Lucknow (Anis, Dabeer) and the classical ghazal with Delhi (Mir, Dard, Sauda)."
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 5
    question: The complete collected works of Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib, taking in both his Urdu and Persian verse together with his prose, are published under the title
    options:
      - Kulliye-e-Ghalib
      - Divan-e-Ghalib
      - Zarb-e-Kaleem
      - Namakdan-e-Zohri
    answer: 0
    explanation: A kulliye gathers everything a poet wrote in all languages, so Kulliye-e-Ghalib covers the Urdu and Persian verse together with his prose. Divan-e-Ghalib, the tempting second option, is the Urdu ghazal collection alone, and Namakdan-e-Zohri is a single prose letter, not the collected works.
    keyConcept: Divan versus kulliye — the bibliographic terms of Urdu collections
    examTip: Divan = restricted lyric collection; kulliye = the complete works. Examiners set this pair against each other deliberately.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
  - id: 6
    question: Mir Taqi Mir, the eighteenth-century master of the Urdu ghazal whose collected verse is the Divan-e-Mir, belonged to the poetic school of which city?
    options:
      - Lucknow
      - Hyderabad Deccan
      - Delhi
      - Lahore
    answer: 2
    explanation: Mir worked in the Delhi school (Dilli ka andaaz), noted for its sober, difficult diction and pathos, before moving to Lucknow late in life; his fame nonetheless rests on the Delhi tradition. Lucknow is the trap because it became the great poetic capital after him and is the home of the marsiya school.
    keyConcept: Delhi versus Lucknow schools of classical Urdu poetry
    examTip: Delhi school = ghazal and soz (Mir, Dard, Sauda, Ghalib); Lucknow school = marsiya and masnavi (Anis, Dabeer).
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 7
    question: Allama Iqbal's first published collection of Urdu poetry, brought out in 1924, is
    options:
      - Zarb-e-Kaleem
      - Bang-e-Dra
      - Ball-e-Jibril
      - Armaghan-e-Hijaz
    answer: 1
    explanation: Bang-e-Dra (The Call of the Bell, 1924) was Iqbal's first Urdu poetry book and includes the Shikwa-Jawab-e-Shikwa material. Ball-e-Jibril (1935), Zarb-e-Kaleem (1936) and Armaghan-e-Hijaz (1938) all appeared later.
    keyConcept: Chronology of Iqbal's Urdu collections
    examTip: Remember the ascending order Bang-e-Dra, Ball-e-Jibril, Zarb-e-Kaleem, Armaghan-e-Hijaz; the question bank tests sequence, not just titles.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 8
    question: Which work of Allama Iqbal, first published in Persian in 1915, sets out his philosophy of 'khudi' (selfhood)?
    options:
      - Payam-e-Mashriq
      - Javid Nama
      - Shikwa and Jawab-e-Shikwa
      - Asrar-e-Khudi
    answer: 3
    explanation: Asrar-e-Khudi (Secrets of the Self), first published in Persian in 1915, is the foundational statement of Iqbal's khudi idea. Javid Nama (1932) and Payam-e-Mashriq (1923) are later Persian works, while Shikwa and Jawab-e-Shikwa (1913) are Urdu poems on a different subject.
    keyConcept: Iqbal's Persian philosophical verse and the concept of khudi
    examTip: "Pairs to hold: Asrar-e-Khudi (1915, khudi), Javid Nama (1932, mystical journey), Bang-e-Dra (1924, first Urdu book)."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS

    question: The poem that opens with the line 'bol ke lab azaad rah hain' was written by
    options:
      - Faiz Ahmed Faiz
      - Josh Malihabadi
      - Ahmed Faraz
      - Nasir Kazmi
    answer: 0
    explanation: "'Bol' is one of Faiz Ahmed Faiz's most anthologised songs of resistance, written in the prison-poetry tradition of his generation. Josh is the tempting distractor because he too wrote committed, militant verse, but this lyric is Faiz's."
    keyConcept: Faiz Ahmed Faiz and the poetry of resistance
    examTip: For line-to-poet matching, learn Faiz’s opening lines alongside the collections they come from; Naqsh-e-Faryadi (1941) is his first.

  - id: 10
    question: By which honorific is Josh Malihabadi, the poet of militant and politically committed Urdu verse, popularly known?
    options:
      - Shair-e-Mashriq
      - Khuda-e-Sukhan
      - Shair-e-Jang
      - Shair-e-Marsiya
    answer: 2
    explanation: Josh Malihabadi is styled Shair-e-Jang, the Poet of Struggle, for his combative, revolutionary ghazal and nazm. Shair-e-Mashriq belongs to Iqbal and Khuda-e-Sukhan to Mir, so both are familiar-name traps.
    keyConcept: Standard honorifics (alqaab) of modern Urdu poets
    examTip: "Build a one-line table of titles: Iqbal Shair-e-Mashriq, Mir Khuda-e-Sukhan, Josh Shair-e-Jang, and keep it in revision notes."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - FPSC
  - id: 11
    question: The Progressive Writers' Movement, which turned Urdu writing towards social realism and anti-fascist commitment, was launched at its first conference in London in
    options:
      - "1926"
      - "1946"
      - "1956"
      - "1936"
    answer: 3
    explanation: The movement was inaugurated at a founding conference of progressive Indian writers held in London in 1936, and its first Indian conference followed at Lucknow in 1938. The 1946 date is the trap, being close to the pre-partition Popular Front period rather than the founding.
    keyConcept: The Progressive Writers' Movement and its literary programme
    examTip: Anchor the chain 1936 (founding) - 1938 (Lucknow conference) - 1940s (afsana writers); questions usually ask only for the founding year.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PMS
      - PPSC
  - id: 12
    question: Under which pen-name did the writer who became famous as Munshi Premchand first publish his Urdu stories?
    options:
      - Dagh
      - Nawab Rai
      - Josh
      - Miraji
    answer: 1
    explanation: Premchand, born Dhanpat Rai, began writing in Urdu as 'Nawab Rai' and only later adopted the name Premchand. Dagh, Josh and Miraji are genuine takhalluses of other Urdu poets, which is why they appear as plausible distractors.
    keyConcept: Pen-names and bilingual (Urdu-Hindi) production of early modern Urdu fiction
    examTip: Premchand is a standard 'author and pen-name' item; his later Hindi masterpiece is Godan, which examiners pair with it.
    testedIn:
      - PMS
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
