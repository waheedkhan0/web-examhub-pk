    question: If 40% of a number is 168, what is the number?
    options:
      - "336"
      - "400"
      - "420"
      - "440"
    answer: 2
    explanation: 40% of 420 = 0.40 x 420 = 168, so 420 is the number. The tempting 336 is simply 168 x 2, which would be 80% of the answer, not 40% of it; 400 would give 160 at 40%.
    keyConcept: If p% of N = V, then N = V x 100 / p.
    examTip: "Divide by 40 and multiply by 100: 168 x 100 / 40 = 168 x 2.5 = 420. Or find 1% = 4.2 and shift the decimal two places."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 2
    question: A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost price and then allows a 10% discount on the marked price. What is his profit per cent?
    options:
      - 24%
      - 26%
      - 30%
      - 36%
    answer: 1
    explanation: Take cost = 100, so marked price = 140 and a 10% discount leaves selling price = 126, giving a profit of 26%. The 30% choice is the classic error of subtracting 10 from 40, but the discount is charged on the marked price, not on cost.
    keyConcept: SP = CP x (1 + markup) x (1 - discount); with CP = 100 the profit per cent is SP - 100.
    examTip: "Multiply the factors instead of adding percentages: 1.40 x 0.90 = 1.26, so 26%."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
  - id: 3
    question: Rs. 12,000 is invested at 10% per annum for 2 years. By how much does the compound interest (annually compounded) exceed the simple interest on the same sum?
    options:
      - "0"
      - "120"
      - 2,400
      - 2,520
    answer: 1
    explanation: Simple interest = 12,000 x 10 x 2 / 100 = 2,400. Compound amount = 12,000 x 1.1 x 1.1 = 14,520, so compound interest = 2,520. The difference is 2,520 - 2,400 = 120.
    keyConcept: For 2 years, CI - SI = P x (R/100)^2, because compounding charges interest on the first year's interest.
    examTip: "Apply the one-line shortcut: 12,000 x (10/100)^2 = 12,000 x 0.01 = 120, no full amount needed."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 4
    question: A batsman scored 34, 46, 28 and 52 runs in four innings. How many runs must he score in the fifth innings to average 42?
    options:
      - "44"
      - "46"
      - "50"
      - "54"
    answer: 2
    explanation: The total needed for five innings is 42 x 5 = 210. He already has 34 + 46 + 28 + 52 = 160, so he must score 210 - 160 = 50.
    keyConcept: Average = total / number of items, therefore total = average x number of items.
    examTip: Always convert the target average into a target total first (42 x 5 = 210), then subtract what is already on the board.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
  - id: 5
    question: "The length and breadth of a rectangular field are in the ratio 5 : 3 and its perimeter is 240 metres. What is its area?"
    options:
      - 2,700 sq m
      - 3,375 sq m
      - 3,600 sq m
      - 4,050 sq m
    answer: 1
    explanation: Perimeter = 2(5x + 3x) = 16x = 240, so x = 15, giving length 75 m and breadth 45 m. Area = 75 x 45 = 3,375 sq m. The 3,600 option comes from wrongly treating 240 as the area input or rounding the sides to 60 and 60.
    keyConcept: A ratio plus one linear measurement (perimeter) fixes the common multiplier; area is then the product of the two sides.
    examTip: Semi-perimeter 120 = 8 ratio parts, so one part = 15; multiply 75 x 45 as 75 x 45 = 75 x 40 + 75 x 5.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 6
    question: A train 150 metres long runs at 90 km/h. How much time does it take to cross a platform 250 metres long?
    options:
      - 6 seconds
      - 10 seconds
      - 16 seconds
      - 20 seconds
    answer: 2
    explanation: 90 km/h = 90 x 5/18 = 25 m/s. The distance to be covered is the train plus the platform = 150 + 250 = 400 m, so time = 400 / 25 = 16 seconds. The 6 s and 10 s choices divide only the train length or only the platform length by 25.
    keyConcept: "Crossing a platform or bridge: distance = length of train + length of structure; km/h to m/s means multiplying by 5/18."
    examTip: Convert the speed once by 5/18 (90 x 5/18 = 25), add the two lengths, then divide. Never drop the train's own length.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 7
    question: A can finish a piece of work in 10 days and B can finish it in 15 days. Working together, how many days will the work take?
    options:
      - 6 days
      - 12.5 days
      - 25 days
      - 30 days
    answer: 0
    explanation: "Their one-day work adds to 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6, so together they need 6 days. Averaging the two times (12.5 days) or adding them (25 days) are the standard traps: rates add, durations never do."
    keyConcept: Work rates are additive; if A takes m days and B takes n days, together they take mn / (m + n) days.
    examTip: Use mn/(m+n) = 150/25 = 6, or take 30 units of work (the LCM) and add daily outputs 3 + 2 = 5 units per day.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 8
    question: "Number series: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ... Which number comes next?"
    options:
      - "36"
      - "38"
      - "40"
      - "42"
    answer: 3
    explanation: "The gaps are 4, 6, 8, 10, rising by 2 each time, so the next gap is 12 and 30 + 12 = 42. The same terms are n(n+1): 1x2, 2x3, 3x4, 4x5, 5x6, then 6x7 = 42. Choosing 36 assumes the gap repeats at 6."
    keyConcept: "Difference tables: when the first differences form an arithmetic sequence, extend that sequence and add."
    examTip: Confirm the answer with two independent patterns (differences 4,6,8,10,12 and n(n+1)) before marking it.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 9
    question: What are the LCM and the HCF of 12, 18 and 30 respectively?
    options:
      - 60 and 3
      - 90 and 3
      - 120 and 6
      - 180 and 6
    answer: 3
    explanation: 12 = 2^2 x 3, 18 = 2 x 3^2 and 30 = 2 x 3 x 5. The LCM takes the greatest powers, 2^2 x 3^2 x 5 = 180, while the HCF takes the common primes at their lowest powers, 2 x 3 = 6. The 60 and 90 options cannot be the LCM at all because neither is divisible by 12.
    keyConcept: LCM = product of the highest powers of every prime present; HCF = product of the common primes at their lowest powers.
    examTip: "Eliminate fast: the LCM must divide evenly by all three numbers, and only 180 does."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
  - id: 10
    question: Which of the following fractions has the greatest value?
    options:
      - 3/7
      - 5/11
      - 7/13
      - 9/16
    answer: 3
    explanation: As decimals these are 3/7 = 0.4286, 5/11 = 0.4545, 7/13 = 0.5385 and 9/16 = 0.5625, so 9/16 is the largest. The tempting 7/13 is close behind at 0.538 but still smaller.
    keyConcept: "Comparing fractions: convert each to a decimal, or cross-multiply two candidates at a time."
    examTip: Divide numerator by denominator to two decimal places; 9/16 = 0.5625 is the only option above 0.55.
    testedIn:

  - id: 11
    question: What is the value of the square root of 0.16 plus the cube root of 0.729?
    options:
      - "0.5"
      - "1.1"
      - "1.3"
      - "1.9"
    answer: 2
    explanation: Square root of 0.16 is 0.4 because 0.4 x 0.4 = 0.16, and the cube root of 0.729 is 0.9 because 0.9 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 0.729. The sum is 0.4 + 0.9 = 1.3. The 1.1 option results from taking the cube root as 0.7.
    keyConcept: "Roots of decimals: take the root of the digits, then place the decimal point (one place per root degree for square roots, three for cube roots)."
    examTip: "Reduce to whole numbers first: 16 gives 4 and 729 gives 9, so 0.4 + 0.9 = 1.3."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - NTS

    question: Ten years ago a father was four times as old as his son. Ten years from now he will be twice as old as his son. What is the son's present age?
    options:
      - "20"
      - "25"
      - "30"
      - "40"
    answer: 0
    explanation: "Let the present ages be F and S. Then F - 10 = 4(S - 10) and F + 10 = 2(S + 10), which gives F = 2S + 10. Substituting: 2S + 10 - 10 = 4S - 40, so 2S = 40 and S = 20, making the father 50."
    keyConcept: Age problems reduce to two linear equations in two unknowns referred to one common present time.
    examTip: "Test the pair 50 and 20: ten years ago 40 was four times 10; ten years later 60 is twice 30. Checking is quicker than re-solving."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
  - id: 13
    question: A 40-litre mixture of milk and water contains 10% water. How many litres of water must be added to make the mixture 25% water?
    options:
      - "8"
      - "10"
      - "12"
      - "15"
    answer: 0
    explanation: "The mixture holds 4 litres of water and 36 litres of milk. Adding x litres of water requires (4 + x) / (40 + x) = 25/100 = 1/4, so 16 + 4x = 40 + x and 3x = 24, giving x = 8. Check: 48 litres containing 12 litres of water is exactly 25%."
    keyConcept: In mixture questions one component stays constant (here milk); the new concentration is written as a fraction of the new total.
    examTip: "Anchor on the milk: 36 L must be 75% of the new total, so total = 36 / 0.75 = 48 L and water added = 48 - 40 = 8 L."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 14
    question: Two fair dice are thrown once. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers shown is 9?
    options:
      - 1/12
      - 1/9
      - 5/36
      - 1/6
    answer: 1
    explanation: "There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes and exactly four give a sum of 9: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4) and (6,3). So the probability is 4/36 = 1/9. The 5/36 option counts a fifth pair that does not exist."
    keyConcept: Probability = favourable outcomes / total outcomes, and two dice produce 36 ordered outcomes.
    examTip: List ordered pairs for the target sum; sums climb in frequency up to 7 (six pairs), so 9 can only have four.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
