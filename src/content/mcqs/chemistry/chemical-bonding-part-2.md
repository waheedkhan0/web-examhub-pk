  - id: 1
    question: Which compound will decolorize bromine water?
    options:
      - Ethane
      - Benzene
      - Ethene
      - Methane
    answer: 2
    explanation: Alkenes like ethene (C2H4) have a double bond that undergoes an addition reaction with bromine water, decolorizing it from reddish-brown to colorless. Alkanes and benzene (under normal conditions) do not react.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 2
    question: "The bond angle in water (H₂O) is approximately:"
    options:
      - 180°
      - 120°
      - 109.5°
      - 104.5°
    answer: 3
    explanation: According to VSEPR theory, the central O atom in H₂O has two bond pairs and two lone pairs. The lone pairs repel more strongly, compressing the bond angle from the ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°) to about 104.5°.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 3
    question: Which one of the following is a strong electrolyte?
    options:
      - Acetic acid
      - Glucose
      - Urea
      - NaCl
    answer: 3
    explanation: Strong electrolytes are substances that completely dissociate into ions in solution. NaCl is an ionic compound that dissociates completely into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in water.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 4
    question: Which of the following has a linear structure?
    options:
      - H₂O
      - NH₃
      - CO₂
      - SO₂
    answer: 2
    explanation: CO₂ has a linear structure due to sp hybridization of the carbon atom. The molecule is O=C=O, with a bond angle of 180°.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 5
    question: "Hydrogen bonding is maximum in:"
    options:
      - Ethanol
      - Diethyl ether
      - Water
      - Chloroform
    answer: 2
    explanation: Hydrogen bonding is maximum in water because each water molecule can form up to four hydrogen bonds with neighboring water molecules (two through its H atoms and two through its lone pairs on oxygen).
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 6
    question: Which of the following liquids has the highest vapor pressure at room temperature?
    options:
      - Water
      - Mercury
      - Kerosene oil
      - Diethyl ether
    answer: 3
    explanation: Vapor pressure depends on intermolecular forces. Diethyl ether has weak dipole-dipole interactions and no hydrogen bonding, so it evaporates easily and has a high vapor pressure at room temperature.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 7
    question: Which of the following compounds shows the phenomenon of geometrical isomerism?
    options:
      - CH₄
      - C₂H₆
      - C₂H₄
      - C₂H₂Cl₂
    answer: 3
    explanation: Geometrical isomerism occurs in compounds with restricted rotation, such as alkenes. 1,2-dichloroethene (C₂H₂Cl₂) exists as cis and trans isomers due to the double bond.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 8
    question: Which of the following forces are the weakest?
    options:
      - Ionic bond
      - Covalent bond
      - Hydrogen bond
      - London dispersion forces
    answer: 3
    explanation: London dispersion forces (induced dipole-induced dipole interactions) are the weakest intermolecular forces. They are temporary and exist in all molecules, especially nonpolar ones.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 9
    question: "The geometry of a molecule with 4 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs on the central atom is:"
    options:
      - Trigonal pyramidal
      - Bent
      - Tetrahedral
      - Linear
    answer: 2
    explanation: According to VSEPR theory, an AB₄ type molecule with no lone pairs on the central atom (e.g., CH₄) has a tetrahedral geometry with bond angles of approximately 109.5°.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 10
    question: "The presence of a double bond in a compound is confirmed by the:"
    options:
      - Iodoform test
      - FeCl₃ test
      - Baeyer's test
      - 2,4-DNP test
    answer: 2
    explanation: Baeyer's test (using cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄) is used to test for unsaturation. The purple color of KMnO₄ is discharged as it reacts with the double or triple bond, forming a colorless diol or other products.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 11
    question: Which molecule has a linear geometry?
    options:
      - H₂O
      - NH₃
      - CH₄
      - CO₂
    answer: 3
    explanation: CO₂ has sp hybridization with bond angle 180°. H₂O (bent, 104.5°), NH₃ (trigonal pyramidal, 107°), CH₄ (tetrahedral, 109.5°).
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 12
    question: Which type of solid has highest melting point?
    options:
      - Molecular solid
      - Ionic solid
      - Metallic solid
      - Covalent network solid
    answer: 3
    explanation: Covalent network solids (diamond, SiO₂) have strong directional covalent bonds throughout lattice, giving very high melting points.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 13
    question: "Bond energy is the energy required to:"
    options:
      - Form a bond
      - Break a bond
      - Ionize an atom
      - Excite an electron
    answer: 1
    explanation: Bond dissociation energy is energy required to break one mole of bonds in gaseous state.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 14
    question: Which element shows maximum catenation?
    options:
      - Nitrogen
      - Oxygen
      - Sulfur
      - Carbon
    answer: 3
    explanation: Carbon shows maximum catenation (self-linking ability) due to strong C-C bonds and tetravalency, forming long chains and rings.
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
  - id: 15
    question: "Denaturation of proteins involves disruption of:"
    options:
      - Primary structure
      - Secondary and tertiary structure
      - Peptide bonds
      - All of the above
    answer: 1
    explanation: Denaturation disrupts secondary/tertiary structure (H-bonds, hydrophobic interactions) but not primary structure (peptide bonds remain intact).
    keyConcept: Chemical Bonding & Structure
