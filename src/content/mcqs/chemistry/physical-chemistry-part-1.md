    question: "The pH of 0.001 M H₂SO₄ solution is:"
    options:
      - "1"
      - "2"
      - "3"
      - "4"
    answer: 1
    explanation: "H₂SO₄ is a strong diprotic acid but second dissociation is incomplete. For dilute solutions, we consider complete first dissociation: [H⁺] ≈ 0.002 M (since each H₂SO₄ gives 2H⁺ approximately). pH = -log(0.002) = 2.7 ≈ 3? Wait, more accurately: First dissociation complete: [H⁺] = 0.001 M from first H⁺, plus partial second dissociation. But for MDCAT level, often approximated as [H⁺] = 0.002 M → pH = -log(0.002) = 2.7 ≈ 3. However, standard approximation for 0.001 M H₂SO₄ is pH ≈ 3. Actually, precise calculation: Ka₂ = 0.01, so [H⁺] = 0.001 + [SO₄²⁻]. Solving quadratic gives [H⁺] ≈ 0.0019 M, pH ≈ 2.72 ≈ 3. But many textbooks approximate as pH = 3 for 0.001 M H₂SO₄. However, option 2 (pH=2) would be for 0.01 M strong monoprotic acid. Let me recalculate: For 0.001 M H₂SO₄, considering only first dissociation complete: [H⁺] = 0.001 M → pH = 3. But second dissociation adds more H⁺. Standard answer in MDCAT is pH ≈ 3 for 0.001 M H₂SO₄? Actually, careful calculation shows pH ≈ 2.7-2.8, which rounds to 3. But option 2 is pH=2 which would be for 0.01 M strong acid. I think the expected answer is 3 (option 3). Wait the options are: 1,2,3,4 corresponding to pH values 1,2,3,4. For 0.001 M H₂SO₄, pH is approximately 3 (more precisely 2.7-2.8). So answer should be option 3 (pH=3)."
    keyConcept: Physical Chemistry & Stoichiometry
