    question: "Sociology is a Science because it is:"
    options:
      - Objective
      - Quantitative
      - Methodical
      - Qualitative
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Let's break down why the answer is C, Methodical, and what that means for Sociology! What makes something a science? Basically, science isn't just about *what* you study, but *how* you study it. Option A: Objective While striving for objectivity (being unbiased) is important in science, it's extremely difficult to achieve perfectly, especially in a field like Sociology that deals with human behavior. So, it's not the defining characteristic. Option B: Quantitative Quantitative means using numbers and statistics. Sociology *does* use quantitative methods sometimes, but it's not the only way sociologists gather information. It's not a *requirement* for it to be a science. Option C: Methodical - THIS IS THE ANSWER! Methodical means following a specific, organized plan or procedure. Sociology uses the scientific method , which involves: * Asking a question * Doing background research * Forming a hypothesis (an educated guess) * Testing the hypothesis through experiments or observations * Analyzing the data * Drawing conclusions. Because Sociology uses this methodical approach, it's considered a science. Option D: Qualitative Qualitative means using descriptions, interviews, and observations to understand things. Sociology *heavily* relies on qualitative data but that alone doesn't make it a Science, without following the scientific method. In simple terms: Sociology is a science because it uses a structured, step-by-step approach (the scientific method) to study society, even though it studies people and not chemicals or planets!"

    question: According to whom, "the scope of sociology is the study of forms of social relationships"?
    options:
      - Ferdinand Tönnies
      - Max Weber
      - Albion W. Small
      - Georg Simmel
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Georg Simmel is the sociologist who stated that \"the scope of sociology is the study of forms of social relationships\" . He developed the concept of formal sociology , which focuses on identifying and analyzing the recurring forms of interactions —such as cooperation, competition, subordination, and exchange—regardless of the specific content or subject matter of the interaction. Simmel believed that by studying these forms, sociology can uncover patterns that exist across different societies and situations. This formalist approach set him apart from other sociologists who focused more on the content or purpose of social actions. Thus, Simmel’s work directly supports the idea that sociology is concerned with the forms of social relationships. Other Options: Ferdinand Tönnies is best known for distinguishing between two types of social groups: Gemeinschaft (community) and Gesellschaft (society). His focus was on the nature of social bonds—personal and intimate versus impersonal and contractual—but not specifically on the forms of relationships in the way Simmel described. Max Weber focused on social action and the subjective meanings individuals attach to their behavior. He emphasized understanding the motivations behind actions and the role of values, beliefs, and intentions, rather than the structural forms of social interaction. Albion W. Small was a pioneer of American sociology and founded the first department of sociology at the University of Chicago. While he played a key role in institutionalizing sociology as a discipline, he did not specifically define its scope in terms of studying forms of social relationships. Therefore, among all the options, only Georg Simmel clearly articulated the view that sociology's scope lies in analyzing the forms of social relationships."

    question: Who of the following is very much associated with Group Mind Theory about the origin of the state?
    options:
      - Aristotle
      - Machiavelli
      - Hegel
      - Harold J. Laski
    answer: 2
    explanation: The Group Mind Theory of the origin of the state suggests that the state is not merely a collection of individuals but represents a collective consciousness, common will, or group mind that develops within society. Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel is most closely associated with this theory. According to Hegel, the state is the highest expression of the collective spirit of a people and embodies their ethical and moral life. Hegel viewed the state as an organism that possesses a will and purpose greater than those of individual citizens. In his philosophy, individuals achieve their true freedom through participation in the state, which represents the universal will of society . Option A (Aristotle) is incorrect because he is primarily associated with the theory that man is a political animal and that the state evolved naturally from the family and village. Option B (Machiavelli) is incorrect because he is known for his ideas on political power, statecraft, and practical governance rather than the Group Mind Theory. Option D (Harold J. Laski) is incorrect because he was a pluralist political thinker who emphasized the importance of various social groups and did not support the concept of the state as a supreme collective mind. Therefore, Hegel is the thinker most closely associated with the Group Mind Theory of the origin of the state , making Option C the correct answer.
