    options:
      - one fourth

    answer: 2

According to Joule’s Law, the heat produced (H) in a conductor is given by the formula: H = I² R t where I = current, R = resistance, and t = time. Therefore, when the resistance of a conductor is reduced to half (keeping voltage constant), its thermal effect doubles . Hence, the correct answer is Option C: double ."

    question: Self inductance of a Solenoid is
    options:

      - directly proportional to its length
      - directly proportional to area of cross section
      - inversely proportional to area of cross-section
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Self-inductance is a property of a coil that opposes changes in the current flowing through it. Think of it like inertia for electricity! Several factors affect how much self-inductance a solenoid (a coil of wire) has. Let's break down why the correct answer is C: directly proportional to area of cross section . The formula for self-inductance (L) of a solenoid is: L = (μ₀ * N² * A) / l Where: μ₀ = permeability of free space (a constant) N = number of turns in the solenoid A = area of cross-section of the solenoid l = length of the solenoid Now let's look at why the other options are wrong: Option A: directly proportional to current flowing through the coil - Self-inductance itself doesn't depend on the current. It's a property of the coil, like its resistance. The *voltage* induced *does* depend on the *change* in current. Option B: directly proportional to its length - The formula shows that self-inductance is *inversely* proportional to the length (l) of the solenoid, not directly proportional. If you make the solenoid longer, keeping everything else the same, the inductance decreases. Option D: inversely proportional to area of cross-section - The formula clearly shows that self-inductance is *directly* proportional to the area of cross-section (A), not inversely proportional. A larger area allows for more magnetic flux to be created, increasing the inductance. Therefore, the self-inductance of a solenoid is directly proportional to the area of its cross-section ."
