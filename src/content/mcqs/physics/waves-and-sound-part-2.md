    question: "The fundamental frequency of a vibrating string is 256 Hz. If the length is doubled, the new fundamental frequency will be:"
    options:
      - 512 Hz
      - 256 Hz
      - 128 Hz
      - 64 Hz
    answer: 2
    explanation: For a string fixed at both ends, frequency f ∝ 1/L. If length L is doubled, the frequency becomes half. Therefore, new frequency = 256 Hz / 2 = 128 Hz.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 2
    question: "According to the Doppler effect, the apparent frequency of sound increases when the source and observer are:"
    options:
      - Moving away from each other
      - Moving towards each other
      - At rest
      - Moving perpendicular
    answer: 1
    explanation: When a source of sound and an observer move towards each other, the number of wavefronts received per second increases. This results in an increase in the observed frequency.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 3
    question: "The energy of a photon is given by:"
    options:
      - hcλ
      - hλ/c
      - hc/λ
      - h/cλ
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The energy (E) of a photon is directly proportional to its frequency (f): E = hf. Since frequency f = c/λ, where c is the speed of light and λ is the wavelength, we get E = hc/λ."
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 4
    question: "In a half-wave rectifier, the output frequency is:"
    options:
      - Same as input frequency
      - Double the input frequency
      - Half the input frequency
      - Zero
    answer: 0
    explanation: In a half-wave rectifier, only one half of the input AC cycle is allowed to pass. The output waveform consists of pulses for each positive half-cycle. Therefore, the frequency of the output ripple is the same as the input AC frequency (e.g., 50 Hz).
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 5
    question: Which of the following is NOT a mode of heat transfer?
    options:
      - Conduction
      - Convection
      - Radiation
      - Absorption
    answer: 3
    explanation: "Heat is transferred via three main modes: conduction (through solids), convection (through fluids), and radiation (via electromagnetic waves). Absorption is a process by which a material takes in energy, not a mode of transfer."
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 6
    question: "The speed of sound is maximum in:"
    options:
      - Vacuum
      - Air
      - Water
      - Steel
    answer: 3
    explanation: Sound travels fastest in solids because the particles are more tightly bound, allowing vibrations to transfer more quickly. It is slowest in gases. Speed in steel >> speed in water >> speed in air.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 7
    question: "Nodes and antinodes are formed in:"
    options:
      - Stationary waves
      - Progressive waves
      - Transverse waves only
      - Longitudinal waves only
    answer: 0
    explanation: A stationary wave (standing wave) is characterized by points of zero displacement called nodes and points of maximum displacement called antinodes, formed by the superposition of two identical waves traveling in opposite directions.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 8
    question: "The photoelectric effect supports the:"
    options:
      - Wave theory of light
      - Quantum theory of light
      - Electromagnetic theory
      - Principle of superposition
    answer: 1
    explanation: The photoelectric effect could not be explained by the wave theory of light. Einstein explained it using the quantum theory, proposing that light consists of discrete packets of energy called photons.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 9
    question: "In a series LCR circuit, at resonance, the impedance is:"
    options:
      - Maximum
      - Minimum
      - Zero
      - Infinite
    answer: 1
    explanation: At resonance, the inductive reactance (XL) equals the capacitive reactance (XC). They cancel each other out, so the impedance Z = √(R² + (XL - XC)²) becomes purely resistive and equal to R, which is the minimum possible impedance.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 10
    question: Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the highest frequency?
    options:
      - Radio waves
      - Microwaves
      - Ultraviolet rays
      - Gamma rays
    answer: 3
    explanation: The electromagnetic spectrum is arranged by frequency and wavelength. Gamma rays have the highest frequency and shortest wavelength, followed by X-rays and then ultraviolet rays.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 11
    question: "When a ray of light enters from air into a glass slab, its wavelength:"
    options:
      - Decreases
      - Increases
      - Remains the same
      - Becomes zero
    answer: 0
    explanation: When light enters a denser medium (glass) from a rarer medium (air), its speed (v) and wavelength (λ) decrease. However, its frequency (f) remains constant because it depends on the source. v = fλ, so if v decreases, λ must also decrease.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 12
    question: "In a series resonant circuit, the power factor is:"
    options:
      - "0"
      - "0.5"
      - "1"
      - Infinity
    answer: 2
    explanation: At resonance, the circuit is purely resistive because the inductive and capacitive reactances cancel out. For a purely resistive circuit, the phase angle φ = 0°, so the power factor cos φ = 1.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 13
    question: "The phenomenon of polarization of light proves that light waves are:"
    options:
      - Longitudinal
      - Transverse
      - Mechanical
      - Stationary
    answer: 1
    explanation: Polarization is a property of transverse waves. Longitudinal waves (like sound) cannot be polarized because their vibration is parallel to the direction of propagation. Polarization confirms that light waves are transverse in nature.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 14
    question: "In a photoelectric experiment, the stopping potential depends on:"
    options:
      - Intensity of light
      - Frequency of light
      - Distance from the source
      - Work function of the metal
    answer: 1
    explanation: The stopping potential (V₀) is given by eV₀ = hf - φ, where f is the frequency and φ is the work function. While it depends on the work function, for a given metal, it varies linearly with the frequency of the incident light. It is independent of intensity.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound
  - id: 15
    question: "Two waves of the same frequency and amplitude traveling in opposite directions produce:"
    options:
      - Beats
      - Stationary waves
      - Interference pattern
      - Diffraction pattern
    answer: 1
    explanation: When two identical waves traveling in opposite directions superimpose, they produce a stationary wave (standing wave). The wave appears to be standing still with nodes and antinodes.
    keyConcept: Waves & Sound

    question: "The frequency of A.C. mains in Pakistan is:"
    options:
      - 50 Hz
      - 60 Hz
      - 100 Hz
      - 220 Hz
    answer: 0
    explanation: In Pakistan, as in many parts of the world (Europe, Asia), the standard frequency for alternating current (AC) mains electricity is 50 Hz.
