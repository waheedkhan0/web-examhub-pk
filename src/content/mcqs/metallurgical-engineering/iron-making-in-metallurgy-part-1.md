    question: The deadman's zone in the blast furnace consists of
    options:
      - closely packed central columm of coke, limestone and iron ore
      - gases only
      - closely packed central column of coke only
      - column of hot metal and slag
    answer: 1
    explanation: The deadman's zone in a blast furnace refers to the central region at the bottom of the furnace where the burden materials slow down and become relatively stagnant. This zone is composed primarily of a closely packed column of coke that remains largely stationary while the surrounding materials continue to descend. The coke in this region supports the overlying burden and provides permeability for the upward flow of gases. It is called the "deadman" because of its limited movement compared to other parts of the furnace burden. Therefore, Option C is correct as the deadman zone consists mainly of coke only .

    question: Desulfurisation of hot metal is favoured by
    options:
      - reducing and neutral slag
      - high activity co-efficient of sulphur in metal
      - oxidising and basic slag
      - reducing and basic slag
    answer: 3
    explanation: Desulfurisation is the process of removing sulphur from hot metal (usually molten iron) to improve its quality. This process is favoured by a reducing atmosphere because an oxidising environment can lead to reversion of sulphur back into the metal. A basic slag (rich in lime, CaO) reacts with sulphur to form calcium sulphide (CaS) , which is stable and can be easily removed. Thus, a reducing and basic slag environment promotes maximum sulphur removal by encouraging the formation and retention of CaS in the slag phase. Therefore, Option D is the correct answer as it provides the most favourable conditions for desulfurisation.
