    question: The law of statistics, which says "Moderately large number of items chosen at random from a large group possess the characteristics of the large group", is referred to as
    options:
      - The central limit theorem
      - The law of statistical regularly
      - The law of inertia of large numbers
      - None of the above
    answer: 1
    explanation: The law of statistical regularity states that a moderately large number of items chosen at random from a large group will likely possess the same characteristics as the large group. This law is fundamental in sampling theory and ensures that a sample is representative of the population from which it is drawn. It relies on the principle of probability, which guarantees that random selection leads to an accurate reflection of the population's properties. This law is crucial in fields like business statistics where sampling is essential for data analysis.

    question: Classification of respondents only on the basis of gender is an application of
    options:
      - Ordinal scale
      - Nominal scale
      - Interval scale
      - Ratio scale
    answer: 1
    explanation: "Understanding Scales of Measurement: In statistics, we use different scales to measure data. Think of it like measuring things with different rulers. Nominal Scale: This is like labeling things with names. We just give them categories without any order or ranking. Example: Colors (red, blue, green), Gender (male, female), Types of cars (sedan, SUV, truck). Ordinal Scale: This scale has order or ranking, but the difference between each rank isn't necessarily equal. Example: Education level (high school, bachelor's, master's), Customer satisfaction (very satisfied, satisfied, neutral, dissatisfied, very dissatisfied). Interval Scale: Here, the difference between each point is equal, but there's no true zero point. Example: Temperature in Celsius or Fahrenheit (0°C doesn't mean there's no temperature). Ratio Scale: This scale has equal intervals and a true zero point. Zero means the absence of the thing being measured. Example: Height, weight, age, income. Applying it to the Question: The question asks about classifying respondents by gender (male, female). We're just assigning labels, not ranking or measuring anything numerically. Therefore, this is a nominal scale . Therefore, the correct answer is B: Nominal scale"

    question: Which of the following is the non-random method of selecting sampling from a population?
    options:
      - Stratified sampling
      - Quota sampling
      - Systematic sampling
      - Cluster sampling
    answer: 1
    explanation: "In sampling methods, both random and non-random techniques are employed to gather data from a population. Stratified Sampling: Explanation: This is a random sampling technique where the population is divided into subgroups or strata based on certain characteristics, and then samples are randomly selected from each stratum. Example: If a population is categorized by age, samples are randomly taken from each age group. Quota Sampling: Explanation: In quota sampling, the population is divided into specific groups, and a certain number of individuals are chosen from each group, not necessarily through a random process. Example: If a population is divided by gender, a quota of participants from each gender may be selected purposefully. Systematic Sampling: Explanation: In systematic sampling, a fixed interval is used to select samples after an initial random start. It is a structured approach but not entirely random. Example: Choosing every 5th person from a list after a random starting point. Cluster Sampling: Explanation: The population is divided into clusters, and then some clusters are randomly selected, and all members from those clusters are included in the sample. Example: If a city is divided into districts, randomly select some districts, and survey all individuals within those districts. In the context of the question, Quota Sampling is the non-random method as it involves selecting individuals purposefully based on certain quotas rather than entirely at random. Therefore, the correct answer is Option B: Quota sampling."
