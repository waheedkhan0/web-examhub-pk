    question: "The approach used when the marketer wants the consumer to respond on an emotional, rather than rational basis:"
    options:
      - Predatory Pricing
      - Economy Pricing
      - Psychological Pricing
      - Penetration Pricing
    answer: 2
    explanation: "The approach where marketers aim to evoke an emotional response from consumers rather than a purely rational one is known as Psychological Pricing . This strategy often involves setting prices that end in certain digits, like $9.99 instead of $10.00, to create a perception of a lower price and encourage emotional buying decisions. Option A: Predatory Pricing is a strategy where a company sets very low prices to drive competitors out of the market, which is not related to emotional responses. Option B: Economy Pricing is focused on offering products at low prices based on cost efficiency, not emotional responses. Option D: Penetration Pricing is used to gain market share by setting initially low prices, but it's not primarily aimed at emotional responses. So, the correct answer is Option C: Psychological Pricing , as it targets emotional responses in consumers through pricing strategies."

    question: Market sub-divided on the basis of behavioural characteristics is called
    options:
      - Segmentation
      - Aggregation
      - Precision
      - None of the above
    answer: 0
    explanation: "Market segmentation is the process of dividing a broad market into smaller groups of consumers based on specific characteristics, needs, preferences, or behaviours. When a market is divided on the basis of behavioural characteristics , such as purchasing behaviour, usage rate, brand loyalty, benefits sought, or response to a product, it is known as market segmentation . Option A: Segmentation — The process of dividing a market into smaller groups based on characteristics such as behaviour, needs, or preferences. This is the correct answer. Option B: Aggregation — Refers to combining or grouping different elements rather than dividing a market into behavioural segments. Option C: Precision — Refers to accuracy or exactness and is not the term used for dividing a market based on behavioural characteristics. Option D: None of the above — Incorrect because Segmentation is the appropriate term. Final Answer: Segmentation"

  - id: 3
    question: . . . . . . . . pricing means assigning a low price tag for a product and providing the benefits of low-cost mass production to the customers.
    options:
      - Cost plus
      - Value
      - Power price points
      - Penetration
    answer: 3
    explanation: "The correct answer is Option D: Penetration . Let's understand this step-by-step for beginners: The question is asking about a specific way to set a product's price. This way involves two main things: 1. Giving the product a very low price . 2. Making sure this low price benefits customers because the company produces a lot of the product at a low cost (mass production) . This strategy is called Penetration Pricing . Imagine you want to \"penetrate\" or enter a new market with a product very quickly. To do this, you set a super low initial price . This low price attracts a lot of customers fast, helping you to: 1. Get a large number of buyers quickly . 2. Grab a big share of the market before competitors can react. 3. This strategy works well when you can produce many items cheaply (mass production) and pass those savings to customers through a low price. Now, let's see why the other options don't fit: Option A: Cost plus This means you calculate the total cost to make your product and then add a percentage for your profit. So, it's \"cost + a little extra.\" It doesn't necessarily mean starting with a *very low* price to attract customers, but rather covering costs and making a set profit. Option B: Value Value pricing is about setting a price based on what customers think the product is worth to them, considering its benefits and quality. While it aims to offer good value, it's not specifically about using a *super low initial price* because of mass production to enter a market. Option C: Power price points This term is not a standard or common pricing strategy in marketing management. It does not describe assigning a low price specifically because of mass production benefits. Therefore, the description in the question perfectly matches Penetration Pricing , where a low price is used to quickly \"penetrate\" the market, often leveraging low costs from mass production."
