    question: Which country extended the first de jure recognition to Pakistan in December 1947?
    options:
      - The United Kingdom
      - The United States
      - China
      - The Soviet Union
    answer: 3
    explanation: Standard diplomatic histories record that the Soviet Union granted de jure recognition in December 1947, the first state to do so, even though Soviet-Pakistani relations stayed cold through the treaty-bound 1950s. The United States also recognised Pakistan in 1947, but not before the Soviet step.
    keyConcept: Recognition in Pakistan's first year and the split between Western allies and the USSR in 1947-1953.
    examTip: "Memorise the pair: first de jure recognition - the USSR (December 1947); first large aid and treaty partner - the USA (from 1954)."

    question: The Non-Aligned Movement, rooted in the 1955 Bandung Conference and formally launched in 1961 with Pakistan among its founding members, held its first summit in which city?
    options:
      - Cairo
      - Belgrade
      - New Delhi
      - Bandung
    answer: 1
    explanation: The first Non-Aligned Movement summit met at Belgrade, Yugoslavia, from 1 to 6 September 1961. Bandung is the tempting answer because the 1955 Asian-African Conference that inspired the movement was held there, but Bandung hosted no NAM summit.
    keyConcept: The road from Bandung 1955 to Belgrade 1961, and Pakistan's founding-member role in NAM.
    examTip: Bandung = 1955 conference; Belgrade = 1961 founding summit; later summits include Cairo 1964, Lusaka 1970 and Havana 1979.

    question: The headquarters and international secretariat of SEATO, the alliance created by the 1954 Manila Treaty to which Pakistan adhered as a founding member, were located in which city?
    options:
      - Manila
      - Jakarta
      - Karachi
      - Bangkok
    answer: 3
    explanation: Although the founding treaty was signed at Manila on 8 September 1954, the SEATO secretariat was headquartered in Bangkok, Thailand. The organisation was wound up in 1977 after its members drifted away.
    keyConcept: "Pakistan's Cold-War Western alignments: SEATO from 1954 and the Baghdad Pact from 1955."
    examTip: "Examiners switch the two cities: signed at Manila, headquartered at Bangkok."

    question: After Iraq withdrew following the 1958 revolution, the Baghdad Pact of 1955 - joined by Pakistan in that year - was renamed in 1959 to which organisation?
    options:
      - SEATO
      - NATO
      - ANZUS
      - CENTO
    answer: 3
    explanation: The pact was reconstituted in 1959 as the Central Treaty Organisation, CENTO, with its secretariat moving from Baghdad to Ankara. ANZUS and NATO are real alliances of the same era, and SEATO was Pakistan's other membership, which makes all three plausible-looking distractors.
    keyConcept: "Baghdad Pact (1955) becoming CENTO (1959): the northern-tier strategy against Soviet expansion."
    examTip: "Chain it: 1955 Baghdad Pact, 1958 Iraqi revolution, 1959 renamed CENTO at Ankara, collapsing in 1979 after the Iranian revolution."

    question: The China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) was formally launched through a package of agreements signed during the April visit of China's President Xi Jinping to Pakistan in which year?
    options:
      - "2008"
      - "2013"
      - "2015"
      - "2017"
    answer: 2
    explanation: CPEC was formalised in April 2015 during Xi Jinping's Islamabad visit, which produced the corridor's framework agreements and announced project commitments. 2013 is the strongest distractor because that is when the corridor's planning was revived by Pakistan's new government, and 2008 saw the Pakistan-China free trade agreement.
    keyConcept: CPEC as the modern successor to Karakoram Highway-era connectivity, formally launched in 2015.
    examTip: "Sequence the China milestones: 1951 recognition, 1963 border accord, Karakoram Highway built from 1966 to the late 1970s, 2015 CPEC launch."

    question: Which pairing of a Pakistan-India agreement with the year it was signed is correct?
    options:
      - Tashkent Declaration - 1971; Shimla Agreement - 1966
      - Tashkent Declaration - 1966; Shimla Agreement - 1972
      - Indus Waters Treaty - 1960; Tashkent Declaration - 1965
      - Shimla Agreement - 1971; Indus Waters Treaty - 1965
    answer: 1
    explanation: The Tashkent Declaration was signed on 10 January 1966 after the 1965 war, and the Shimla Agreement on 2 July 1972 after the 1971 war, which makes option 2 the only fully correct pair. Option 3 is attractive because the Indus Waters Treaty year is right, but it is joined to the wrong Tashkent date - every half of a paired answer must hold.
    keyConcept: "Ordering the post-war settlements: Indus Waters 1960, Tashkent 1966, Shimla 1972."
    examTip: "Remember the rhythm: war then settlement - 1965 war to 1966 Tashkent, 1971 war to 1972 Shimla."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC

    question: Under the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960, brokered by the World Bank, which three rivers were allocated to Pakistan?
    options:
      - Indus, Jhelum and Chenab
      - Ravi, Beas and Sutlej
      - Indus, Ravi and Beas
      - Jhelum, Sutlej and Chenab
    answer: 0
    explanation: The treaty gave Pakistan the three western rivers - the Indus, Jhelum and Chenab - while India took the eastern rivers, the Ravi, Beas and Sutlej. Option 3 mixes one western river with two eastern ones, the typical trap. India retains limited non-consumptive uses such as run-of-the-river power projects on the western rivers.
    keyConcept: "Indus Waters Treaty 1960: western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan; eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India."
    examTip: "Eastern equals India: Ravi, Beas and Sutlej are the three rivers India uses, so Pakistan keeps the Indus system."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC

    question: Pakistan's first series of nuclear weapon tests at the Chagai test range was conducted in May of which year?
    options:
      - "1994"
      - "1996"
      - "1998"
      - "2002"
    answer: 2
    explanation: Operation Chagai-I was carried out on 28 May 1998, followed by a second series on 30 May, days after India's Pokhran-II tests of 11 to 13 May 1998. Both tests fell in 1998, which rules out the 1996 and 2002 options; the outcome made Pakistan the first nuclear-armed Muslim-majority state.
    keyConcept: "Nuclear diplomacy: May 1998 Chagai tests, the resulting sanctions and the export-control regimes that followed."
    examTip: "Fix the sequence: India 11 to 13 May 1998, Pakistan 28 and 30 May 1998 - the same year, a fortnight apart."

    question: Allama Muhammad Iqbal first set out the vision of a separate Muslim polity in North-Western India in his presidential address to the Muslim League session at Allahabad in December of which year?
    options:
      - "1928"
      - "1930"
      - "1937"
      - "1940"
    answer: 1
    explanation: Iqbal delivered the address as president of the 21st annual Muslim League session at Allahabad in December 1930. The 1940 option is the trap, since that is the year of the Lahore Resolution, which turned his idea into the party's formal political demand; his wider pan-Islamic thought also underlies the Islamic-solidarity strand of Pakistan's foreign policy.
    keyConcept: Iqbal's 1930 Allahabad address as the ideological seed of Pakistan and of its Islamic-world foreign outlook.
    examTip: Allahabad 1930 is Iqbal's idea; Lahore 1940 is the Resolution. Never swap the two.
