    question: How many bones are there in the complete adult human skeleton?
    options:
      - "206"
      - "300"
      - "212"
      - "198"
    answer: 0
    explanation: The adult human skeleton has 206 bones. A newborn has roughly 300, because bones of the skull, spine and pelvis fuse during growth, so 300 is the infant figure and not the adult one.
    keyConcept: Adult skeleton = 206 bones; infants have about 300 which fuse with age
    examTip: Examiners offer 300 as an option to catch candidates who recall the infant figure instead of the adult count.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
      - FPSC
  - id: 2
    question: How many pairs of ribs are normally found in the human thoracic cage?
    options:
      - 10 pairs
      - 11 pairs
      - 12 pairs
      - 13 pairs
    answer: 2
    explanation: Humans normally have 12 pairs of ribs, 24 in all. The first seven pairs attach directly to the sternum (true ribs), pairs eight to ten join it indirectly through costal cartilage, and the last two pairs are floating ribs with no anterior attachment.
    keyConcept: Thoracic cage = 12 pairs of ribs (7 true, 3 false, 2 floating)
    examTip: The eleventh and twelfth pairs are the floating ribs; remember that ribs protect the heart and lungs.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 3
    question: Which ABO blood group is known as the universal donor?
    options:
      - Group AB
      - Group A
      - Group B
      - Group O
    answer: 3
    explanation: Group O red blood cells carry neither the A nor the B antigen, so they can be given to a recipient of any other group in an emergency, with O negative being the classic universal donor. Group AB is the opposite case, the universal recipient, because its plasma contains no anti-A or anti-B antibodies.
    keyConcept: Group O = universal donor (no A or B antigens); group AB = universal recipient
    examTip: "Do not reverse the pair: donor status depends on antigens on the red cells, recipient status on antibodies in the plasma."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 4
    question: "In a normal adult blood pressure reading of about 120/80 mmHg, the higher figure 120 indicates:"
    options:
      - The pressure in the arteries while the heart muscle relaxes
      - The pressure in the arteries while the ventricles contract
      - The pressure standing in the veins at rest
      - The difference between arterial and venous pressure
    answer: 1
    explanation: 120 mmHg is the systolic pressure, recorded while the ventricles contract and eject blood into the arteries; 80 mmHg is the diastolic pressure recorded while the heart relaxes and refills. Venous pressure is far lower than either figure.
    keyConcept: Normal adult blood pressure about 120/80 mmHg; the larger figure is systolic (contraction)
    examTip: Systolic is always the upper number and diastolic the lower; a reading consistently at or above 140/90 mmHg is called hypertension.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 5
    question: How many chromosomes are normally present in a human body (somatic) cell?
    options:
      - "23"
      - "44"
      - "46"
      - "92"
    answer: 2
    explanation: "Human body cells are diploid and contain 23 pairs, that is 46 chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes plus one pair of sex chromosomes. Gametes, the sperm and the ovum, are haploid and carry 23 chromosomes."
    keyConcept: Human diploid number = 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); haploid gamete = 23
    examTip: 23 is the tempting distractor because it is the gamete number, and 44 is the number of autosomes alone.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
  - id: 6
    question: "The basic structural and functional unit of the human kidney is the:"
    options:
      - Alveolus
      - Nephron
      - Villus
      - Neuron
    answer: 1
    explanation: Each kidney contains about a million nephrons, which filter blood, reabsorb the substances the body needs and pass the rest out as urine. Alveoli are the air sacs of the lung, villi line the small intestine and neurons are nerve cells.
    keyConcept: Nephron = the urine-forming filter unit of the kidney
    examTip: "Learn the four units together so they cannot be swapped: nephron (kidney), neuron (nerve), alveolus (lung), villus (intestine)."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 7
    question: Which of the following is the largest gland in the human body?
    options:
      - Thyroid gland
      - Adrenal gland
      - Pancreas
      - Liver
    answer: 3
    explanation: The liver is the largest gland and also the largest internal organ, weighing roughly 1.4 to 1.5 kg in an adult; it secretes bile and carries out the metabolism of proteins, carbohydrates, fats and drugs. The skin is the largest organ of the body overall, but it is not a gland.
    keyConcept: Liver = largest gland and largest internal organ; skin = largest organ overall
    examTip: "Read the stem closely: 'largest organ' is skin, 'largest gland' or 'largest internal organ' is liver."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 8
    question: Which part of the human brain coordinates voluntary movements and maintains posture and balance?
    options:
      - Cerebrum
      - Cerebellum
      - Hypothalamus
      - Thalamus
    answer: 1
    explanation: The cerebellum co-ordinates and fine-tunes voluntary muscle activity and keeps posture and balance steady, so injury to it makes movements jerky and unsteady. The cerebrum carries out conscious thought and initiates voluntary action, while the hypothalamus regulates temperature, hunger and hormone release.
    keyConcept: Cerebellum = coordination of voluntary movement, posture and balance
    examTip: The medulla oblongata, not the cerebellum, controls involuntary acts such as heartbeat and breathing; the hypothalamus is the thermostat of the body.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 9
    question: "Insulin, the hormone that reduces the glucose level of the blood, is secreted by:"
    options:
      - The islets of Langerhans of the pancreas
      - The follicles of the thyroid gland
      - The cortex of the adrenal gland
      - The posterior pituitary gland
    answer: 0
    explanation: Insulin is made by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas, and too little of it, or insensitivity to it, produces diabetes mellitus. The thyroid follicles secrete thyroxine, the adrenal cortex secretes cortisol and the posterior pituitary releases ADH and oxytocin.
    keyConcept: Pancreas (islets of Langerhans, beta cells) secretes insulin; deficiency causes diabetes mellitus
    examTip: "Match hormone to gland before reading the options: insulin-pancreas, thyroxine-thyroid, adrenaline-adrenal medulla, growth hormone-pituitary."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - FPSC
  - id: 10
    question: "Scurvy, with its swollen bleeding gums and slow healing of wounds, is caused by deficiency of:"
    options:
      - Vitamin A
      - Vitamin B1 (thiamine)
      - Vitamin C
      - Vitamin D
    answer: 2
    explanation: Scurvy results from a shortage of vitamin C (ascorbic acid), which is needed to build collagen; it classically struck sailors without fresh fruit or vegetables and is relieved by citrus juice. Vitamin B1 deficiency causes beriberi, vitamin A causes night blindness and vitamin D causes rickets.
    keyConcept: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) deficiency = scurvy; sources are citrus fruit and fresh vegetables
    examTip: "Learn the deficiency table as one block: A night blindness, B1 beriberi, B3 pellagra, C scurvy, D rickets, B12 anaemia."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 11
    question: Which vitamin is produced in the human skin when it is exposed to the ultraviolet rays of sunlight?
    options:
      - Vitamin K
      - Vitamin A
      - Vitamin B12
      - Vitamin D
    answer: 3
    explanation: Ultraviolet radiation converts a cholesterol derivative in the skin into vitamin D, which is then activated by the liver and the kidney; the vitamin is required for the absorption of calcium and phosphate and so for healthy bone. Vitamin K is also made inside the body, but by bacteria living in the intestine rather than by sunlight.
    keyConcept: Vitamin D is synthesised in the skin by ultraviolet light and is needed for calcium absorption
    examTip: "Two vitamins have an internal source: D from sunlight on the skin and K from intestinal bacteria; the rest must come from food."
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - PPSC
  - id: 12
    question: "Malaria, a disease caused by the protozoan Plasmodium, is transmitted to a healthy person by the bite of:"
    options:
      - The female Anopheles mosquito
      - The Aedes aegypti mosquito
      - The Culex mosquito
      - The sandfly
    answer: 0
    explanation: Only the female Anopheles mosquito spreads Plasmodium, which multiplies in the liver and then bursts red blood cells, producing the repeated attacks of fever and rigour. Aedes transmits dengue and yellow fever, Culex transmits filariasis and the sandfly transmits kala-azar.
    keyConcept: Malaria = Plasmodium parasite carried by the female Anopheles mosquito
    examTip: "Examiners swap the four vectors: Anopheles-malaria, Aedes-dengue, Culex-filariasis, sandfly-kala-azar."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 13
    question: Which one of the following diseases is caused by a bacterium rather than by a virus?
    options:
      - Influenza
      - Tuberculosis
      - Measles
      - Dengue
    answer: 1
    explanation: Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which chiefly attacks the lungs and can be treated with antibiotics. Influenza, measles and dengue are viral diseases, so antibiotics have no effect on them.
    keyConcept: Tuberculosis = bacterial (Mycobacterium tuberculosis); influenza, measles and dengue = viral
    examTip: Standard bacterial examples are TB, cholera, typhoid and pneumonia; standard viral examples are AIDS, hepatitis, rabies, polio, measles and influenza.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - CSS
  - id: 14
    question: Most of the absorbed products of digestion pass into the blood in which part of the alimentary canal?
    options:
      - Stomach
      - Large intestine
      - Small intestine
      - Oesophagus
    answer: 2
    explanation: The small intestine is lined with villi and microvilli that multiply its surface area, making it the chief site for absorption of the end products of digestion. The stomach mainly churns food and begins protein digestion, while the large intestine reabsorbs water and salts.
    keyConcept: Small intestine (villi) = principal site of absorption; large intestine absorbs water and salts
    examTip: Bile from the liver and pancreatic juice both enter the duodenum, which is why most digestion is completed in the small intestine.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
