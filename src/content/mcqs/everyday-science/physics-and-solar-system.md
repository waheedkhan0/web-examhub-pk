    question: "The SI base unit of electric current is the:"
    options:
      - Volt
      - Ampere
      - Watt
      - Coulomb
    answer: 1
    explanation: The ampere is one of the seven SI base units and measures electric current. The volt measures potential difference, the watt measures power and the coulomb measures charge; all three of these are derived units.
    keyConcept: "The seven SI base units: metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela"
    examTip: Current is the base quantity here; the coulomb is defined from it as one ampere flowing for one second.
    testedIn:

      - NTS
  - id: 2
    question: "In astronomy, a light-year is a unit of:"
    options:
      - Time
      - The brightness of a star
      - Distance
      - The speed of light
    answer: 2
    explanation: A light-year is the distance light travels through vacuum in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres (9.46 x 10^15 m), so it measures astronomical distance and never a length of time. The parsec, another unit of astronomical distance, equals about 3.26 light-years.
    keyConcept: Light-year = distance travelled by light in one year (about 9.46 x 10^15 m); parsec = about 3.26 light-years
    examTip: The word 'year' pushes candidates towards 'time'; a light-year is always a distance.
    testedIn:
      - CSS
      - FPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 3
    question: "A barometer is an instrument used to measure:"
    options:
      - Atmospheric pressure
      - The humidity of the air
      - The specific gravity of a liquid
      - Electric current in a circuit
    answer: 0
    explanation: A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, commonly reported in millibars or in millimetres of mercury, and a falling reading usually signals changing or rainy weather. Humidity is measured by a hygrometer, the density of a liquid by a hydrometer and current by an ammeter.
    keyConcept: Barometer = atmospheric pressure; hygrometer = humidity; hydrometer = liquid density; manometer = gas pressure in a vessel
    examTip: These instrument names look alike; learn barometer, hygrometer, hydrometer and manometer as one comparison table.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 4
    question: "The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:"
    options:
      - 3 x 10^6 m/s
      - 3 x 10^7 m/s
      - 3 x 10^10 m/s
      - 3 x 10^8 m/s
    answer: 3
    explanation: Light travels at about 3 x 10^8 metres per second in vacuum, exactly 299,792,458 m/s, and it slows a little in water and in glass. The value 3 x 10^10 is the same speed expressed in centimetres per second, so always read the unit attached to each option.
    keyConcept: Speed of light c is about 3 x 10^8 m/s = 3 x 10^5 km/s in vacuum, and it is the cosmic speed limit
    examTip: "Same speed, three ways of writing it: 300,000 km/s, 3 x 10^8 m/s and about 186,000 miles per second."
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 5
    question: "The speed of sound in air at about 20 degrees Celsius is approximately:"
    options:
      - 34 m/s
      - 343 m/s
      - 3,430 m/s
      - 34,300 m/s
    answer: 1
    explanation: Sound travels at about 343 m/s in air at room temperature, and at about 331 m/s at 0 degrees Celsius; it becomes faster in warmer air. In liquids and solids it is faster still, because the particles are closer together and pass on the vibration more readily.
    keyConcept: Speed of sound is about 343 m/s in air at 20 degrees C; sound needs a material medium and is fastest in solids
    examTip: "Compare with light: lightning is seen before thunder is heard because sound is roughly a million times slower than light."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 6
    question: Which of Newton's laws gives the relationship force equals mass times acceleration, F = ma?
    options:
      - Newton's first law of motion
      - Newton's second law of motion
      - Newton's third law of motion
      - Newton's law of universal gravitation
    answer: 1
    explanation: The second law states that the rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the applied force, which reduces to F = ma when the mass is constant. The first law defines inertia, the third law pairs action with reaction, and gravitation gives the attraction between two masses.
    keyConcept: Newton's second law, F = ma; one newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s^2
    examTip: If a stem mentions force, acceleration or momentum it is the second law; the words 'remains at rest' or 'in motion' point to the first law.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 7
    question: "For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume. This statement is:"
    options:
      - Boyle's law
      - Charles's law
      - Ohm's law
      - Archimedes' principle
    answer: 0
    explanation: Boyle's law states that pressure multiplied by volume stays constant when the temperature is fixed, which is why pulling back the plunger of a syringe enlarges the volume, lowers the pressure and draws fluid in. Charles's law instead links the volume of a gas to its temperature at constant pressure.
    keyConcept: "Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume, so PV = constant"
    examTip: Boyle joins pressure and volume; Charles joins volume and temperature; Ohm's law (V = IR) belongs to electricity, not to gases.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 8
    question: "In a hydroelectric power station, the energy conversion that takes place is:"
    options:
      - Chemical energy into electrical energy
      - Nuclear energy into thermal energy into electrical energy
      - Gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy into electrical energy
      - Electrical energy into mechanical energy
    answer: 2
    explanation: Water held behind the dam stores gravitational potential energy; as it falls this becomes kinetic energy, which turns a turbine coupled to a generator that produces electricity. The last option describes an electric motor, which runs the same conversion in reverse.
    keyConcept: "Energy conversion chain in a dam: potential, then kinetic, then mechanical in the turbine, then electrical in the generator"
    examTip: "Read conversion questions left to right: a generator produces electricity, a motor consumes it, and a cell turns chemical energy into electrical energy."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 9
    question: "Short-sightedness, or myopia, is corrected by using:"
    options:
      - A convex lens
      - A concave lens
      - A cylindrical lens
      - A plane mirror
    answer: 1
    explanation: In myopia the image of a distant object is focused in front of the retina, and a diverging concave lens spreads the rays so the image falls on the retina. A converging convex lens corrects long-sightedness (hypermetropia), and a cylindrical lens is used for astigmatism.
    keyConcept: Myopia corrected by a concave (diverging) lens; hypermetropia by a convex lens; astigmatism by a cylindrical lens
    examTip: "Note the twist: a concave lens diverges light, but a concave mirror converges it, so state whether the instrument is a lens or a mirror."
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - PPSC
  - id: 10
    question: Which planet of the Solar System has the highest surface temperature?
    options:
      - Venus
      - Mercury
      - Mars
      - Jupiter
    answer: 0
    explanation: Venus is the hottest planet, with a surface temperature of about 465 degrees Celsius, because its dense carbon dioxide atmosphere and clouds of sulphuric acid trap heat in a runaway greenhouse effect. Mercury lies closer to the Sun but has almost no atmosphere, so it cannot retain heat.
    keyConcept: Venus = hottest planet (about 465 degrees C) owing to the carbon dioxide greenhouse effect; Mercury = nearest planet to the Sun
    examTip: Nearest to the Sun does not mean hottest; Venus is the exception examiners like to test, and Mars is the red planet.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 11
    question: "The largest planet in the Solar System, both in diameter and in mass, is:"
    options:
      - Saturn
      - Neptune
      - Jupiter
      - Uranus
    answer: 2
    explanation: Jupiter is the largest planet, with a diameter about eleven times and a mass about 318 times that of Earth; it is a gas giant made chiefly of hydrogen and helium. Saturn is the second largest planet and is the one famous for its prominent rings.
    keyConcept: Jupiter = largest planet (gas giant); Saturn = second largest and the ringed planet
    examTip: "Order of size, largest first: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury."
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
  - id: 12
    question: Which one of the following is classified as a dwarf planet rather than as a major planet or a moon?
    options:
      - Ganymede
      - Europa
      - Titan
      - Pluto
    answer: 3
    explanation: Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet by the International Astronomical Union in 2006 because it has not cleared the neighbourhood of its orbit of other Kuiper Belt bodies. Ganymede and Europa are moons of Jupiter and Titan is a moon of Saturn, and the dwarf planet list also includes Ceres, Eris, Makemake and Haumea.
    keyConcept: "Dwarf planets recognised by the IAU: Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Makemake and Haumea"
    examTip: Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System, bigger in diameter than Mercury, yet it remains a satellite and not a planet.
    testedIn:
      - FPSC
      - CSS
  - id: 13
    question: The Earth takes approximately how long to complete one revolution around the Sun, which is why a leap day is added every fourth year?
    options:
      - 365 days exactly
      - 354 days
      - 365 1/4 days
      - 366 days
    answer: 2
    explanation: One revolution takes about 365.25 days, roughly 365 days and 6 hours, so the leftover quarter days accumulate into one extra day every four years. A lunar year of twelve months is only about 354 days, which is why a purely lunar calendar drifts against the seasons.
    keyConcept: Revolution about the Sun = about 365 1/4 days (the year); rotation on the axis = about 24 hours (the day)
    examTip: Rotation gives day and night, revolution plus the tilted axis gives the seasons; do not trade the two periods.
    testedIn:
      - NTS
      - PPSC
  - id: 14
    question: Which subatomic particle carries no electric charge?
    options:
      - Electron
      - Proton
      - Positron
      - Neutron
    answer: 3
    explanation: The neutron, which sits in the nucleus beside the proton, is electrically neutral and has very nearly the same mass as a proton. The electron carries a negative charge and the positron is its anti-particle with a positive charge, each of magnitude about 1.6 x 10^-19 coulomb.
    keyConcept: Proton +1, electron minus 1, neutron 0; atomic number = number of protons, mass number = protons plus neutrons
    examTip: Isotopes of an element have the same proton number but different neutron counts, which changes the mass number only.
    testedIn:
      - PPSC
      - NTS
