## Why Geography is the Foundation of Pakistan Studies

In Pakistani competitive examinations (CSS, PMS, FPSC, PPSC, KPPSC), **Physical and Strategic Geography** accounts for 15% to 25% of general knowledge and Pakistan Affairs papers. Examiners repeatedly test precise measurements, border treaties, mountain pass elevations, river confluences, and hydro-infrastructure.

A candidate who masters the geographic contours of Pakistan can quickly secure direct objective marks and bring spatial depth to descriptive geopolitical essays.

Below is an authoritative reference detailing Pakistan's boundaries, mountain systems, strategic passes, and river networks.

---

### Section 1: International Borders & Strategic Boundary Lines

Pakistan spans an area of **881,913 square kilometers** (including Azad Jammu & Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan). It shares land borders with four sovereign nations and borders the Arabian Sea to the south:

| Neighboring Entity | Border Name / Status | Length | Key Treaty / Boundary Demarcation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Afghanistan** (North-West) | **Durand Line** | ~2,640 km | Signed on November 12, 1893, between Sir Mortimer Durand and Amir Abdur Rahman Khan |
| **India** (East & North-East) | **International Border / LoC / Working Boundary** | ~3,323 km total | Divided into: Radcliffe Line (Punjab/Sindh/Rajasthan), Line of Control (LoC - 740 km established under 1972 Simla Agreement), and Working Boundary (Punjab with Indian-administered Jammu) |
| **Iran** (South-West) | **Goldsmid Line / Pak-Iran Border** | ~909 km | Demarcated by the British-Persian Commission (1871–1896) under Sir Frederic Goldsmid; key crossing at **Taftan–Mirjaveh** and **Gabd–Rimdan** |
| **China** (North-East) | **Sino-Pakistan Boundary** | ~523 km | Formally delineated under the **Sino-Pakistan Agreement of March 2, 1963**, signed by Z.A. Bhutto and Chen Yi |
| **Arabian Sea** (South) | **Maritime Coastline** | ~1,046 km | Sindh coast (~330 km) and Balochistan/Makran coast (~770 km); Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) spans 290,000 sq km |

* **The Wakhan Corridor:** A narrow strip of Afghan territory (ranging from 16 to 65 km in width) that separates Pakistan’s north from Tajikistan and Central Asia.

### Section 2: Mountain Systems & Highest Peaks

Pakistan features the unique geological junction of three of the world’s greatest mountain systems: **The Himalayas, The Karakoram, and The Hindu Kush**.

```
Major Mountain Ranges of Pakistan:
├── The Karakoram Range: Home to K2 (8,611m) - World's 2nd highest peak
├── The Himalayas: Home to Nanga Parbat (8,126m) - World's 9th highest peak ("Killer Mountain")
├── The Hindu Kush: Home to Tirich Mir (7,708m) - Highest peak outside Karakoram/Himalayas
├── The Sulaiman Range: Home to Takht-e-Sulaiman (3,487m)
├── The Kirthar Range: Natural boundary between Sindh and Balochistan
└── The Salt Range: Ancient mineral formations; highest point at Sakesar (1,522m)
```

#### The Five 8,000+ Meter Giants in Pakistan
Out of the world’s 14 "Eight-Thousander" peaks, **5 are located in Pakistan** (all in Gilgit-Baltistan):
1. **K2 (Godwin-Austen / Chhogori):** 8,611 meters (Karakoram Range).
2. **Nanga Parbat (Diamir):** 8,126 meters (Western Himalayas).
3. **Gasherbrum I (Hidden Peak):** 8,080 meters (Karakoram Range).
4. **Broad Peak (K3):** 8,051 meters (Karakoram Range).
5. **Gasherbrum II (K4):** 8,035 meters (Karakoram Range).

### Section 3: High-Altitude Mountain Passes & Strategic Corridors

Mountain passes have shaped migration, trade, and military campaigns across the subcontinent for millennia. These passes are tested extensively in PPSC and FPSC examinations:

| Mountain Pass | Mountain Range | Links / Strategic Connection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Khunjerab Pass** | Karakoram Range | Links Pakistan (Gilgit-Baltistan) with China (Xinjiang); highest paved international border crossing in the world (4,693 meters) |
| **Khyber Pass** | Spīn Ghar (White Mountains) | Historic gateway linking Peshawar (Pakistan) with Jalalabad and Kabul (Afghanistan) |
| **Bolan Pass** | Toba Kakar / Central Brahui | Strategic pass connecting Sibi and Quetta with southern Afghanistan (Kandahar) |
| **Babusar Pass** | Great Himalayas | Connects Kaghan Valley (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) with Chilas (Gilgit-Baltistan) |
| **Lowari Pass** | Hindu Raj Range | Connects Dir with Chitral (bypassed by the Lowari Tunnel) |
| **Shandur Pass** | Hindu Kush / Karakoram | Links Chitral with Gilgit; renowned as the "Roof of the World" and venue of the annual Shandur Polo Festival (3,700m) |
| **Khojak Pass** | Toba Kakar Range | Connects Qila Abdullah (Chaman) with Kandahar (Afghanistan); features the historic Khojak Railway Tunnel |
| **Gomal Pass** | Sulaiman Range | Connects South Waziristan with Afghanistan |

### Section 4: The Indus River System & Hydrography

The **Indus River (Sindhu)** is Pakistan's longest river, originating in the Tibetan plateau near Lake Mansarovar. It flows roughly **3,180 kilometers** before emptying into the Arabian Sea near Thatta.

#### Confluences and Tributaries
* **Eastern Tributaries (The Punjab Rivers):**
  * **Jhelum River:** Originates from Verinag Spring in Jammu & Kashmir; Mangla Dam is built upon it.
  * **Chenab River:** Formed by the confluence of the Chandra and Bhaga rivers in Himachal Pradesh. Jhelum joins Chenab at **Trimmu**.
  * **Ravi River:** Originates in Himachal Pradesh near Rohtang Pass. Joins Chenab near **Ahmadpur Sial**.
  * **Sutlej River:** Originates near Lake Rakshastal in Tibet. Merges with the Beas in India, enters Pakistan near Kasur, and joins Chenab at **Uch Sharif** to form the **Panjnad** (Five Rivers).
  * **Panjnad Confluence:** The consolidated five rivers flow for 72 km before discharging into the Indus at **Mithankot**.
* **Western Tributaries:** Kabul River, Swat River, Kurram River, Gomal River, and Haro River.
* **The Indus Waters Treaty (1960):** Brokered by the World Bank. Pakistan received exclusive rights over the **three Western Rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab)**, while India was allocated the **three Eastern Rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej)**.

#### Major Barrages along the Indus River
From north to south: **Jinnah Barrage (Kalabagh) $\rightarrow$ Chashma Barrage $\rightarrow$ Taunsa Barrage $\rightarrow$ Guddu Barrage $\rightarrow$ Sukkur Barrage (oldest, built 1932) $\rightarrow$ Kotri Barrage (Jamshoro)**.

### Section 5: Deserts of Pakistan

Pakistan hosts four distinct desert ecosystems:
1. **Thar Desert:** The world's 18th largest desert, covering southeast Sindh (Tharparkar) and extending into India (Rajasthan). Rich in coal reserves.
2. **Cholistan Desert (Rohi):** Located in southern Punjab (Bahawalpur division); famous for Derawar Fort and nomadic culture.
3. **Thal Desert:** Located in the doab between the Indus and Jhelum rivers in Punjab (Bhakkar, Layyah, Mianwali).
4. **Kharan Desert:** A cold, hyper-arid sandy desert situated in Balochistan; site of Pakistan's 1998 nuclear test (Chagai-II).
