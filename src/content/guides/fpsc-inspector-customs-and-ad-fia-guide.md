## Overview of Premier Federal Executive Posts

Among the thousands of vacancies advertised annually by the **Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC)** under Consolidated Advertisements, two posts consistently attract the highest number of applicants:
1. **Inspector Customs / Intelligence Officer / Preventive Officer (BS-16)** under the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR).
2. **Assistant Director (AD) FIA (BS-17)** and **Inspector FIA (BS-16)** under the Federal Investigation Agency, Ministry of Interior.

Both cadres offer exceptional administrative authority, nationwide postings, and dynamic investigative mandates. However, securing these positions requires navigating an intensely competitive multi-phase screening process where qualifying merit cutoffs routinely exceed **78 to 84 marks out of 100**.

---

FPSC administers a standardized **100-mark objective MCQ test (100 minutes)** divided into two mandatory parts:

### Mastering Cadre-Specific Legislation (The Scoring Decider)

The 15-mark statutory law section is the single most critical scoring component. While general candidates score similarly across Pakistan Affairs and Islam, mastering the bare acts guarantees you top-tier marks.

---

### A. For Inspector Customs: The Customs Act, 1969 (IV of 1969)

Customs screening tests draw questions directly from the statutory language of **The Customs Act, 1969**. Focus on these high-yield chapters and sections:

| Chapter / Section | Statutory Subject Matter | Key Exam-Tested Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 2** | **Definitions** | Legal definitions of *Smuggling*, *Goods*, *Conveyance*, *Customs Area*, *Customs Station*, and *Dutiable Goods* |
| **Section 3 & 4** | **Appointment of Officers** | Hierarchy: Chief Collector $\rightarrow$ Collector $\rightarrow$ Additional Collector $\rightarrow$ Deputy Collector $\rightarrow$ Assistant Collector $\rightarrow$ Superintendent / Principal Appraiser $\rightarrow$ Inspector / Preventive Officer |
| **Section 18 & 19** | **Levy of Customs Duties & Exemptions** | Powers of the Federal Government to exempt goods from customs duty or impose regulatory duties |
| **Section 79 & 80** | **Goods Declaration (GD) & Clearance** | Filing of Goods Declaration by importer/clearing agent, self-assessment mechanisms, and physical examination |
| **Section 99–119** | **Warehousing** | Public vs. private bonded warehouses, maximum warehousing retention period, and bond security rules |
| **Section 158–172** | **Search, Seizure & Arrest Powers** | Powers of Customs Officers to stop conveyances, search individuals, enter premises with or without a warrant (Section 163), and seize smuggled goods |
| **Section 156** | **Penalties Schedule** | Comprehensive schedule of offenses, fines, and imprisonment terms for contraband smuggling and duty evasion |
| **Section 179–194** | **Adjudication & Appeals** | Monetary adjudication limits of officers, appeals to Collector (Appeals), and Appellate Tribunal jurisdiction |

### B. For Assistant Director & Inspector FIA: The FIA Act, 1974 (VIII of 1975)

The **Federal Investigation Agency Act, 1974** is a concise statute consisting of 10 core sections. Candidates must study every section verbatim:

* **Section 3 (Constitution of the Agency):** The FIA is constituted by the Federal Government to investigate and inquire into offenses specified in the Schedule to the Act.
* **Section 4 (Superintendence and Administration):**
  * Superintendence of the Agency vests in the **Federal Government** (Ministry of Interior).
  * General administration vests in the **Director General (DG) FIA**, appointed by the Federal Government, exercising powers equivalent to an Inspector-General of Police (IGP).
* **Section 5 (Powers of the Members of the Agency):**
  * Throughout Pakistan, members of the FIA possess all the powers, privileges, and liabilities that police officers have in connection with the investigation of offenses under the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1898**.
  * Any officer of the Agency of or above the rank of **Sub-Inspector** may exercise any of the powers of an Officer-in-Charge of a Police Station (*SHO*).
  * Powers of arrest, search, and seizure without warrant in respect of scheduled offenses.
* **The Schedule to the FIA Act:**
  * Understand which offenses fall exclusively under FIA jurisdiction: offenses against the state, counterfeit currency and government stamps, official secrets, emigration and passport violations, anti-corruption against federal employees, cybercrime, and money laundering.

#### Supplementary FIA Legislation to Review:
* **PECA 2016 (Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act):** Cyber terrorism, electronic forgery, unauthorized access to critical infrastructure, cyber harassment, and child online exploitation (handled by FIA Cyber Crime Wing).
* **Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010 (AMLA):** Scheduled offenses, Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU) reporting, and freezing of illicit assets.

### Part 2 Quantitative Ability Breakdown (20 Marks)

FPSC quantitative questions require swift manual computation without calculators. Master these 5 high-yield arithmetic archetypes:
1. **Ratio and Proportion:** Dividing prize pools or corporate capital among partners based on fractional shares.
2. **Time, Speed and Distance:** Relative speed problems (two trains traveling in opposite directions) and average speed across two journeys ($\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$).
3. **Percentage and Markup:** Calculating original cost price from retail discount percentages and sales tax inclusions.
4. **Age Problems:** Formulating linear equations based on family age relationships (*"Father is 3 times older than his son; in 12 years he will be twice his age"*).
5. **Work and Pipe Cisterns:** Combining filling rates of inlet pipes and emptying rates of leakage drains.

### The Descriptive Exam Stage (For BS-17 Assistant Director FIA)

Unlike BS-16 posts which conclude with the screening test and interview, **BS-17 appointments (such as AD FIA) require candidates in the top merit bracket (typically 1:10 ratio) to sit for a written Descriptive Examination (200 Marks)**:
* **Paper 1: English Essay (50 Marks) & English Précis and Composition (50 Marks).**
* **Paper 2: General Studies (100 Marks)** covering Pakistan Affairs, Current Affairs, Everyday Science, and basic criminal law principles.

Preparing deeply for your objective screening test lays the factual foundation for both your screening merit and your subsequent descriptive written papers.
