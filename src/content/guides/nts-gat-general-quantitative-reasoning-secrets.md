## Conquering the Quantitative Section in NTS GAT & HEC HAT

The **Graduate Assessment Test (GAT General)** administered by **NTS**—alongside the **HEC Higher Education Aptitude Test (HAT)**—is mandatory for admissions into MS/M.Phil programs and for competing for HEC indigenous and overseas postgraduate scholarships.

In GAT General (Categories A, B, and C), the **Quantitative Reasoning section comprises 30% to 35% of the total paper (30 to 35 MCQs)**. Because candidates are strictly prohibited from using calculators in the test center, success depends on mental math shortcuts, identifying underlying problem archetypes, and managing time efficiently.

Below is our comprehensive compendium of formulas, shortcuts, and high-frequency problem archetypes tested in NTS examinations.

---

### Section 1: Arithmetic & Speed Calculation Shortcuts

#### 1. Percentage Change & Successive Percentages
* **Direct Percentage Formula:**
  $$\text{Value} = \text{Base} \times \left(\frac{\text{Percentage}}{100}\right)$$
* **Successive Percentage Changes:**
  If a value increases by $a\%$ and subsequently increases by $b\%$, the net percentage change is given by:
  $$\text{Net Percentage Change} = a + b + \frac{a \times b}{100}$$
  * *Example:* If an item's price increases by $20\%$ and then drops by $10\%$ ($a = +20, b = -10$):
    $$\text{Net Change} = 20 - 10 + \frac{20 \times (-10)}{100} = 10 - 2 = +8\% \text{ net increase}$$

#### 2. Ratio, Proportion & Investment Partnerships
* **Proportional Allocation:** If a sum of money $S$ is divided among three partners in the ratio $a : b : c$:
  $$\text{Share of Partner } A = S \times \left(\frac{a}{a + b + c}\right)$$
* **Inverse Proportion (Work-Men Relationship):**
  If 12 workers can construct a boundary wall in 8 days, how many days will 16 workers take?
  $$M_1 \times D_1 = M_2 \times D_2 \implies 12 \times 8 = 16 \times D_2 \implies D_2 = \frac{96}{16} = 6 \text{ days}$$

#### 3. Average Speed (The Harmonic Mean Trap)
* **The Trap:** If a vehicle travels from city A to city B at $40\text{ km/h}$ and returns along the same route at $60\text{ km/h}$, most candidates mistakenly calculate the simple arithmetic mean $\frac{40+60}{2} = 50\text{ km/h}$. This is incorrect.
* **The Correct Formula:**
  $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2 \times v_1 \times v_2}{v_1 + v_2} = \frac{2 \times 40 \times 60}{40 + 60} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48 \text{ km/h}$$

### Section 2: High-Frequency Problem Archetypes in NTS

#### Archetype 1: Combined Work & Pipe Cistern Problems
* **The Standard Work Formula:** If person A completes a job in $x$ days and person B completes it in $y$ days, their combined daily work output is $\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y}$. Together, they will complete the job in:
  $$\text{Combined Time} = \frac{x \times y}{x + y} \text{ days}$$
* **Pipe & Leakage Cisterns:** If an inlet pipe fills a tank in 4 hours and a drain empties it in 6 hours:
  $$\text{Net Rate} = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3 - 2}{12} = \frac{1}{12} \implies 12 \text{ hours to fill}$$

#### Archetype 2: Age Word Problems
* **Systematic Setup:** Always define the **present age** as $x$ and $y$.
* *Problem Statement:* "A father is currently four times older than his son. In 20 years, the father will be twice as old as his son. What is the son's present age?"
  * Present: $F = 4S$
  * In 20 years: $F + 20 = 2(S + 20)$
  * Substitute $F = 4S$:
    $$4S + 20 = 2S + 40 \implies 2S = 20 \implies S = 10 \text{ years}$$

#### Archetype 3: Distance, Relative Speed & Train Crossings
* **Relative Speed Rule:**
  * Two objects moving in **opposite directions** (approaching each other): add speeds ($v_{\text{rel}} = v_1 + v_2$).
  * Two objects moving in the **same direction** (overtaking): subtract speeds ($v_{\text{rel}} = v_1 - v_2$).
* **Crossing a Stationary Platform:** Distance = Length of Train + Length of Platform.
* **Speed Unit Conversion Benchmark:**
  $$\text{To convert km/h to m/s: multiply by } \frac{5}{18}$$
  $$\text{To convert m/s to km/h: multiply by } \frac{18}{5}$$

### Section 3: Essential Geometry & Coordinate Principles

NTS tests basic geometric formulas rather than complex formal proofs:

| Geometric Figure | Perimeter / Circumference | Area Formula | Volume Formula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rectangle** | $2(l + w)$ | $l \times w$ | — |
| **Right-Angled Triangle** | $a + b + c$ | $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$ | — |
| **Circle** | $2\pi r$ | $\pi r^2$ | — |
| **Cylinder** | — | Total Surface Area $= 2\pi r(r + h)$ | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| **Sphere** | — | Surface Area $= 4\pi r^2$ | $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |

* **The Pythagorean Triplets to Memorize:**
  Instead of manually computing $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, memorize recurring right-triangle integer triplets:
  * $(3, 4, 5)$ and its multiples $(6, 8, 10), (9, 12, 15)$
  * $(5, 12, 13)$
  * $(8, 15, 17)$
  * $(7, 24, 25)$

### Test-Day Pacing & Tactical Rules

1. **The 60-Second Rule:** GAT General allocates roughly **72 seconds per question** across 100 questions. If a math calculation takes longer than 60 seconds without a clear path to the solution, mark a tentative guess, place a circle on your question paper, and continue.
2. **Back-Solve from Options:** When algebraic equations seem complex, test the four multiple-choice options directly. Start by testing **Option B or C** (the middle values). If it's too large or too small, you instantly eliminate two options in one step.
