## The Core Pillar of Pakistani Competitive Testing

In every major examination across Pakistan—from the Federal Civil Superior Services (CSS) compulsory **Pakistan Affairs** paper to the 100-mark screening tests administered by **PPSC**, **FPSC**, **KPPSC**, and **SPSC**—**Pakistan Studies and Constitutional History** is the foundational academic discipline.

Examiners test candidates on historical chronology, constitutional law, institutional milestones, and geopolitical decision-making. Aspirants often struggle when they view history as a collection of disconnected dates. To achieve high scores, you must understand the **cause-and-effect constitutional sequence** that shaped the modern state.

Below is our comprehensive chronological roadmap spanning the four defining eras of Pakistan’s history.

---

### Era 1: The Freedom Movement & Political Evolution (1857–1947)

Mastering pre-partition history requires studying the sequence of constitutional reforms and political negotiations:

```
Chronological Sequence of Key Pre-Partition Milestones:
1857: The War of Independence (End of British East India Company; direct Crown rule begins)
1867: Urdu-Hindi Controversy in Benares (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan articulates Two-Nation basis)
1875: Founding of Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) High School at Aligarh (College in 1877)
1905: Partition of Bengal by Lord Curzon (Creation of Muslim-majority Eastern Bengal & Assam)
1906 (Oct 1): Simla Deputation led by Sir Aga Khan presents demands to Viceroy Lord Minto
1906 (Dec 30): All-India Muslim League founded in Dhaka (Nawab Salimullah, Viqar-ul-Mulk)
1909: Minto-Morley Reforms (Indian Councils Act) officially grants Separate Electorates
1911: Annulment of the Partition of Bengal by King George V at Delhi Durbar
1913: Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah formally joins the All-India Muslim League
1916: The Lucknow Pact (Joint Congress-League accord recognizing separate electorates)
1919: Montague-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act 1919 introduces Provincial Dyarchy)
1919–1924: The Khilafat Movement led by the Ali Brothers (Maulana Muhammad Ali & Shaukat Ali)
1927: Simon Commission appointed (All-white statutory commission boycotted by major parties)
1928: The Nehru Report (Rejects separate electorates and federal autonomy for Muslims)
1929: Jinnah's Fourteen Points (Demands federal constitution, 1/3rd central representation, Sindh separation)
1930: Allama Iqbal's Historic Allahabad Address (Articulates vision for a north-western Muslim state)
1930–1932: Round Table Conferences convened in London
1932: Communal Award announced by British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald
1933: "Now or Never" pamphlet published in Cambridge by Choudhry Rahmat Ali coining the name "Pakistan"
1935: The Government of India Act, 1935 (Introduces Provincial Autonomy; serves as interim constitution)
1937: Provincial Elections; Congress forms ministries in 7 provinces; Muslim grievances emerge
1939 (Dec 22): "Day of Deliverance" observed by Muslims upon resignation of Congress ministries
1940 (March 23): Lahore Resolution (Pakistan Resolution) moved by A.K. Fazlul Huq at Minto Park
1942: The Cripps Mission (Offers post-war dominion status; rejected by Congress and League)
1944: Gandhi-Jinnah Talks based on the C.R. Formula (Conclude without agreement)
1945: The Simla Conference convened by Viceroy Lord Wavell
1945–1946: General Elections (Muslim League wins 100% central Muslim seats and 87% provincial Muslim seats)
1946: The Cabinet Mission Plan (Proposes three-tier federation; League initially accepts, then withdraws)
1946 (Aug 16): Direct Action Day observed by the Muslim League
1947 (June 3): 3rd June Plan (Mountbatten Plan) announces partition and transfer of power
1947 (July 18): Indian Independence Act, 1947 enacted by British Parliament
1947 (Aug 14): Pakistan emerges as an independent sovereign dominion (Quaid-e-Azam takes oath as 1st Governor-General)
```

### Era 2: Early State Consolidation & Constitution-Making (1947–1958)

The first decade of independence was marked by severe administrative challenges and constitution-making struggles:
* **The Radcliffe Award (August 17, 1947):** Contested demarcation of Punjab and Bengal boundaries, including the controversial allocation of the Muslim-majority tehsil of Gurdaspur to India, providing India access to Jammu & Kashmir.
* **The Objectives Resolution (March 12, 1949):** Moved by Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan. Established that sovereignty over the entire universe belongs to Allah Almighty alone, and the authority to be exercised by the people of Pakistan is a sacred trust. Later made a substantive part of the 1973 Constitution via **Article 2A**.
* **Basic Principles Committee (BPC) Reports:** Multiple drafts debated between 1950 and 1953 addressing parity between East and West Pakistan and religious minority safeguards.
* **The One-Unit Scheme (1955):** Merged the four western provinces and princely states into a single unified province ("West Pakistan") to establish legislative parity with East Pakistan.
* **The 1956 Constitution (Enacted March 23, 1956):** Pakistan’s first constitution. Declared Pakistan an "Islamic Republic", established a unicameral legislature (300 seats with 50-50 parity between East and West Pakistan), and parliamentary executive governance. Abrogated on October 7, 1958, by President Iskander Mirza under martial law.

### Era 3: The 1962 Constitution & The 1971 Crisis (1958–1971)

* **The 1962 Constitution (Enacted June 8, 1962):** Promulgated by Field Marshal Ayub Khan based on the Shahabuddin Commission recommendations. Established a strong **Presidential system**, unicameral legislature, and an electoral college of **80,000 Basic Democrats**.
* **The Indus Waters Treaty (September 19, 1960):** Brokered by the World Bank; signed by President Ayub Khan and Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru in Karachi.
* **The 1965 War & Tashkent Declaration (January 10, 1966):** Mediated by Soviet Premier Alexei Kosygin between Ayub Khan and Lal Bahadur Shastri.
* **The Legal Framework Order (LFO) 1970:** Issued by General Yahya Khan. Dissolved the One-Unit scheme, restored the four historical provinces of West Pakistan, abolished the parity principle, and instituted direct adult franchise based on population.
* **The December 1970 Elections:** Awami League led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman won 160 out of 162 seats in East Pakistan; Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) led by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto won 81 out of 138 seats in West Pakistan. Political deadlock culminated in the tragic events of December 1971 and the emergence of Bangladesh.

### Era 4: The 1973 Constitution & Modern Democratic Evolution

Enacted on **August 14, 1973**, the current Constitution of Pakistan represents an authentic democratic consensus among all political parties:

#### Key Structural Features of the 1973 Constitution
1. **Federal Parliamentary System:** Executive authority vests in the Prime Minister; the President acts as the ceremonial Head of State.
2. **Bicameral Legislature (Majlis-e-Shoora):**
   * **National Assembly:** Represents the population through direct adult franchise.
   * **Senate:** Represents the federating units equally, ensuring provincial equality regardless of demographic size.
3. **Fundamental Rights (Articles 8 to 28):** Inviolable guarantees covering life, liberty, fair trial (Article 10A), freedom of speech, freedom of association, and non-discrimination.
4. **Islamic Provisions:** Islam declared the State Religion (Article 2); laws brought in conformity with the Injunctions of Islam (Council of Islamic Ideology - Articles 227–231).

#### High-Yield Constitutional Amendments Tested by Commissions

| Amendment | Year | Core Legal & Political Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **8th Amendment** | 1985 | Introduced **Article 58(2)(b)**, granting the President discretionary power to dissolve the National Assembly |
| **13th Amendment** | 1997 | Stripped the President of Article 58(2)(b) powers; restored parliamentary primacy under Nawaz Sharif |
| **17th Amendment** | 2003 | Re-introduced Article 58(2)(b) under General Pervez Musharraf following the Legal Framework Order 2002 |
| **18th Amendment** | **2010** | **The most significant constitutional reform in modern history:** Abolished the Concurrent Legislative List, devolved 17 federal ministries to the provinces, revoked Article 58(2)(b), made the right to education a fundamental right (Article 25A), and reformed judicial appointments (Article 175A) |
| **19th & 21st Amendments** | 2011 / 2015 | Refined Judicial Commission composition (19th); established military courts for terrorism trials under the National Action Plan (21st) |
| **25th Amendment** | **2018** | **Historic FATA Merger:** Merged the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) and Provincially Administered Tribal Areas (PATA) into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, abolishing the colonial Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) |

* **Understand the Counter-Argument:** In descriptive CSS and PMS papers, avoid one-dimensional historical narratives. Acknowledge competing historiographical perspectives to demonstrate mature academic analysis.
