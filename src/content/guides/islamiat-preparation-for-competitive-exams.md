## The Academic Scope of Islamic Studies in Competitive Exams

In Pakistani competitive examinations, **Islamic Studies** holds a unique status. It is a mandatory 100-mark compulsory descriptive subject in both the **CSS Competitive Examination** and the **Punjab Provincial Management Service (PMS)**, while simultaneously accounting for 10% to 15% of objective marks in **PPSC**, **FPSC**, and **NTS** screening examinations.

Examiners do not evaluate subjective emotional devotion; rather, they assess **factual chronological accuracy, understanding of governance institutions, knowledge of primary Islamic legal sources, and the ability to articulate Islamic ethical solutions to modern socio-political dilemmas**.

Below is our comprehensive academic roadmap organized by core thematic modules.

---

### Module 1: Chronological Milestones of Seerat-un-Nabi (PBUH)

Competitive exams test specific dates, companion names, and treaty clauses across the Makkan and Madinan epochs:

#### A. The Makkan Period (570 CE – 622 CE)
* **Birth & Early Life:** Born in the Year of the Elephant (*Aam-ul-Feel* - 570 CE) in Mecca. Foster mother: Hazrat Halima Saadia (RA). Received titles *Al-Amin* (The Trustworthy) and *Al-Sadiq* (The Truthful).
* **Hilf-al-Fudul (Pact of the Virtuous):** Early pre-Islamic peace alliance established to protect travelers, orphans, and the oppressed in Mecca; actively endorsed by the Holy Prophet (PBUH).
* **First Revelation (610 CE / 40th Year of Age):** Cave Hira; Surah Al-Alaq verses 1–5 revealed through Angel Jibreel (AS).
* **Early Converts:** First woman: Hazrat Khadija (RA); first adult male: Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA); first child: Hazrat Ali (RA); first freed slave: Hazrat Zayd ibn Haritha (RA).
* **Social Boycott of Banu Hashim (7th to 10th Year of Nabawi):** Three years of severe economic confinement in *Shib Abi Talib*.
* **Year of Sorrow (*Aam-ul-Huzn* - 10th Year of Nabawi):** Passing of Hazrat Khadija (RA) and beloved protector uncle Abu Talib. Journey to Ta'if.
* **Al-Isra wal-Miraj (10th / 11th Year of Nabawi):** The miraculous night journey from the Sacred Mosque in Mecca to Masjid Al-Aqsa, followed by the celestial ascension; the **five daily prayers (Salah)** were instituted.
* **Pledges of Aqabah (11th and 12th Year of Nabawi):** The First and Second Pledges by the tribes of Yathrib (Aus and Khazraj), laying the security foundation for the migration (*Hijrah*) to Medina in 622 CE.

#### B. The Madinan Period (1 AH – 11 AH)
* **1 AH (622 CE):** Arrival in Quba and Medina. Construction of Masjid-e-Nabawi. Establishment of *Mu'akhah* (Fraternity bond between Muhajirun and Ansar).
* **Charter of Madinah (*Misaq-e-Madinah*):** The world's first written democratic constitution. Guaranteed freedom of religious practice to Jewish tribes, established a mutual defense obligation against external aggressors, and designated the Holy Prophet (PBUH) as the supreme judicial arbiter.
* **2 AH (624 CE):** 
  * Change of Qibla (*Tahweel-e-Qibla*) from Jerusalem to the Holy Kaaba in Mecca (Surah Al-Baqarah).
  * Fasting in Ramadan (*Sawm*) and Zakat mandated.
  * **The Battle of Badr (17th Ramadan 2 AH):** The first decisive confrontation; 313 ill-equipped Muslims defeated a coalition of 1,000 Quraysh warriors.
* **3 AH (625 CE):** **The Battle of Uhud.** Initial Muslim advantage reversed due to the abandonment of the strategic archers' pass on Mount Rumah.
* **5 AH (627 CE):** **The Battle of the Trench (*Ghazwa-e-Khandaq / Ahzab*).** Trench strategy suggested by Hazrat Salman Farsi (RA).
* **6 AH (628 CE):** **Treaty of Hudaibiyah (*Sulh al-Hudaibiyah*).** Signed between Muslims and Suhayl ibn Amr representing Quraysh; declared a "manifest victory" (*Fathan Mubeena*) in Surah Al-Fath.
* **7 AH (629 CE):** **The Battle of Khyber.** Conquest of the fortified Jewish citadels in Khyber led by Hazrat Ali (RA) (*Khyber-Shikan*).
* **8 AH (630 CE):** 
  * Battle of Mu'tah.
  * **The Bloodless Conquest of Mecca (*Fath-e-Makkah* - 20th Ramadan 8 AH):** Declaration of general amnesty; cleansing of 360 idols from the Kaaba.
  * Battle of Hunayn against the Hawazin and Thaqif confederations.
* **9 AH (631 CE):** Expedition to Tabuk; year of delegations (*Aam-ul-Wufood*).
* **10 AH (632 CE):** **The Farewell Pilgrimage (*Hajjat-ul-Wida*).** Delivery of the monumental universal human rights sermon at the Plain of Arafat.

### Module 2: The Compilation & Preservation of the Holy Quran

The Holy Quran was revealed incrementally over a span of approximately **23 years** (13 years in Mecca, 10 years in Medina):
* **Structure:** 114 Surahs, 6,666 verses (standard classical count), 30 Juz (Paras), 7 Manzils.
* **Makki vs. Madani Surahs:** 86 Makki Surahs (focusing on Tawheed, Akhirah, moral fortitude) and 28 Madani Surahs (focusing on legislation, societal governance, civil codes, and international relations).
* **First Phase of Compilation (Era of Hazrat Abu Bakr RA - 11–13 AH):** Following the Battle of Yamama where dozens of Huffaz (Quran memorizers) were martyred, Hazrat Umar (RA) proposed compiling the dispersed parchments into a single book (*Suhuf*). A commission led by **Hazrat Zayd ibn Thabit (RA)** executed the task with rigorous dual-witness verification.
* **Second Phase of Standardization (Era of Hazrat Usman RA - 24–35 AH):** To prevent pronunciation discrepancies across non-Arab conquests, Hazrat Usman (RA) obtained the authentic master copy from Hazrat Hafsa (RA), commissioned official transcripts in the standard Quraysh dialect, and distributed them to regional administrative capitals (*Jami-ul-Quran*).

### Module 3: Governance Model of the Khilafat-e-Rashida (11–40 AH)

Examiners in CSS and PMS frequently ask candidates to analyze the administrative structures of the Righteous Caliphate:
1. **Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddiq (RA) (11–13 AH):** Eradication of false prophets (Musaylimah al-Kadhdhab) and suppression of the apostasy rebellions (*Fitna-e-Irtidad*).
2. **Hazrat Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) (13–23 AH):** 
   * Established the consultative assembly (*Majlis-e-Shoora*).
   * Created the central treasury (*Bait-ul-Maal*) and the military pension register (*Diwan*).
   * Established independent judiciary courts (*Qazi* system separated from executive governors).
   * Instituted the police watch (*Sahib-ul-Ahdath*) and the Hijri calendar (beginning 622 CE).
3. **Hazrat Usman ibn Affan (RA) (23–35 AH):** Established the first Islamic maritime naval fleet under Amir Muawiyah. Expanded state territorial frontiers into North Africa, Central Asia, and Cyprus.
4. **Hazrat Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) (35–40 AH):** Championed judicial impartiality, administrative accountability of provincial tax collectors, and strict protection of minority subjects (*Dhimmi*).

### Module 4: Sources of Islamic Law (Usul-al-Fiqh)

Candidates must understand the hierarchy of Islamic jurisprudence:
1. **The Holy Quran:** The primary, immutable divine source of guidance and legal injunctions (*Nass*).
2. **The Sunnah / Hadith:** The authentic sayings, actions, and approvals (*Taqreer*) of the Holy Prophet (PBUH) which interpret and detail Quranic principles.
3. **Ijma (Consensus of Jurists):** The unanimous agreement of qualified Islamic legal scholars of a particular era upon a juristic ruling.
4. **Qiyas (Analogical Deduction):** Deriving an answer for a novel situation by comparing it to an established ruling sharing an identical underlying effective cause (*Illah*).
5. **Ijtihad:** The disciplined intellectual exertion of a qualified jurist (*Mujtahid*) to derive solutions for contemporary dilemmas within Islamic principles.

### Tactical Advice for the Examination Hall

* **Cite Authoritative Surah & Hadith References:** In descriptive papers, every major argument regarding human rights, economic justice, or women's rights should be supported by an authentic Quranic verse reference or verified Hadith citation.
* **Draw Institutional Parallels:** When answering questions on the Khilafat-e-Rashida, connect historical governance practices with modern constitutional principles (rule of law, welfare state, separation of powers).
