## The Administrative Role of Tehsildar & Naib Tehsildar

In the administrative machinery of Punjab, the positions of **Tehsildar (BPS-16)** and **Naib Tehsildar (BPS-14)** within the Punjab Revenue Department are among the most influential and coveted public service appointments. 

A Tehsildar operates as the chief executive officer of a tehsil for land administration, exercising three distinct types of authority:
1. **Executive Revenue Powers:** Collection of agricultural income tax, water rates (Abyana), and government dues.
2. **Assistant Collector Grade I Powers:** Attestation of contested land mutations (*Dakhil Kharij*), partition of agricultural land (*Taqseem*), and correction of revenue records (*Fard Badr*).
3. **Sub-Divisional Magistrate / Special Judicial Powers:** Maintenance of public order, administrative inspections, and executive magistracy under specific local statutes.

Because of the vast prestige and administrative authority attached to these roles, the **Punjab Public Service Commission (PPSC)** conducts an intensive multi-paper recruitment process.

---

### The Two-Paper Examination Pattern

Unlike single-paper general recruitment tests, the Tehsildar examination consists of **two independent 100-mark objective MCQ papers**, conducted either on the same day or over a single weekend.

```
PPSC Tehsildar Written Examination (Total: 200 Marks)
├── Paper 1: English Comprehension & Composition (100 Marks | 90 Mins)
└── Paper 2: General Knowledge & Revenue Laws (100 Marks | 90 Mins)
     ├── Part A: General Knowledge, Pakistan Studies, Islam, IT (70%)
     └── Part B: Punjab Land Revenue Act 1967 & Tenancy Laws (30%)
```

Both papers carry a **0.25 negative marking penalty** per wrong answer. To qualify for the interview stage, candidates must pass both papers individually (minimum 40% in each) and secure an aggregate score that places them within the merit cutoff (historically **140 to 155 marks out of 200**).

---

### Paper 1: English Comprehension & Composition (100 Marks)

Paper 1 tests grammatical competence, contextual vocabulary, syntax, and sentence mechanics. Examiners avoid obscure archaic terms in favor of practical administrative English.

#### Topic-Wise Distribution in Paper 1
* **Prepositions & Phrasal Verbs (20–25 Marks):** Tests fixed prepositions (*abstain from, averse to, adhere to, comply with*) and phrasal verb meanings (*break down, call off, put up with*).
* **Direct & Indirect Speech & Voice (15–20 Marks):** Active/Passive conversions and reported speech involving imperatives, exclamations, and interrogatives.
* **Sentence Correction & Syntax (15–20 Marks):** Subject-verb agreement, misplaced modifiers, conditional sentences (Types 1, 2, and 3), and parallel structure.
* **Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms (15–20 Marks):** Standard high-frequency vocabulary drawn from past papers and competitive word lists.
* **Reading Comprehension & Analogies (15–20 Marks):** Short passages testing inference, main ideas, tone, and relationship analogies (*Pencil : Graphite :: Pen : Ink*).

---

### Paper 2: General Knowledge & Land Revenue Legislation (100 Marks)

Paper 2 is divided into two distinct components:

#### Part A: General Ability & Pakistan Affairs (70 Marks)
* **Pakistan Studies & History (20 Marks):** Freedom movement (1857–1947), constitutional milestones (1973 Constitution Articles and Amendments), geography, canals, and barrages.
* **Islamic Studies (15 Marks):** Pillars of Islam, Seerat-un-Nabi, battles, and Quranic chronology.
* **Everyday Science & Arithmetic (15 Marks):** Human physiology, planetary facts, vitamins, SI units, basic percentages, and ratios.
* **Computer Skills & IT (10 Marks):** Microsoft Excel (VLOOKUP, formulas, cell referencing), MS Word formatting shortcuts, networking, and digital land records (*Arazi Record Centers*).
* **Current Affairs (10 Marks):** Major international treaties, bilateral visits, national budget allocations, and key institutional appointments.

### Part B: The Punjab Land Revenue Act 1967 (30 Marks)

This is the decisive section where competitive differentiation occurs. General candidates who rely solely on conventional GK guides lose critical marks here. To score 25+ out of 30 in this section, you must study the original statutory provisions of **The Punjab Land Revenue Act (XVII of 1967)**.

#### High-Yield Sections & Core Revenue Terminology

| Statutory Section | Subject Matter | Key Details Tested by PPSC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 7** | **Classes of Revenue Officers** | Hierarchy: Board of Revenue $\rightarrow$ Commissioner $\rightarrow$ Collector $\rightarrow$ Assistant Collector Grade I $\rightarrow$ Assistant Collector Grade II |
| **Section 39–41** | **Record of Rights (*Misal-e-Haqiyat*)** | Contains genealogy of owners (*Shajra Nasab*), register of holdings (*Jamabandi*), rights in wells/canals, and village customs (*Wajib-ul-Arz*) |
| **Section 42** | **Procedure for Mutation (*Dakhil Kharij*)** | Obligation of Patwari to record transactions in the register of mutations within 3 months; attestation conducted in common assembly (*Jalsa-e-Aam*) |
| **Section 52** | **Presumption of Truth** | Entries in the Record of Rights and Periodical Records carry legal presumption of truth in courts of law until disproven |
| **Section 56–73** | **Assessment of Land Revenue** | Distinction between general and special assessment; maximum assessment limitation (cannot exceed 25% of net assets) |
| **Section 79–91** | **Recovery of Arrears** | 7 processes for recovering arrears: Notice of Demand (Sec 81), Arrest and Detention (Sec 82), Distress and Sale of Movables (Sec 83), Transfer of Holding (Sec 84), Attachment of Estate (Sec 85) |
| **Section 117–122** | **Demarcation of Boundaries (*Hadd-Shikani*)** | Power of Revenue Officers to settle boundary disputes and penalize destruction of survey boundary pillars |
| **Section 135–150** | **Partition of Land (*Taqseem*)** | Who can apply for partition; distinction between agricultural land and village abadi (*Gora Deh*); exclusion of public grazing grounds |
| **Section 161–164** | **Appeals, Review & Revision** | Limitation period: 30 days to Collector, 60 days to Commissioner, 90 days to Board of Revenue; supervisory powers of BoR |

#### Traditional Revenue Vocabulary to Memorize
* **Khasra:** An independent plot or parcel of land assigned a distinct survey identification number.
* **Khewat:** An account number representing one or more landowners holding shared proprietary rights in a revenue estate (*Mauza*).
* **Khatooni:** An operational cultivator's holding ledger detailing tenancy and possession rights under a specific Khewat.
* **Girdawari:** Bi-annual harvest crop inspection carried out by the Patwari (Kharif inspection in October; Rabi inspection in March).
* **Lal Kitab (Village Note Book):** The structural repository containing historical records of agricultural statistics, area yields, and land transfers for every village.

### Additional Statutory Coverage: The Punjab Tenancy Act 1887

Candidates must also master key principles of the **Punjab Tenancy Act (XVI of 1887)**:
* **Types of Tenants:** Occupancy Tenants (*Muzareen-e-Mustaqil*) versus Tenants-at-Will (*Ghair-Muzareen*).
* **Batai System:** Rules governing the division of produce between landlord and tenant, and statutory restrictions against arbitrary enhancement of rent or unlawful ejectment (*Bedakhli*).

### Preparation Strategy & Recommended Reading

1. **Bare Act Reading:** Download the original PDF text of *The Punjab Land Revenue Act 1967* as amended up to date. Read Sections 1 through 184 at least three times, making one-line summaries of powers and limitations.
2. **Computerized Land Records (PLRA):** Familiarize yourself with the workflow of the *Punjab Land Records Authority (PLRA)*, including *Fard issuance*, biometric verification, and digital mutations through Arazi Record Centers (ARCs).
