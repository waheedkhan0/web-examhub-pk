## Official Context: Why the Précis Paper Decides CSS Success

The **English Précis & Composition** paper (100 Marks) is statistically the single greatest hurdle for aspirants sitting for the Federal Civil Superior Services (CSS) and Provincial Management Services (PMS) competitive examinations. According to annual Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC) examination reports, over **70% to 80% of candidates fail this compulsory subject**.

The examiner's reports consistently emphasize that failure is rarely caused by a candidate's lack of vocabulary; rather, it is driven by systemic structural errors, inability to identify core arguments, verbatim borrowing, and flawed syntactic construction. 

Below is an exhaustive breakdown of the **Top 10 most common mistakes** cited by FPSC examiners, accompanied by pedagogical solutions and concrete examples to help you secure a qualifying score.

---

### Mistake 1: Directly Borrowing Phrases from the Original Text
The single most frequent comment in FPSC reports is: *"Candidates demonstrated an acute inability to express the author's ideas in their own words, resorting to verbatim lifting of whole clauses."*

* **The Trap:** Candidates copy key sentences from the beginning, middle, and end of the original passage, stringing them together with transitional words like *moreover* and *furthermore*.
* **The Examiner's Assessment:** Zero marks for originality. The examiner perceives this as a failure of comprehension.
* **The Correct Technique:** Read the passage three times until you grasp the central premise. Turn the original text face down and write a rough draft summarizing the thesis strictly from your own understanding, substituting complex original phrasing with precise, modern synonyms.

---

### Mistake 2: Violating the Strict One-Third Length Requirement
A standard CSS précis passage spans between 300 and 420 words. Candidates are instructed to reduce the passage to exactly one-third of its length (±5 words).

* **The Trap:** Writing a précis that is either too detailed (approaching half the length) or excessively abbreviated (omitting critical supporting premises).
* **The Penalty:** Severe deduction of technical marks. Examiners explicitly count words or estimate lines to verify conformity.
* **The Formula:**
  $$\text{Target Words} = \frac{\text{Total Words in Passage}}{3} \pm 5 \text{ words}$$
Always append the word count footer at the conclusion of your précis:
  * *Words in original passage:* 360
  * *Required words (one-third):* 120
  * *Words in candidate's précis:* 118

---

### Mistake 3: Inserting Personal Opinions, Commentary, and Extraneous Knowledge
Many aspirants confuse a précis with a critical review or an essay paragraph. 

* **The Trap:** If the passage discusses climate change or democracy in developing states, candidates inject external statistics, quotes, or their personal viewpoints (e.g., *"As we observe in Pakistan today..."*).
* **The Golden Rule of Précis Writing:** **The précis is a mirror, not a commentary.** You must never praise, refute, supplement, or interpret the author's ideas. If the original author makes an assertion that you disagree with, you must faithfully condense that exact assertion without editorializing.

---

### Mistake 4: Choosing an Inaccurate, Vague, or Overly Lengthy Title
The title carries 2 to 3 dedicated marks and provides the examiner with immediate insight into whether you understood the thesis statement.

* **Incorrect Titles:**
  * Single, generic words: *"Education"*, *"Democracy"*, *"Science"*.
  * Sentence-long explanations: *"Why Developing Countries Need to Invest More Funds into Primary Education Rather than Universities"*.
* **Correct Title Construction:**
  * The title should be a crisp noun phrase (3 to 6 words) capturing both the subject and the author's specific angle.
  * *Examples:* *"The Dilemma of Modern Technological Alienation"*, *"Fiscal Autonomy in Federal Governance"*, *"The Decline of Classical Literary Taste"*.
  * Capitalize according to title casing rules and underline or bold the title clearly above your text.

---

### Mistake 5: Writing in the First or Second Person (Retaining "I", "We", or "You")
Original passages frequently use rhetorical devices where the author speaks in the first person (*"When I reflect upon our modern condition..."*) or addresses the reader directly (*"You must recognize..."*).

* **The Trap:** Writing: *"The author says that when I look at the condition..."* or retaining direct dialogue.
* **The Standard Requirement:** A précis must be written strictly in the **third person indirect speech** (*"He observes"*, *"The writer asserts"*, *"It is argued"*). Convert all direct rhetorical statements into objective third-person summaries.

---

### Mistake 6: Inconsistent or Shifting Tenses
Tense inconsistency ruins readability and violates standard academic syntax.

* **The Trap:** Jumping between present, past, and future tenses within consecutive sentences (e.g., *"The author argued that society is corrupt, and people will suffer"*).
* **The Standard Requirement:** Maintain a consistent tense throughout. If the passage explains a timeless philosophical or scientific concept, write in the **present tense**. If the passage recounts historical events, maintain the **past tense**. Consistently use attributive present verbs when referring to the author (*"The author demonstrates"*, *"The text highlights"*).

---

### Mistake 7: Retaining Examples, Analogies, Illustrations, and Statistics
Passages in CSS exams commonly employ illustrative anecdotes, metaphors, and figures to clarify a thesis.

* **The Trap:** Candidates spend 30 to 40 valuable words reproducing an analogy (e.g., spending words explaining Plato's allegory of the cave rather than the philosophical point it illustrates).
* **The Rule of Compression:** Examples, rhetorical questions, proverbs, parenthetical remarks, and statistical breakdowns must be stripped away. Only the underlying concept that the example supports belongs in your final précis.

---

### Mistake 8: Neglecting Rough Drafts and Word-Budgeting
Attempting to write the final précis directly onto the exam script without an iterative draft is the root cause of messy crossings and uneven length.

* **Recommended 45-Minute Time Allocation:**
  1. **Reading & Annotation (10 mins):** Read three times. Underline the topic sentence of each paragraph and highlight transitional keywords (*however, consequently, whereas*).
  2. **Rough Draft 1 (15 mins):** Write bullet points of core arguments and produce a first draft on the rough-work sheets. Calculate word count.
  3. **Refinement & Pruning (10 mins):** Eliminate redundancy, combine compound clauses with relative pronouns, and verify total word count.
  4. **Neat Final Transcript (10 mins):** Copy the final version neatly onto the script with the title and word count table. Strike through rough work with a clean diagonal line.

---

### Mistake 9: Grammatical and Punctuation Slips in the Final Draft
Because the précis is a compressed exercise of 100–130 words, grammatical errors stand out glaringly to the examiner.

* **Top 3 Grammatical Pitfalls in Précis:**
  1. **Subject-Verb Agreement Errors:** Especially after complex noun phrases (*"The collective decision of the commissioners were..."* instead of *"was"*).
  2. **Comma Splices:** Joining two independent clauses with a comma instead of a semicolon or coordinating conjunction.
  3. **Dangling Modifiers:** Phrases that do not logically attach to the subject that immediately follows them.

---

### Mistake 10: Disregarding the Remaining 80 Marks of the Paper
Many aspirants obsess over the précis question (20 Marks) to the complete neglect of the remaining 80 marks of the English Précis & Composition paper:
* **Reading Comprehension (20 Marks)**
* **Sentence Correction (10 Marks)**
* **Idioms & Phrasal Verbs (10 Marks)**
* **Pair of Words (10 Marks)**
* **Direct / Indirect Speech or Active / Passive Transformation (10 Marks)**
* **Translation from Urdu to English (10 Marks)**

* **The Strategic Error:** Spending 2 hours out of the 3-hour exam refining the précis draft, leaving insufficient time to construct complete, error-free sentences for comprehension questions and idioms.
* **Balanced Strategy:** Allocate no more than **50 minutes** to the précis. Secure easy marks on objective grammar and vocabulary sections through daily past paper practice.

---

### Summary Checklist for Candidates

Before submitting your answer script, conduct a 60-second review against this rubric:
- [ ] Is the précis exactly one-third of the original text?
- [ ] Is there an original, concise title at the top?
- [ ] Is the writing entirely in your own words, free of copied phrases?
- [ ] Are all personal opinions, examples, and statistics removed?
- [ ] Is the voice strictly third person with consistent tense?
- [ ] Is the word count summary clearly recorded at the bottom?
